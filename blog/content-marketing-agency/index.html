What a content marketing agency should actually deliver

Four blog posts a month is not a strategy. It is a quota. The agencies worth hiring start from who is searching and end with enquiries.

Media Puppies · 17 October 2026

A content marketing agency sells the promise that the right articles, pages and guides will bring customers who are already looking for what you sell. That promise is true, and it is also the most abused one in the industry, because content is easy to produce and hard to measure. The gap between the two is where most content budgets disappear.

Here is what a good engagement contains, and how to tell it from a content factory.

What a content marketing agency is for

To put your business in front of people at the moment they are researching a purchase, with a page that answers their question better than anything else they will find, and then moves them towards an enquiry. Every part of that sentence is a deliverable: the research into who is searching and with what intent, the page, the quality of the answer, and the path to the enquiry.

Content that does not come from that chain is content for its own sake. It fills a calendar and a report, and the enquiry count does not move. We wrote about the difference in content that ranks and converts.

What the engagement should contain

A keyword and intent map before any writing. Which searches your buyers make, what stage they are at, which pages already exist, and which gaps are worth filling first. Buying-intent pages come before informational ones because they are closer to revenue; informational pages earn their place by feeding the buying pages.

Service and category pages as well as articles. A content agency that only writes blog posts is leaving the pages that convert untouched. The pages where people decide to enquire usually need the most work. Our blog vs service pages post explains the order.

Subject matter from you, writing from them. The best pages carry things only your business knows: how you price, what goes wrong, what you refuse to do. A good agency interviews you for that and writes it up. A content factory writes from the top ten search results and produces page eleven.

Human review on every page. Drafting with AI is standard now. Publishing without a person checking claims, adding specifics and removing filler is how sites get caught in a core update; does Google penalise AI content has the detail.

Internal links and a conversion path. Every page points to the next step and to the service page it supports. Every page has a way to enquire. Without this the content ranks and does nothing.

Measurement in enquiries. Which pages people read before they enquired, and which organic landing pages produced leads. Traffic alone is not the measure; a content agency that reports sessions and nothing else is reporting its own output.

Signs you are paying for volume

A fixed number of posts per month with no keyword map. Topics chosen from a tool's "ideas" rather than from your buyers. No changes to the service pages. Reports that lead with traffic and word counts. Pages that could be swapped onto a competitor's site without editing. An agency that cannot tell you which three pages are most likely to produce an enquiry next quarter.

How we run content

As part of SEO, not separately. The keyword map comes first, the buying-intent pages come second, the articles support them, and the report leads with organic enquiries by page. If you would like to see what a map looks like for your site before hiring anyone, the free SEO audit includes one.

Questions people ask before hiring

How many articles a month should a content marketing agency produce?

As many as the keyword map justifies, which is often fewer than you would expect. One well-researched buying-intent page a month with service pages improved alongside it outperforms eight generic articles. Volume quotas are a sign the strategy came after the contract.

How is content marketing measured?

By organic enquiries and sales attributed to pages, with traffic and rankings as supporting indicators. If an agency reports sessions and word counts only, it is reporting its own output rather than your result.

Is content marketing the same as SEO?

Content is the largest part of SEO but not all of it. Technical fixes, site structure, local listings and links sit alongside it. A content agency that ignores those will produce good pages that never rank.

Does AI-written content work?

Drafting with AI is fine. Publishing it without a knowledgeable person checking the claims, adding what only your business knows, and removing filler is what causes sites to lose visibility. The review step is the work.
