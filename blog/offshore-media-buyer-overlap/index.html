Working with an offshore media buyer: the only number that matters

A team eight thousand kilometres away with five shared hours is easier to work with than one three time zones away where both sides keep rigid local hours.

Media Puppies · 9 October 2026 · Updated 11 October 2026

Every conversation about hiring an offshore media buyer starts in the wrong place. People ask where the team is. The useful question is how many hours a day you can actually talk to each other, and that is arithmetic rather than geography.

Do the overlap sum before hiring an offshore media buyer

Take your working day, convert theirs onto your clock, and measure the intersection. India to London is four and a half hours, which puts a 9-to-6 India day squarely across a London morning and midday — about four and a half shared hours without anyone changing anything.

India to New York on standard hours is zero. Not small — zero. A 9-to-6 India day ends before a New York day begins. No amount of goodwill fixes that; only a deliberate shift does.

What each band of overlap feels like

  • Zero. Every exchange costs a full day. Viable only with a shifted team or an entirely written way of working, and you should price that honestly.
  • One to two hours. One standing call a day. Everything else in writing. Workable for execution, painful for anything requiring back-and-forth.
  • Two to four hours. Same-day answers, a daily call, real collaboration if both sides protect the window.
  • Four or more. An ordinary working relationship. Nobody has to think about it.

Shifting is a real cost, and should be paid for

A team covering US mornings from India works an afternoon-to-late-evening shift. That is somebody’s evening, every day. It works when it is chosen, compensated and staffed deliberately. It fails when it is assumed, because people quietly stop being available and you find out during an incident.

Ask directly: is the person on my account shifted, and are they paid for it? An honest answer tells you a lot about how the relationship will hold up.

A shift that nobody agreed to is a shift that stops happening in month three.

Make writing the default

The teams that work across a gap are the ones where the written record is good enough to run the relationship from. Decisions, the reasoning behind them, what was expected, what happened. Not a status update — a decision log.

The test: if your main contact were unavailable for a week, could someone else pick up the account from what is written down? If the answer is no, the time zone is not your problem.

The advantage nobody mentions

A large gap means work happens while you sleep. For anything monitored rather than discussed — live ad accounts, servers, support queues — that is a genuine benefit. A spend anomaly at 3am gets caught and fixed before your team is at a desk, rather than discovered at 10am with eight hours of waste behind it.

Most offshore pitches lead with cost. Overnight coverage is the more durable argument, and it is the one clients notice after six months.

What to ask for

Named hours, written down. Not "we are flexible". A stated window during which you get a same-hour reply, and a stated expectation outside it. Flexibility sounds generous and means nobody is accountable for anything.

Overlap hours matter less than who is in the account during them — agency staffing is the variable most pitches leave vague.

Questions people ask

Does distance matter when hiring an offshore media buyer?

Distance does not; overlap does. The useful question is how many hours a day you can actually talk, and whether those hours fall when decisions need making. A team twelve hours away with four good hours beats one six hours away with none.

How many overlap hours are enough?

Four or more makes the relationship feel normal. Two to three works if writing is the default and decisions are batched. Below two, every question costs a day and the arrangement only survives on very stable accounts.

Should I ask the team to shift their hours?

You can, and it should be paid for rather than assumed. Shifted hours are a real cost to the people working them, and an arrangement that pretends otherwise degrades quietly over a few months.

What is the advantage nobody mentions?

Written communication. Teams with limited overlap document decisions because they have to, which produces a clearer record of what changed and why than most co-located teams ever keep.
