Arabic or English? Where UAE search leads actually come from

Most UAE businesses either ignore Arabic entirely or machine-translate the whole site. Both are mistakes, and they cost differently.

Media Puppies · 21 September 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026

Arabic SEO is a genuine decision in the UAE, not a formality. It is one of the few markets where the language question is actually open. The resident population is majority expatriate, English is the working language of most business, and a large share of commercial search happens in English. Arabic is the official language, a substantial audience searches in it, and the content available to them is thinner.

Neither “English is enough” nor “translate everything” is the right answer, and the cost of getting it wrong differs. Ignoring Arabic leaves a less contested audience to competitors. Translating everything badly produces a large volume of low-quality pages that hurt the whole domain.

For a Dubai brokerage selling to overseas investors, English is where the money is and Arabic is a later refinement. For a clinic serving Emirati families in Abu Dhabi, the reverse.

What Arabic SEO actually involves

It is not translation. Three things make it a different job:

  • Dialect and register. Written search tends toward Modern Standard Arabic, but real queries mix in Gulf dialect. A translation agency producing formal MSA will miss the phrasing people type.
  • Transliteration. Users frequently search brand names, community names and technical terms in Latin script, or mix scripts in one query. Your Arabic page needs to accommodate both.
  • Right-to-left layout. Not just dir="rtl" — form fields, breadcrumbs, icons with directional meaning and number formatting all need checking. A broken RTL layout reads as a foreign company that did not care.

Machine-translating two hundred pages into Arabic does not double your reach. It adds two hundred pages nobody wants to read.

Getting the technical setup right

A bilingual site fails most often on structure rather than content.

  1. Separate, stable URLs per language. /en/ and /ar/ subdirectories are the simplest reliable arrangement. Never serve two languages from one URL based on a detected browser setting — search engines cannot index a page that changes underneath them.
  2. Reciprocal hreflang. Each page declares ar-AE, en-AE and a sensible x-default, and every page in the set points at every other. One-directional hreflang is ignored.
  3. Self-referencing canonicals within each language. Do not canonical the Arabic page to the English one — that tells Google to drop it.
  4. A visible language switcher that keeps the user on the same page rather than dumping them on the homepage.
  5. Translate the metadata too. An Arabic page with an English title tag will not rank in Arabic.

The pragmatic middle path

For most UAE businesses the sensible sequence is: build the English site properly, identify the five or six pages that produce enquiries, and have those translated by a person who knows the sector. Not the blog. Not the two hundred archive pages. The service pages, the pricing page, the contact page and the two or three articles that convert.

Measure it honestly after a quarter. If the Arabic pages produce enquiries, extend. If they produce traffic and no enquiries, you have learned that your buyer researches in Arabic and transacts in English, which is common and worth knowing before you translate anything else.

Questions people ask

Does a UAE business need Arabic SEO?

It depends entirely on who buys from you. If your customers are expatriate professionals or international B2B buyers, English carries most of the commercial search. If you sell to Emirati households or to government-adjacent buyers, Arabic is where the demand sits and the competition is thinner.

Will a bilingual site cause duplicate content problems?

Not if the versions are properly declared. Give each language its own URL, add hreflang tags pointing at each other and at an x-default, and set a self-referencing canonical on each. Problems come from serving both languages on one URL, or from switching by IP.

Is machine translation good enough for Arabic SEO?

For a first pass on long-form reference content, sometimes. For anything a customer makes a decision on, no. Translated Arabic reads as translated, and the search phrasing people actually use rarely survives a literal translation of English keywords.

Which should I build first?

Build the language your current customers use, and do it properly, before adding the second. A strong site in one language outperforms two thin ones, and the second version is far cheaper to add once the structure already works.
