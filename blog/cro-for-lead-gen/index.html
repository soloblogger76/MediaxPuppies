CRO for lead-gen: the tests worth running first

Button colours are not CRO. These are the tests that change enquiry volume, ordered by how much they typically move.

Media Puppies · 6 August 2026 · Updated 11 October 2026

Most advice on conversion rate optimisation for lead generation was actually written for ecommerce sites with thousands of daily sessions. A lead-gen business with 400 visitors a month cannot run those tests — there is not enough traffic to reach a conclusion before the market changes.

So the approach has to be different: fewer tests, bigger swings, and a willingness to decide on judgement when the data will never be conclusive.

Test big things, because you cannot detect small ones

At low traffic, a 5% improvement is invisible. A 40% one is not. So test changes large enough to show up:

Worth testingNot at your traffic
A different offer entirelyButton colour
Form with 3 fields vs 7Headline reworded slightly
Price shown vs hiddenImage swapped for a similar one
WhatsApp option addedTestimonial moved up 200px

The order that usually pays

1. The offer

Not the page — the thing being offered. Free consultation is what everyone says. A room-by-room quote in 24 hours, no site visit is specific, low-effort and easy to say yes to. This regularly doubles enquiries and no amount of layout work competes with it.

2. Form length

Cut to the minimum needed for the first call. Usually name, phone, one qualifying question. Ask the rest on the phone when they are already talking to you.

3. Showing price

Counterintuitive for many businesses and frequently a win. Fewer total enquiries, more qualified ones, far less wasted sales time. Measure this on cost per qualified lead or you will read the result backwards.

4. Adding WhatsApp

A significant share of Indian buyers will message when they will not call or fill in a form. Offering both channels regularly lifts total enquiries meaningfully — provided somebody actually answers.

5. Proof placement

Move your strongest evidence — a number, a named customer, a photo of real work — above the fold. This one is cheap to try and occasionally surprising.

At 400 visitors a month you are not running experiments. You are making informed changes and watching the trend.

How to decide without statistical significance

You will often not reach it. That is a fact about your traffic, not a reason to do nothing. The practical approach:

  1. Run one change at a time, for at least three weeks or 100 conversions, whichever comes first.
  2. Look at direction and size. A 40% move in three weeks is worth acting on even without a p-value.
  3. If the result is small and ambiguous, keep the simpler version. Simplicity is its own advantage.
  4. Never run two changes at once. You will learn nothing from either.

What to measure instead of conversion rate

Raw conversion rate is misleading in lead-gen, because a page can double its enquiries and halve their quality. Track:

  • Qualified enquiries per 100 visitors — the number that matters.
  • Cost per qualified lead, combining the page with the ad spend.
  • Sales time per closed deal. If enquiries rise but your team burns twice the hours, that is a loss dressed as a win.

The cheapest conversion rate optimisation for lead generation there is

Record sessions and watch ten of them. Not a heatmap summary — actual sessions. You will see people hesitate at a field, scroll past the thing you thought was obvious, and leave at a point you never suspected. Ten sessions routinely beats a month of theorising.

Conversion rate is also the term that sets how much you can pay per lead — the arithmetic behind your own target makes that relationship explicit.

Questions people ask

Can I run A/B tests with low traffic?

Not useful ones on small changes. With a few hundred visitors a month you can only detect large differences, so test the offer, the headline and the form length rather than button colours.

How do I decide without statistical significance?

Run the change for a fixed period, decide in advance what size of difference would make you keep it, and accept that you are making a judgement rather than proving a fact. A clearly better page does not need a p-value.

What usually moves enquiries most?

The offer, then the form, then speed on a real phone, then the match between the ad and the page. Layout and colour are far down the list and are where most effort goes.

What should I measure instead of conversion rate?

Cost per qualified lead. A page that converts better but produces people your sales team cannot use has made the business worse while improving the metric.
