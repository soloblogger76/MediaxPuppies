Who owns your ad account, really

The single most expensive mistake in an agency relationship is discovered on the day it ends. Check this before you are ready to leave, not after.

Media Puppies · 11 October 2026

Who owns your ad account sounds like a paperwork question. It is not. It decides whether, on the day you change agency, you walk away with years of conversion history and audience data — or start from zero while your competitors do not.

What is actually at stake

If the account belongs to the agency, so does everything attached to it:

  • Conversion history. Google's and Meta's bidding models are trained on your past conversions. Losing that account means a new one enters the learning phase with nothing. The learning phase calculator shows how many weeks of spend that costs.
  • The pixel or dataset. Rebuilding a pixel means rebuilding every custom audience, every retargeting pool and every lookalike seed.
  • Historical reporting. Year-on-year comparison becomes impossible, which removes your ability to judge the next agency.
  • Conversion actions and offline imports. Especially painful for B2B, where offline conversion import takes months to tune.
  • Review and page assets. Google Business Profile reviews and Meta page history are not transferable in any easy sense.

How to check who owns your ad account, in five minutes

Google Ads. Open Admin, then Access and security. Look at who holds Admin level access and whether the account sits under a manager (MCC) account you do not control. An agency MCC linked to your own account is fine. Your ads living inside the agency's MCC with no separate account of your own is not.

Meta. Open Business Settings. Check whether the ad account and the pixel are owned by your Business Manager with the agency added as a partner, or owned by theirs. The giveaway is the Owner column — it names a business, and that business should be yours.

Analytics and Tag Manager. Same question. A GA4 property inside an agency's Google account disappears with the agency.

Domain, hosting and landing pages. Separate question, same principle. Pages built on an agency's subdomain are theirs.

What good looks like

You own the Business Manager, the ad accounts, the pixel, the analytics property and the domain. The agency is added as a partner or granted user access at the level they need. When the relationship ends you remove their access and nothing else changes — no migration, no export, no negotiation.

The test is simple: if you revoked the agency's access tonight, would anything stop running tomorrow? If the answer is yes, you do not own your account.

Fixing it while you are still on good terms

This is much easier to do now than during a breakup. Create your own Business Manager and your own Google Ads account if you do not have them. Ask the agency to transfer ownership of the ad account and pixel, or to grant your business partner access to the existing ones and then reassign ownership. Most reasonable agencies will do this without argument; the reaction is informative either way.

Where ownership genuinely cannot be transferred — which happens with some legacy Google accounts — the fallback is to run new campaigns from your own account going forward and let the old one wind down, accepting the learning cost once rather than every time you change supplier.

Where ad account ownership belongs in a contract

One clause, in plain words: the client owns all advertising accounts, data, pixels, creative assets and landing pages, and the agency will transfer or release access within a set number of days of termination. If an agency resists that sentence, the resistance is the information. The rest of what to check in an agency contract is a shorter list than you think.

We set accounts up this way as a matter of course and say so on the process page. It costs us nothing and removes the one thing that makes leaving an agency expensive — which is also, not coincidentally, the thing that keeps bad agencies employed. If you are already mid-exit, leaving an agency cleanly covers the sequence.

Questions people ask before hiring

Who owns your ad account if the agency created it?

They do, unless your contract says otherwise. An account created under the agency’s business belongs to the agency, and they are usually within their rights to keep it. That is exactly why the ownership clause matters more than the fee.

What do I lose if I have to start a new ad account?

Conversion history, pixel-based audiences, learning on every campaign, and year-on-year reporting. Practically it means a period of higher cost per result while the new account gathers enough data to optimise against.

How do I check who owns my Meta pixel?

In Business Settings, open Data Sources and then Datasets. The owning business is listed against each one. If it names the agency rather than your company, the pixel is theirs with you as a partner.

Can I transfer an ad account from an agency to my own business?

Usually yes, and it is routine administration between willing parties. Google Ads accounts can be moved between manager accounts and Meta assets can be reassigned between businesses. The difficulty is almost never technical — it is that the request arrives after notice has been served.

Is it worth moving accounts if my agency relationship is fine?

Yes, and it is far easier while things are fine. Transfers are routine administration between willing parties and become a negotiation once notice has been served.
