Solar lead generation in India: subsidy questions and site surveys

Solar has the clearest qualification criteria of any category we work in. Most campaigns ignore all of them.

Media Puppies · 14 July 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026

Solar lead generation is unusual: whether someone can buy is almost entirely determined by three facts you can ask about in a single form. Most accounts don't ask, then wonder why the sales team is drowning.

The three questions that qualify solar lead generation

  1. Do you own the roof? Tenants and most apartment residents can't proceed. This single question removes a large share of wasted enquiries.
  2. What's your monthly electricity bill? Below a threshold, the payback period makes the sale unrealistic. You know your threshold; put it in the form.
  3. Residential or commercial? Completely different economics, subsidies and sales cycles. They should not share a campaign.

Putting these three in the ad copy and the form reduces volume and raises cost per lead. It also raises the number of site surveys, which is what you're actually buying.

Be straight about subsidies

Subsidy schemes change, vary by state, and are the single biggest source of disappointment in this category. Vague promises in ads produce enquiries that die the moment someone explains the real position.

The better approach: state what's generally available, state that eligibility depends on the state and the system, and promise a clear answer on the call. Customers respond well to being told the truth about something they suspect is being oversold.

Overpromising on subsidy doesn't lose you the sale at the quote stage. It loses you the review afterwards.

Creative that converts

  • A real bill, before and after. The most persuasive asset in the category. Redact the name, show the numbers.
  • Payback period, stated. "Pays back in about four years on a ₹6,000 bill" beats any adjective.
  • Installations in their area. Rooftops that look like their rooftop, in a locality they recognise.
  • The maintenance question answered. It's the second objection after price and almost nobody addresses it.

The follow-up sequence

Solar has a longer consideration cycle than most lead-gen categories, so the sequence matters more:

  1. Instant WhatsApp with an indicative saving based on the bill size they entered.
  2. Call within minutes to confirm roof and location.
  3. Site survey booked — this is your real conversion event.
  4. A patient sequence for the undecided, with a real customer's numbers rather than a discount.

What to measure

  • Cost per qualified lead — roof owner, bill above threshold.
  • Cost per site survey completed. The number the business runs on.
  • Survey-to-installation rate.
  • Lead-to-contact rate, which exposes whether the follow-up is the bottleneck.

Feed the survey-booked event back to the ad platform and the account starts finding people who actually book. That single change is usually worth more than any targeting work.

Questions people ask

What qualifies a solar lead?

Roof ownership, monthly electricity bill, and property type. Someone renting a flat cannot buy a rooftop system whatever else is true, and bill size decides whether the payback maths works. Asking all three in the form removes most of the waste before a salesperson is involved.

How should I handle subsidy questions?

Honestly and early, because the gap between what people expect and what they receive is where deals die. Stating what the subsidy covers, who qualifies and how long it takes costs you some enquiries and saves the sales team from the ones that were never going to close.

What creative works for solar?

Installed systems on homes that look like the buyer’s, with the bill reduction stated. Abstract panels against a blue sky convert poorly because they do not answer the only question the buyer has, which is what this does to their bill.

What should I measure?

Cost per site survey booked, then cost per installation. Cost per lead in solar is almost meaningless because the qualification gap between a form fill and a surveyable roof is so wide.
