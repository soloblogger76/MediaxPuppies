Special Ad Categories: why your US targeting disappeared

If you advertise property, jobs, loans or insurance to a US audience, half the targeting options you have read about are not available to you. Better to know that before the build than after.

Media Puppies · 9 October 2026

A US client comes to us with an underperforming lead campaign and a reasonable complaint: the targeting looks nothing like what their previous agency promised. No age range. No gender. Location locked to a wide radius rather than the ZIP codes they actually serve. Lookalikes behaving oddly.

Nothing is broken. The account is declared under a Special Ad Category, and Meta has removed those controls on purpose.

What the categories are

Meta restricts targeting for ads about housing, employment, credit, and social issues, elections or politics. The restrictions exist because of discrimination concerns — the ability to show a housing advert to one demographic and not another is exactly the thing US fair housing and equal employment law is designed to prevent.

If you are a real estate brokerage, a property developer, a recruiter, a lender, an insurer, or anyone advertising a credit product, you are in scope. So is a staffing agency running a "we’re hiring" campaign for a client.

What you actually lose

  • Age and gender. Gone. You target 18+ and all genders, and that is the only option.
  • Detailed targeting. Heavily reduced. Most interest and behaviour options that could proxy for a protected characteristic are removed.
  • Tight geography. Location targeting is forced to a minimum radius rather than a ZIP code, which matters enormously for a brokerage working one neighbourhood.
  • Exclusions. You cannot exclude audiences in the way you normally would.
  • Lookalike behaviour. Special ad audiences exist, but they are built differently and will not perform like a standard lookalike.

What you keep, and where the work moves

You keep the creative, the offer, the landing page and the conversion signal. Which is to say: everything that was already doing most of the work.

When targeting is removed, qualification has to happen somewhere else. In practice that means the ad itself does the filtering. An ad that opens with "Buying your first home in Austin under $450,000?" selects its own audience far more precisely than a demographic filter would, and it keeps doing so after Meta’s controls are gone.

In a restricted category the creative is not the message. It is the targeting.

Declare it on day one

This is the part that costs people real money. If you run for six weeks undeclared and then apply the category — or Meta applies it for you — the campaign is rebuilt and everything it learned is reset. You pay for the learning phase twice.

Worse, repeated violations attach to the account rather than the ad. Accounts that accumulate them are the ones that end up restricted, and a restricted ad account stops the business, not just the campaign.

How we build these accounts differently

  1. Fewer, broader ad sets. Since granular targeting is unavailable, splitting budget achieves nothing except keeping every ad set in the learning phase. Consolidate.
  2. Heavier creative rotation. With the ad doing the qualifying, you need more angles, not more audiences.
  3. Stronger on-page qualification. A form that asks budget or timeline early filters what Meta no longer can.
  4. Feed CRM outcomes back. With targeting removed, the conversion signal is the main thing steering delivery. Sending back which leads were genuinely qualified matters more here than in an unrestricted account.

None of this is a workaround, and anyone selling you one should be refused. Attempts to evade the category — running the same offer under a different page, or disguising the subject matter — are a policy violation in their own right.

The practical upshot

Restricted categories are harder, and they are not hopeless. Your competitors face exactly the same constraints, which means the advantage goes to whoever writes the better advert and qualifies better after the click. That has always been true; the restrictions simply remove the ability to pretend otherwise.

If the budget is spread across several ad sets while you wait for a restricted account to settle, check it against the learning phase calculator first — consolidation is usually free and usually the biggest available improvement.
