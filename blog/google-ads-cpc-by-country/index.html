Google Ads CPC by country: US vs UK vs Canada vs Australia, and why Ahrefs and Semrush disagree on price

We pulled 1,800 keywords from Ahrefs across four countries and set the US ones beside our Semrush data for the same searches. The two tools disagreed on every shared keyword, so that is where we start.

We set out to measure Google Ads CPC by country for the four markets our readers buy in: the US, the UK, Canada and Australia. We pulled 1,800 keywords from Ahrefs for nine trades and services on 17 November 2026. Before comparing countries, we checked the US prices against the Semrush data we collected two weeks earlier, and the two tools did not agree on a single keyword.

The short answer has two parts. On the 52 US keywords both tools priced, Ahrefs was lower every time, and its median figure was 36.52% of Semrush's. So the absolute CPC you read in any one tool is a weak basis for a budget. Within Ahrefs alone, where the method is the same everywhere, Google Ads CPC by country follows a clear order: on 71 identical keywords, the median UK price was 53.33% of the US price, Canada 62.5% and Australia 80%.

Ahrefs vs Semrush CPC: 52 US keywords, two sets of prices

Ahrefs' CPC was lower than Semrush's on all 52 keywords. The median Semrush CPC across them was $18.47 and the median Ahrefs CPC was $3.50. Only 1 keyword landed within 25% either way: "business lawyer", at $6.63 in Semrush and $5 in Ahrefs. At the other end, 13 keywords, 25% of the set, were priced by Ahrefs at under a tenth of the Semrush figure.

The biggest gaps were in legal. These are the eight keywords where the tools were furthest apart.

We expected the tools to differ in level, since each one builds its CPC from its own model. We did not expect them to disagree on order. They did. The rank correlation between the two sets of prices was 0.16, where 1 would mean the same order and 0 no relation. Of Semrush's 10 most expensive keywords in the set, only 4 were also in Ahrefs' top 10. A personal injury lawyer near-me search is one of the dearest clicks in US search by Semrush's estimate, and one of the cheapest in this set by Ahrefs'.

By vertical, the gap was widest in dental. On 6 dental keywords the median Ahrefs price was 22.75% of Semrush's ($2.25 against $11.16). On 20 legal keywords it was 36.52% ($3.50 against $40.90), and on 25 home-service keywords 39.04% ($5 against $18.45). The one marketing keyword in both sets came out at 42.96%.

How accurate is Semrush CPC, or Ahrefs CPC?

We cannot tell you, and we would be wary of anyone who says they can from tool data alone. The true price of a click is set in each auction, by each advertiser's bid, quality and location, and neither company sees it. Both publish a modelled estimate. Ours is a comparison between two estimates, so it shows how far apart they are and nothing about which one is right.

What the data does show is that the gap is uneven. If Ahrefs were simply a fixed share of Semrush, you could convert one to the other. With a rank correlation of 0.16, you cannot. Our view: for a spend plan, the absolute dollar figure from one tool is a guess, and the order of keywords from one tool is only a slightly better one.

Google Ads CPC by country: the same keyword in four markets

For the country comparison we stayed inside Ahrefs, because one tool's method applied to four databases is a fairer like-for-like test than mixing tools. We kept the 71 buyer keywords that appeared in the top 50 for all four countries. For each one we divided the UK, Canadian and Australian price by the US price.

The UK came out cheapest. Its median was 53.33% of the US price, and 61.97% of the keywords cost less there than in the US. Canada's median was 62.5%, with 69.01% of keywords cheaper. Australia was closest to the US at 80%, with 60.56% cheaper. On the 44 home-service keywords in that set, the gap was wider: the UK at 49%, Canada at 52.29% and Australia at 65%.

CPC UK vs US and the other two, trade by trade

Across all buyer keywords in each country's top 50, not only the shared ones, the median for home services was close everywhere: $3.25 in the US, $3 in the UK, $3.50 in Australia and $3 in Canada. That looks like a contradiction of the section above, and it is mostly a sample effect. Each country's top 50 is a different list. The US lists hold many city long-tail searches, and the matched set is the fairer comparison.

By trade, the medians moved more. Australia had the highest median for 4 of the 9 seeds, which surprised us.

Emergency searches carry a bigger premium in the US

We split home-service keywords into emergency searches (emergency, 24 hour, same day, open now) and the rest. In the US, the emergency median was $10 against $3 for other searches, 3.33 times as much. Australia came next at 2.67 times ($8 against $3), then the UK at 1.8 times ($4.50 against $2.50). In Canada the two medians were equal at $3, though only 14 emergency keywords were in the Canadian sample.

"Emergency plumber" shows it clearly: $20 in the US, $9 in Australia, $6 in the UK and $5 in Canada. Our view: in the US and Australia, emergency terms deserve their own campaign and budget line, because their price runs on a different scale from the rest of the account.

Where the tools show no price at all

Some keywords had no CPC in Ahrefs. The UK had the fewest, 7.11% of its rows. The US had 14%, Canada 20% and Australia 21.11%. A missing CPC can mean few advertisers bid on the term, or that the tool lacks data for it, and we cannot tell those apart. For Canadian and Australian budgets it is one more reason to check prices inside Google's own tools before spending.

What we would change in how budgets get set

  • Stop budgeting from one tool's CPC. Our view: a single tool's number is a starting guess. When Semrush and Ahrefs differ by a median of 36.52% and a rank correlation of 0.16, a plan built on either one alone can be off by several times.
  • Check Google Keyword Planner for your exact locations. Its top-of-page bid range comes from Google and can be set to your city or region. It is still an estimate, so we treat the range as the planning figure and the tools as a second opinion.
  • Run a small test before the real budget. In our view, a short test on your core keywords is the only way to learn your own auction price, because it depends on your bids, ads and landing page.
  • Compare countries inside one tool. For a decision such as whether to open the UK or Australia first, the relative gap in one tool is more useful than mixing sources. On our data, the UK was the cheapest of the four on identical keywords.
  • Convert to your own currency. Ahrefs shows every country in US dollars, so UK, Canadian and Australian budgets need converting before they go into a plan.

For each of 9 seed terms we opened the Ahrefs "Matching terms" report in the US, UK, Australian and Canadian databases and kept the top 50 keywords by monthly search volume, 36 pulls and 1,800 rows in all. Ahrefs shows CPC in US dollars for every country, so all prices on this page are in USD. 280 rows had no CPC and were left out of the medians.

We flagged priced rows that are not buyer searches with a written keyword rule: jobs, salaries, training, definitions, supplies, insurance products, software, a TV show and named brands of other firms. Country and trade medians use the 1,332 buyer rows. The rule is in analyse.py with the dataset.

For the tool comparison we matched Ahrefs US keywords to the Semrush US CPCs we collected on 3 November 2026 for our CPC by industry and home services studies. 52 keywords appeared in both with a price. The rank correlation is Spearman's.

Limits. Both tools publish estimates from their own models. Neither is Google Keyword Planner and neither is an auction result, so we cannot say which is closer to what an advertiser pays. The two pulls were 14 days apart. The top 50 by volume is a different set of keywords in each country, which is why the like-for-like comparison uses only the 71 keywords present in all four. Ahrefs' daily limit stopped us before three more planned seeds (google ads, seo agency, marketing agency).

Questions about CPC by country and keyword tool estimates

Which country has the highest Google Ads CPC?

On Ahrefs' estimates of 17 November 2026, the US was the most expensive overall on 71 keywords present in all four markets. The median UK price was 53.33% of the US, Canada 62.5% and Australia 80%. By trade it varies: Australia had the highest median for plumber, electrician, roofer and lawyer searches.

Is CPC cheaper in the UK than the US?

Usually, on our data. On identical keywords the median UK CPC was 53.33% of the US price, and 61.97% of keywords were cheaper in the UK. Locksmith and PPC agency searches were exceptions, with higher UK medians.

Why do Ahrefs and Semrush show different CPCs?

Each tool estimates CPC from its own model; neither sees Google's auctions. On 52 US keywords, Ahrefs was lower than Semrush on every one, with a median of 36.52% of the Semrush figure, and the two ranked the keywords in quite different orders.

How accurate is Semrush CPC?

We cannot measure that from tool data, because the real price is set in each auction. What we can say is that Semrush and Ahrefs disagree widely, so we check Google Keyword Planner for the exact location and run a small test before setting a budget.

Are these CPCs in local currency?

No. Ahrefs shows CPC in US dollars for all four countries, so every price on this page is in USD.
