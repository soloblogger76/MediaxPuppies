Google Ads for electricians: what a click costs in the US, UK, Canada and Australia

We took the top 50 electrician searches by volume in four countries from Ahrefs, kept the ones where someone wants to hire an electrician, and compared the click prices. Australia came out dearest, which we did not expect.

Key findings

  • On Ahrefs estimates collected on 17 November 2026, the median buyer electrician keyword cost $6.00 a click in Australia, $4.00 in the US, $2.50 in Canada and $2.25 in the UK.
  • Australia was the only market above the US, at 150% of the US median. City searches drove it: 14 Australian city keywords had a median of $7.00, and the US top 50 held no city keywords at all.
  • Emergency searches cost the most in three of four countries. In the US the emergency median was $7.00 against $3.25 for other electrician searches, 2.15 times as much. Australia was 1.42 times and the UK 1.32 times.
  • On the 5 US keywords both tools priced, Ahrefs was a median 30.77% of Semrush. "Emergency electrician" was $21.57 in Semrush and $0.15 in Ahrefs.
  • The dearest clicks around the trade are sold to electricians: "electrician liability insurance" was $30 in the UK, and the top buyer keyword in any country was $10.

We wanted a straight answer to a question electricians and the agencies that work for them ask us: what does Google Ads for electricians cost per click in the US, the UK, Canada and Australia? We took the top 50 "electrician" searches by volume in each country from Ahrefs on 17 November 2026, kept the 92 where someone wants to hire an electrician, and compared the prices.

The short answer. On Ahrefs estimates, the median buyer electrician keyword cost $6.00 a click in Australia, $4.00 in the US, $2.50 in Canada and $2.25 in the UK. So 100 clicks at the median come to about $600 in Australia, $400 in the US, $250 in Canada and $225 in the UK, before your own bids, ads and location move the number. Treat these as tool estimates. On the same US keywords Semrush priced electrician clicks several times higher, and we show that gap below.

Google Ads for electricians: the price in each country

We expected the US to be the dearest market, as it was for most trades in our wider country study. For electricians it was not. Australia's median was 150% of the US figure. The UK sat at 56.25% and Canada at 62.5%.

The volume-weighted average tells a slightly different story. It gives more say to the keywords people search most. On that measure the US ($4.91) and Australia ($4.98) are almost level, because the two biggest US searches, "electrician near me" (217,000 a month) and "electrician" (216,000), are priced at $5 and $6. Australia's median is lifted by many smaller city searches.

The samples are small. The UK has 16 priced buyer keywords, because a lot of its top 50 is jobs, courses and insurance. Read the UK figure as a rough guide.

Electrician PPC by search type: emergency, near me and city

We sorted each buyer keyword into one group: emergency (emergency, 24 hour, 24/7), city (it names a place), near me, or general and service type such as "residential electrician".

In the US, emergency searches had a median of $7.00 against $3.25 for everything else, 2.15 times as much. The two dearest US buyer keywords were "24/7 electrician" at $10 and "24 hour electrician" at $9. Australia's emergency median was $8.50 against $6.00, 1.42 times, and the UK's $2.50 against $1.90, 1.32 times. Canada had only 2 emergency keywords in its top 50, with a median of $2.25, so we would not read anything into its result.

One US number looks wrong to us. Ahrefs priced "emergency electrician" at $0.15 in the US, while "emergency electrician near me" was $7 and Semrush had the same term at $21.57. We kept it in the data, because removing numbers we dislike is a bad habit, but it pulls the US emergency median down.

City searches explain most of Australia's lead. Its 14 city keywords had a median of $7.00, with "electrician gold coast" at $9 and "electrician perth", "electrician brisbane" and "electrician adelaide" at $7 each. Canada's 14 city keywords had a median of $3.25 and the UK's 5 a median of $3.00, both above their national medians. The US top 50 held no city keywords at all, because national volume pushes them out of a top 50 list. So the US figure leans on head terms and the Australian one on cities.

General searches were the cheapest group everywhere: $2.50 in the US, $3.10 in Australia, $1.55 in Canada and $1.30 in the UK. "Near me" searches sat in between in most countries, at $4.50 in the US and $1.90 in the UK.

On the plain head term "electrician", Australia matched the US at $6, and the UK was at 30% of the US price. In a smaller market a cheaper click usually comes with fewer searches to buy, so the lower price alone says little about how many leads a budget will bring.

Ahrefs and Semrush disagree on electrician CPC

Five US keywords were in both our Ahrefs pull and the Semrush data we collected on 3 November 2026. Ahrefs was lower on all five, at a median of 30.77% of the Semrush price.

Across all 25 US electrical keywords in our Semrush set, the median was $10.51, with emergency searches at $20.02 and repair searches at $8.49. So depending on the tool, a US electrician click is a few dollars or close to twenty. Neither tool sees Google's auctions. Our view: use one tool to compare countries and search types, because the method is the same across them, and do not plan a budget on either tool's dollar figure alone. Clicks that are not customers

Most of each top 50 was not people hiring an electrician. 83 priced rows were jobs, pay, courses, tools, insurance and similar searches. The dearest of all were insurance products sold to electricians: "electrician liability insurance" at $30 in the UK, "public liability insurance electrician" at $25 in the UK and $19 in Australia. No buyer keyword in any country went above $10.

In the UK and Australia many searches were for auto electricians, who fix vehicle wiring. Their medians were $0.60 in the UK and $1.10 in Australia. A broad-match "electrician" campaign will spend on jobs, apprenticeships and car wiring unless your negatives cover them. Our negative keywords audit and the search terms report guide show how we check for that.

What we would change in an electrician account

  • Split emergency from the rest in the US. Our view: at 2.15 times the price, 24 hour and emergency terms need their own campaign and budget, so they do not starve the cheaper general terms.
  • Plan Australia city by city. The dear Australian terms name a city. A campaign per metro area lets you see where the $7 to $9 clicks pay back.
  • Do not assume the UK and Canada are bargains. Their clicks were cheaper, but demand was a fraction of the US. Cheap clicks with few searches can still leave you short of calls.
  • Build the negative list before launch. Jobs, apprenticeship, salary, course, insurance, auto and car belong on it in every country.
  • Check Keyword Planner for your own area, then test. Tools disagree by several times on the same keyword. A short test on your core terms is the only way we know to learn your real price.

The rows come from the Ahrefs pull behind our Google Ads CPC by country study: the "Matching terms" report for "electrician" in the US, UK, Australian and Canadian databases, top 50 keywords by monthly search volume, collected on 17 November 2026. Ahrefs shows CPC in US dollars for every country. 9 rows had no CPC and are left out.

That study flags searches that are not hiring searches with a shared keyword rule. We read every electrician row by eye and changed 19 rows to "not buyer" (pay questions, a social media personality, licence grades and job titles such as journeyman and industrial electrician, training, and "electrician seo") and 1 row to "buyer" ("small job electrician near me", caught by the word "job"). We also set auto and car electricians aside, because they fix vehicle wiring. The overrides and reasons are written out in compile.py and findings.json. Because of them, our medians differ from the electrician row in the country study; both sets are in findings.json.

Search type is a keyword rule applied in this order: emergency (emergency, 24 hour, 24/7), city (names a place), near me, and everything else as general or service type. The Semrush comparison uses the 25 US electrical keywords from our home services study, collected on 3 November 2026; 5 of them were also in the Ahrefs list.

Limits. These are Ahrefs estimates from its own model. They are not auction prices and not Google Keyword Planner bids. Samples are small: 16 to 28 buyer keywords per country, and some search-type groups hold only 2 to 5 keywords. Questions about Google Ads for electricians

How much does Google Ads cost for electricians per click?

On Ahrefs estimates collected on 17 November 2026, the median buyer electrician keyword cost $4.00 a click in the US, $6.00 in Australia, $2.50 in Canada and $2.25 in the UK. Semrush put US electrician clicks higher, with a median of $10.51 across 25 electrical keywords on 3 November 2026.

Why are electrician clicks dearer in Australia than the US?

In our data the Australian top 50 is full of city searches such as Gold Coast, Perth and Brisbane, priced at $7 to $9. The US top 50 has none, because national head terms dominate it. On the volume-weighted average the two countries were almost level, $4.98 and $4.91.

Do emergency electrician keywords cost more?

Yes in the US, Australia and the UK. The US emergency median was $7.00 against $3.25 for other electrician searches. Canada had only 2 emergency keywords in its sample, too few to judge.

Is PPC for electricians cheaper in the UK?

Per click, yes: the UK median was $2.25, 56.25% of the US figure. But UK search volume is much lower, with 28,000 monthly searches for "electrician near me" against 217,000 in the US, so there are fewer clicks to buy.

Which tool gives the right electrician CPC, Ahrefs or Semrush?

Neither sees Google's auctions, so we cannot say. On 5 shared US keywords Ahrefs was a median 30.77% of Semrush. We use one tool for comparisons, Keyword Planner for the local range and a small test for the real price.
