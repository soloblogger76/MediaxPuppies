Click-to-WhatsApp ads: when they beat a landing page

For a lot of Indian categories this is the highest-converting ad format available. It is also the easiest one to fill with time-wasters.

Media Puppies · 5 September 2026

Click-to-WhatsApp removes the two things people hate most about lead forms: typing on a phone, and waiting for a call back. The conversation starts immediately, in an app they already have open.

When it wins

  • Considered purchases with questions — interiors, real estate, clinics, education. People want to ask before they commit.
  • Low-trust categories where a form feels like handing your number to a call centre.
  • When your team is fast. The whole advantage is immediacy. If nobody replies for two hours you've bought a worse lead than a form would have given you.

When a landing page wins

  • When you need structured data — budget, configuration, timeline — before a human is worth spending.
  • When volume is high enough that unstructured chats drown the team.
  • When you want the visitor to absorb proof first. A chat skips your case studies entirely.

The format that starts a conversation fastest is only better if someone is actually there to have it.

The mistake that produces junk

Running it with no qualification at all. A generic "Hi, I'm interested" from an ad that stated no price gives your team nothing to work with, and you'll get a lot of them.

Fix it in two places. Put the price range in the ad creative, so browsers self-select out. And set a pre-filled message that carries context — which product, which page — so the first reply can be useful instead of "may I know your requirement".

Setup that works

  1. Pre-filled message with the product or service named.
  2. An instant automated acknowledgement with price range and next step.
  3. Two or three qualifying questions before a human joins.
  4. Conversions API firing on qualified conversations, not on every chat opened — otherwise the algorithm optimises towards people who say hello and vanish.

The opening message does the qualifying

The pre-filled message is the most underused setting in the whole format. Left at the default, everyone arrives saying "Hi, I'm interested" and your team spends the first four exchanges working out what they want.

Set it to something that carries information: "Hi, I saw your ad about modular kitchens. My flat is a [2BHK / 3BHK] in [area] and I'm looking to start in [month]." The visitor edits it in seconds and your first reply can already be useful. It also quietly filters — someone unwilling to fill in three brackets was not going to buy.

What happens in the first ten minutes

The entire advantage of this format is immediacy, and it evaporates fast. A reply within five minutes converts several times better than one at two hours, because the person is still holding the phone.

Three things make that achievable without hiring anyone:

  • A greeting message that buys time honestly. "Thanks — a designer will reply within 15 minutes. Meanwhile, what's your pincode?" keeps the conversation alive and collects something useful.
  • Saved replies for the five questions you always get. Price range, timeline, area covered, what is included, how to start.
  • Ad scheduling that matches staffing. If nobody works Sundays, do not pay for Sunday clicks. This alone often cuts wasted spend by a fifth.

The measurement problem, and the fix

Chats are hard to count. Without setup, you know how many conversations started and nothing about what they became, which makes the channel impossible to compare against anything else.

Use the WhatsApp Business API or a shared-inbox tool that supports labels, then label every conversation: new, qualified, quoted, won, junk. It is a small daily discipline and it converts an unmeasurable channel into one you can optimise. Sending qualified and won events back to Meta as conversions is what lets the algorithm find more of the right people instead of more of the chattiest.

Where it does not belong

Skip this format when the purchase needs structured information before a human should spend time — complex B2B specifications, anything requiring documents, anything where the answer depends on six variables. A form collects those cleanly; a chat turns them into twenty messages.

Skip it too when volume would drown the team. Two hundred unstructured conversations a month with a two-person sales desk is not a lead source, it is a backlog.

Common questions

Business app or the API? The free Business app is fine up to roughly thirty conversations a day with one person answering. Past that, the API and a shared inbox stop conversations being missed.

Will chat leads be cheaper than form leads? Usually yes per conversation, and the quality sits between an instant form and a landing page. Judge it on qualified conversations, as always.

Can I retarget people who messaged? Meta lets you build audiences from people who started a conversation, which is one of the highest-intent retargeting pools available.
