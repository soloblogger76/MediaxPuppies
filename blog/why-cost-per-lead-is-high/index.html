7 reasons your cost per lead is high (that aren't the algorithm)

When a client says “Meta stopped working”, it’s almost never Meta. Here are the seven things we actually find, ranked by how often they’re the culprit.

Media Puppies · 21 August 2026 · Updated 11 October 2026

We audit a lot of accounts. When someone says their cost per lead is high, they usually expect the answer to be targeting, or "the algorithm changing". It almost never is. Here's what it actually turns out to be, roughly in order of frequency.

1. Your offer is the problem, not your ads

"Contact us for a quote" is not an offer. It asks for effort and promises nothing specific in return. Compare it with "Get a room-by-room quote in 24 hours — no site visit needed." Same business, same budget, completely different cost per lead.

Before touching a campaign, we ask: why would someone give you their number today rather than think about it? If there's no answer, no amount of targeting fixes it.

2. Creative fatigue you can see in the data

If the same three ads have been running for two months, your CPL is rising on a schedule. Check frequency: above roughly 2.5–3 in a cold audience, your best prospects have already seen it and decided.

The fix isn't a new colour on the same creative. It's a new angle — a different reason to care. Price, speed, risk reversal, social proof, a specific objection answered.

3. You're optimising for the wrong event

Optimise for Leads and Meta will efficiently find you people who fill in forms. Whether they answer the phone afterwards is not its concern. Many accounts running "successful" ₹90 leads are buying volume from people who will never buy.

Send a qualification signal back — a "qualified lead" event, or offline conversions from your CRM — and the algorithm starts hunting for people who look like your good leads. This is the single highest-leverage fix on this list and the one most often skipped. It's most of what tracking and attribution is actually for.

4. The landing page loses what the ad won

Paid traffic sent to a homepage is money donated to the internet. A visitor arriving from an ad about modular kitchens should land on a page about modular kitchens, with the form visible without scrolling.

  • Load time over three seconds on 4G costs you a meaningful share of arrivals before they see anything.
  • Seven form fields where three would do. Every extra field costs you completions.
  • No proof — no photos, no numbers, no names — so nothing to believe.

We build dedicated pages for exactly this reason. It's usually the cheapest CPL improvement available.

5. Nobody calls the lead fast enough

A lead contacted in five minutes and a lead contacted in five hours are not the same asset. In competitive categories the first business to respond wins a disproportionate share, regardless of who was cheaper.

If your team can't respond in minutes every time, that's what automation is for: an instant WhatsApp acknowledgement, routing to whoever is actually free, and a nudge if nobody has touched the lead in fifteen minutes. This costs nothing per lead and often moves the real cost per customer more than any campaign change.

6. Audiences too narrow, budgets too split

Six ad sets at ₹500 a day each will all sit in learning forever. One ad set at ₹3,000 gets enough data to make decisions with. Broad targeting with strong creative beats narrow interest stacking in almost every account we've touched in the last two years — let the creative do the targeting.

7. You're judging the wrong number

Cost per lead is a vanity metric if the leads don't close. We've cut a client's lead volume by 40%, raised CPL from ₹190 to ₹310, and increased their revenue — because the expensive leads converted at four times the rate.

Optimise for the cheapest lead and you'll get it. Whether you wanted it is a separate question.

Track cost per qualified lead, and if you can, cost per closed customer. Everything above it is a proxy.

Your cost per lead is high: what to fix first

In order: the offer, then the conversion event, then the landing page, then response time. Creative and targeting come after — they're the levers everyone reaches for first and the ones that matter least when the four above are broken.

We will do this on your account for nothing — request the audit.

Before deciding a cost per lead is too high, work out what your own margin allows — setting your own target from close rate and gross profit takes five minutes and replaces every benchmark you can find.

Questions people ask

What is the most common reason a cost per lead rises?

Creative fatigue and a landing page that does not convert account for more of it than targeting ever does. Both are visible in the data — rising frequency with falling click-through, or a healthy click-through with a poor form completion rate.

Is a high cost per lead always a problem?

No. It is only a problem relative to what your margin allows. Work out the ceiling from your close rate and gross profit first; plenty of accounts are being throttled by a target nobody derived from anything.

Does narrowing my targeting lower cost per lead?

Usually the opposite. Narrow audiences cost more per impression and starve the campaign of the conversions it needs to optimise. Broader targeting with sharper creative is the more reliable route.

How quickly should a fix show up?

A landing page or offer change can move the number within a week. A structural change needs the learning phase to settle first, so give it a fortnight before reading anything into it.
