Do Facebook ads work for small business? We coded 100 owners' own reports

We read what small business owners write in public about their own Facebook and Instagram ads and coded 100 reports. The answer depends heavily on who is posting, and the failures look alike.

Key findings

  • We coded 100 reports from owners describing their own Facebook or Instagram ads (52 on Hacker News, 48 on the Shopify Community). 26 said it worked, 17 were mixed, 41 said it did not work and 16 could not tell.
  • The two forums disagree sharply. On Hacker News 24 of 52 owners (46%) said it worked. On the Shopify Community, where new stores ask for help, 2 of 48 (4%) did and 27 (56%) said it did not.
  • Tiny and short tests almost always failed: 12 of the 13 owners spending under $500 a month said it did not work, and so did 7 of the 8 who ran ads for under a month. Of the 12 who ran ads for six months or more, 6 said it worked and only 1 said it did not.
  • Tracking problems came up in 21 of 100 reports, and in 14 of the 16 where the owner could not tell whether the ads paid.
  • Of the 58 reports that did not work or were mixed, owners most often blamed junk clicks or bots (8) and rising cost (8), then the landing page, site or offer (6). 18 of the 41 failures named no cause at all.
  • These are self-reported and lean toward problems. Hacker News skews to software founders and older posts, and Shopify threads are help requests. Read the totals as a picture of who posts, and read the patterns as the useful part.

Ask small business owners about Facebook ads and you hear two stories. One owner says Meta built the business. The next says it burned the budget. We wanted a count. On 22 November 2026 we read what small business owners have written in public about their own Facebook and Instagram ads and coded 100 reports, one per owner, for outcome, spend, time run and what they blamed.

So, do Facebook ads work for small business owners, by their own account? In this sample, 26% said yes, 17% said it partly worked, 41% said no and 16% could not tell. The split by forum matters more than the total. On Hacker News, where founders look back on businesses they built, 46% said it worked. On the Shopify Community, where new store owners ask for help, 4% did. In both, the failures look alike: tiny budgets, short tests, the wrong campaign goal and stores that could not see which clicks became sales.

This is the companion to our Google Ads owner study, which used the same sources and rules on Google Ads. Read the two together if you are choosing where to put a first budget. We run Meta accounts ourselves, so we have opinions, and we label them. None of our own client or account data is in here.

A third study in the series, on Microsoft Ads for small business, found far fewer owner reports, and a calmer picture: fewer clicks than Google, with buyers who converted about as well.

Where these 100 reports come from, and why they lean negative

Reddit blocked plain requests the day before, so we did not try it, and we did not work around any block or login. We used two public sources that load without one: Hacker News, where founders and small software and service businesses post, and the Shopify Community, where store owners ask for help with their Meta ads.

From 5,129 Hacker News comments and 729 Shopify topics, 100 reports passed our rules: an owner describing their own ads and saying how it went. We dropped agencies, media buyers, vendors, pure questions, bans with no result, organic posts and anyone who said they were outside the US, UK, Canada and Australia. 52 reports came from Hacker News and 48 from Shopify.

Two biases matter. First, the Shopify threads are help requests, so something has usually gone wrong already, and the search only returned threads from 2022 onwards. Second, Hacker News skews to software founders and to older posts: 36 of its 52 reports predate 2021. Only 8 reports named a country (5 UK, 2 US, 1 Canada). We expected a negative lean. We did not expect the two forums to land so far apart.

"Mixed" mostly means one of two things. The ads made sales but no profit, or they worked for a while and then fell away. A baking mix brand owner on Shopify wrote that "All sales have been through Meta ads", and then that the ads cost too much to leave any profit. That is a different problem from no sales at all, and it has different fixes.

The type of business mattered, with small groups. 9 of 57 online stores said Facebook ads worked, against 6 of 17 software businesses and 9 of 17 other businesses. The online store group is mostly the Shopify threads, so it carries that forum's lean. The winners in it tended to be specific products with a clear buyer. An online dog treat seller on Hacker News put it simply: "Almost all of my new customer acquisition is by way of Facebook ads."

Why the two forums disagree

Across all 100 reports, owners writing before 2021 said Facebook ads worked far more often: 16 of 36 (44%), against 10 of 64 (16%) since. It is tempting to blame Apple's iOS privacy change, which hit Meta tracking in 2021. The data does not support that on its own. Every Shopify report is from 2022 or later. Look only at Hacker News and the gap disappears: since 2021, 8 of its 16 owners said it worked.

In our view the difference is who is posting. A Hacker News comment is often a founder looking back at a business that survived. A Shopify thread is often an owner in the first weeks of a new store, asking why nothing has sold. Both are honest pictures of different owners. The iOS change still shows up: 6 reports named it. An online gift shop owner wrote: "That worked pretty well for a while but I really noticed a change after the iOS privacy ads opt-in change."

Hacker News blamed the traffic. All 8 junk-click reports came from there: clicks that never showed up in the owner's own analytics, likes from accounts far outside the target area, and app installs from people who never paid. Most of these are from 2012 to 2014, when click quality on Facebook was a common complaint among founders.

Shopify owners blamed the store and the setup. 5 of the 6 landing page and offer causes came from Shopify. A smart watch store owner suspected visitors could buy the same thing on Amazon. All 4 reports that boosted Instagram posts or chose a traffic, landing page view or add to cart goal came from Shopify too, and all 4 said it did not work. Our post on learning limited in Meta explains why the goal you pick decides who Meta shows the ad to.

The quiet finding is how many named nothing. 18 of the 41 owners who said it did not work gave no cause. In our experience the usual reasons in a small Meta account are a goal that is too far from a sale, too few conversions for the system to learn, and one tired creative. Owners rarely see those from inside Ads Manager.

Tracking: the owners who could not tell

Tracking problems came up in 21 of 100 reports, across every outcome. In 14 of the 16 reports where the owner could not say whether the ads paid, tracking was the reason. Ads Manager showed purchases the store never received. The store showed sales Meta never counted. One UK store found that a customer reloading the order status page had been counted as three purchases.

That is the most common Shopify thread we read after "clicks but no sales". If the numbers in Ads Manager do not match your orders, Meta's system learns from the wrong signal and every budget decision is a guess. Our guide to the Meta Conversions API covers the setup we check first.

Small and short tests rarely worked

Only 17 of 100 reports gave a monthly spend and 28 gave a time run, so these groups are small. The direction is still clear. 12 of the 13 owners spending under $500 a month said it did not work. 7 of the 8 who ran ads for under a month said the same, and none of those 8 said it worked. A wedding photography business owner on Hacker News wrote: "We tried it briefly for about a three-month stint and didn't get a single conversion."

At the other end, 12 owners had run ads for six months or more. 6 said it worked, 5 were mixed and 1 said it did not. Treat that with care. Owners keep running ads that work, so a long run is partly a result of success. Long runs can also fade: a local franchise owner wrote, "We went from about 20 good leads per month to 0-1 for the same ad spend."

What the owners who said it worked had in common (our view)

The 26 owners who said Facebook ads worked were a mixed group, and only 4 of them named what they changed. All 7 winners who gave a time had run ads for a month or more. The fixes they did name were new creative, knowing the customer well enough to target them, and retargeting past visitors or customer lists. A UK hardware maker described a first attempt that lost money, then a second season that worked once reviews and surveys had told them who their buyers were.

From that, and from what we see in accounts, these are the checks we would run before deciding Facebook ads do not work for your business:

  1. Is the campaign goal a sale or a lead? Boosted posts and traffic goals buy clicks from people who click. Pick purchases or leads, even if the cost per result looks high at first.
  2. Do Ads Manager and your orders agree? Compare a week of purchases or leads in both. Fix the pixel and Conversions API before you change anything else.
  3. Can the budget buy enough results to learn? If a week of spend buys a handful of sales, Meta cannot find more buyers. Our learning phase guide shows how to size it.
  4. Would you buy from the page? Check price against Amazon, delivery cost, reviews and how the page reads on a phone. Our Facebook ads for local business study lists the page checks we use.
  5. Have you tested more than one creative? Run several ads and replace them when results fade, before you judge the channel.
  6. Where are the clicks coming from? Check placements and countries in the breakdown. Remove the ones that never convert.
  7. Have you given it a fair window? Plan for at least a month and judge on cost per customer after margin.

If you want a second pair of eyes, our Meta ads agency page explains how we run accounts, and our free ad account audit starts with the tracking and goal checks above.

What we would change

In our view, most small businesses that "tried Facebook ads" ran a test that could not answer the question. A few weeks, a small budget, a boosted post or a traffic goal, and tracking nobody checked against real orders. When that fails, the owner decides Facebook does not work for their business. The Hacker News owners who explained their wins described long runs, fresh creative and a buyer they knew well. If you are working out what a fair test costs, our Facebook ads cost study collects real numbers from live accounts.

For agencies that serve small businesses, this is the story your clients have already read in forums before they call you. Start with the goal and the tracking, and show them the orders.

This study copies the method of our Google Ads owner study from 21 November 2026. Reddit blocked plain requests the day before, so we did not try it, and we did not work around any block, CAPTCHA or login. We used two sources that are readable without a login: Hacker News, through its public search API, and the Shopify Community, through its public JSON.

On Hacker News we ran 24 searches such as "facebook ads small business", "facebook ads didn't work" and "instagram ads small business", kept the 5,129 comments they returned, and filtered to 167 that name Facebook, Instagram or Meta ads near a first-person phrase about spending or results. On the Shopify Community we ran 12 searches, found 729 topics, downloaded the 264 with Facebook, Meta or Instagram in the title, and read every one that could hold a result. Two search pages returned a rate-limit error and were not retried.

A report counts when a person describes Facebook or Instagram ads for their own business, or ones they ran unpaid for a family member's business, and says something about the result. We excluded agencies, media buyers and employees describing client accounts, vendors and promotional posts, pure questions, bans and policy problems with no result, organic posts with no paid ads, idea-validation tests, second-hand stories, repeat posts by the same person, and anyone identifiably outside the US, UK, Canada and Australia. Threads where the owner saw sales but could not match them to the ads were coded "could not tell". Each report was coded once, by one person.

Limits. Everything here is self-reported and unchecked. People post when something has gone wrong, so failures are over-represented, and Shopify threads are mostly from new stores asking for help. Hacker News leans toward software founders, and 36 of its 52 reports predate 2021. Only 17 reports gave a monthly spend and 28 a time run, so those groups are small. No usernames are stored; the data file holds thread and post links only.

Questions about Facebook ads for small business

Do Facebook ads work for small business?

They can. In 100 owner reports we coded, 26% said Facebook ads worked, 17% had mixed results, 41% said they did not work and 16% could not tell. On Hacker News 46% said it worked; on the Shopify Community, where new stores ask for help, 4% did.

Are Facebook ads worth it with a small budget?

Rarely, in this sample. 12 of the 13 owners spending under $500 a month said Facebook ads did not work. In our view a small budget works best on one product, one goal (purchases or leads) and a few ads, run long enough for Meta to learn.

Why do Facebook ads get clicks but no sales?

The owners blamed junk clicks or bots, rising cost and the landing page or offer most often. On Shopify, all 4 owners who boosted posts or chose a traffic or add to cart goal said it did not work. A goal far from a purchase buys people who click, and they may never buy.

How long should I run Facebook ads before judging them?

In our view at least a month, judged on cost per customer after margin. 7 of the 8 owners who ran ads for under a month said they did not work, and none said they worked. All 7 winners who gave a time had run ads for a month or more.

Why don't my Facebook ads sales match Shopify?

Tracking problems came up in 21 of 100 reports. Common causes were the pixel counting a reload of the order page as a new purchase, sales lost after Apple's iOS privacy change, and the pixel and Conversions API not matching. Check a week of Ads Manager purchases against your orders.
