Creative agency or performance agency: who should own the ads?

The split that looks tidy on an org chart — one makes it, one runs it — is where most of the waste in paid media now lives.

Media Puppies · 15 October 2026

The creative agency vs performance agency question used to have an obvious answer. The creative agency made the advertising; the media agency bought the space. That division made sense when a campaign was three assets running for a quarter.

It makes much less sense now, and the handover between the two is where a lot of budget quietly goes.

What each is genuinely good at

A creative agency is good at the thing that cannot be derived from data: the idea, the positioning, the line that makes a brand recognisable. Craft, consistency, and the judgement to know what a brand should not say. These are real and they are hard to replace.

A performance agency is good at the feedback loop. Which of these nine openings held attention, which audience responded, what the cost per result was, and what to make next week as a consequence. Volume, iteration, and killing things quickly.

Neither is a substitute for the other. A performance team left alone produces ads that work this month and build nothing. A creative team left alone produces beautiful assets at a rate the platforms exhaust in a fortnight.

Where the handover breaks

Volume. Paid social consumes creative far faster than a traditional production cycle supplies it. A retainer producing a handful of polished assets per quarter cannot feed an account that needs a steady weekly refresh before frequency climbs.

Format. Assets built for a brand film get cropped into a vertical feed, captions get cut off, the logo lands under the interface. The fix is to brief for the placement from the start, which requires the creative team to know how the ad will actually be served.

Feedback. The performance team learns which hook worked and that information frequently never reaches the people making the next batch. Six months later the same tired opening is still being produced.

Accountability. When results fall, the creative agency says the media was mistargeted and the media agency says the creative was weak. Both are sometimes right and nobody is responsible.

Creative agency vs performance agency: three structures

Performance owns everything. Fastest loop, highest volume, best for direct response at scale. The risk is drift: a year of ads optimised for this month's cost per lead and no brand left behind. Mitigate it by having someone own the brand guidelines and review output monthly.

Creative owns brand, performance owns production. The creative agency sets positioning, the look, the tone, and produces the hero assets. The performance team produces the weekly variants inside those rules. This is the arrangement that works most often, and it depends on the rules being written down rather than being a matter of taste.

Creative owns everything. Works when the brand is the product and volume requirements are low. It rarely survives contact with a performance budget, because the production cadence does not match the consumption rate.

What to settle, whichever you choose

  1. A written creative rule set — logo use, colour, tone, what the brand will not claim — so variants can be produced without a review queue.
  2. A monthly concept quota, agreed as a number, with a named owner.
  3. A feedback loop with a date. Performance reports which angles held attention; creative briefs the next batch from that. Monthly, on the calendar.
  4. One person accountable for cost per customer. Not a committee.

Our own position is that production volume and the testing loop belong together, with brand rules set by whoever owns the brand — which is how we run the creative workflow. The wider version of this trade-off, across all disciplines rather than just creative, is in full service agency or specialists.

Questions people ask before hiring

Should a creative agency or a performance agency own the ads?

In most cases the brand rules should sit with whoever owns the brand, and the production volume and testing loop should sit with the performance team. Paid social consumes creative faster than a traditional production cycle supplies it.

Why does the handover between the two break down?

Four reasons: volume requirements the creative cycle cannot meet, assets built for the wrong placement and then cropped, performance learnings never reaching the people making the next batch, and nobody being accountable when results fall.

Can a performance team be trusted with brand?

Within written rules, yes. Without them, an account optimised purely for this month's cost per result will drift, because nothing in the feedback loop rewards consistency. The rules have to be documented rather than left to taste.

How much new creative does a paid social account need?

Enough to keep three to five genuinely different concepts live and to replace them before frequency climbs against a flat cost per result. Set it as a monthly quota with a named owner rather than reacting once performance drops.
