Facebook ads cost in 2026 depends on your market, your offer and your ads, and the YouTube videos that show real accounts make that plain. Eight videos from the past year reported a real cost per lead. Four of them fell between $7.72 and $21.80, and the full range ran from $1 to $2 for a free B2B guide to a roofing campaign that climbed past $50. Seven channels named a starting budget, from $10 to $100 a day.

Very little of this exists as text, because it lives in videos. So we searched YouTube for videos on Facebook ads cost, budgets, CPM and cost per lead uploaded between November 2025 and November 2026, then searched again for case studies and for UK, Canadian and Australian ad buyers. We screened 69 videos and kept the 29 that state concrete numbers. Where they named a country it was the US, UK or Australia. We read every transcript and logged 212 numbers, each labelled as a result from a real ad account, a cited benchmark, a rule of thumb or a worked example.

What Facebook ads cost per lead: real campaigns in recent videos

Eight videos reported a lead campaign from a real account, several with Ads Manager on screen. We take one figure per video: the after figure where they showed a before and after, otherwise the middle of what they reported. Five of these creators never say where they or their clients are, so some amounts may not be US dollars.

The best documented is Tyler Roicki's. $2,184 for a real estate agent in Conroe brought 283 leads at $7.72 each, from an offer of a list of new construction homes, and one closing. His best campaign ran on $15 a day at $6.34 a lead, after he changed the target area. Ash Davis shows a life settlement campaign at $12.33 a lead and $58.21 per booked appointment on $50 a day, and says results in that niche stand out against his other clients.

Two smaller samples sit in the middle. Tayyab Jamil shows a med spa account at $8.74 a lead after a new curiosity-hook ad with before-and-after visuals, against $16.95 on the previous campaign. Juan Carlos De Soto shows a shower remodeler in Texas at $21.80 a lead: 7 leads from $152 in about a week. He says remodeling leads often cost upwards of $80 to $100 through Google Ads or other channels.

We left one figure out. Spencer Claeys's Ads Manager shows $72 a lead for a roof and gutter cleaning ad, but he says it undercounts the leads that arrive by message, and the campaign optimises for messages. At the top of the chart, a roofer interviewed on Big Roofs Clips recalled a 2025 campaign that ran for five months at about $25 a lead before costs rose past $50.

Against the industry medians in WordStream and LocalIQ's 2026 Facebook ads benchmark report, the campaigns people put on YouTube look cheap. Real estate leads there cost $13.74 against Roicki's $7.72, home improvement $42.95 against De Soto's $21.80, and beauty and personal care $50.91 against the med spa's $8.74. Campaigns that people choose to put in a video are likely better than average, so read these as what a good campaign can reach.

CPM: nobody read one out from a real account

We expected CPM, the price of 1,000 impressions, to anchor most cost videos. Only 4 of the 29 gave a CPM figure, 9 numbers in all, and none came from a real account. 7 were worked examples, such as LYFE Marketing's point that at a $10 CPM, $100 buys about 10,000 impressions. LYFE gave $10 to $20 or more as a typical range. TrueProfit cited an $8.15 average Meta CPM from 2026 data it did not source in the video, and made the point that a $30 CPM can be a great one if it brings profitable customers. Jason Gan says there is no CPM benchmark because it depends on the country, so his team compares each campaign with its own past campaigns.

That matches how we work: watch CPM for sudden jumps, and judge the account on cost per lead or sale.

How much to spend on Facebook ads per day

Seven channels named a starting daily budget in dollars, from $10 to $100, with a median of $35. Rich From Anywhere says $10 a day can work for a local business with a small radius. Michael Diaz calls $50 a day the bare minimum. Nick Theriot launched a new account at $100 a day, and the roofer on Big Roofs Clips ran new campaigns at $100 a day. Ben Heath defines a small budget as under $3,000 a month and a tiny one as $600 or less.

Others tied the budget to the cost per sale the business can afford. These are the creators' rules of thumb:

  • Spend at least twice your break-even cost per purchase each day, and give each ad 2 to 3 times that break-even before turning it off, which is $132 at a $44 break-even (Parrottino).
  • Raise budgets in steps of 15% to 20% (Rich From Anywhere) or 20% to 30% (Parrottino). Meta publishes no safe percentage. It says a budget change may or may not restart learning, depending on its size.
  • Treat the first $10,000 as testing money (Parrottino), or spread $5,000 to $7,000 over six months (LYFE Marketing).
  • Aim for about 50 results a week per ad set, the point at which Meta's help pages say an ad set usually leaves the learning phase. In Nick Boddington's UK example that means £350 a day at a £50 cost per result, though he says it is not a strict rule.

For UK and Australian readers, Daniel Dimsey, who runs ads for martial arts gyms, gives country starting points: £25 to £50 a day in the UK, $35 to $70 in the US and A$50 to A$100 in Australia. Boddington's UK lead costs run from £1 to £5 for a free book to £50 to £100 for a mortgage lead. No video in our sample gave Canadian figures.

What we would take from this

A number from a live account tells you more than a rule of thumb, and a worked example shows how the maths works rather than what you will pay. In case-study videos, 79.3% of the numbers came from a real account. In cost, budget and how-to videos it was 20%, and 37.5% were worked examples.

  1. Start from your break-even cost per lead or sale. Work out what a customer is worth after margin, then what share of leads become customers. That number sets your budget.
  2. Optimise for an event Meta can see often enough. Shan Hanif makes the point that booked calls at $100 each give Meta too little data on a small budget. Optimise for leads, then judge them on bookings.
  3. Test new creative and offers before raising budget. The big improvements in these videos came from a new ad or offer. Meta treats a new ad as a significant edit, so plan for each one to restart learning in its ad set.
  4. Count what happens after the lead. Track booked appointments and signed jobs alongside form fills. Ash Davis's client paid $12.33 a lead and $58.21 per booked appointment.

We found videos through web searches of YouTube for Facebook ads cost, budget, CPM and cost per lead, then searched again for case studies and for UK, Canadian and Australian ad buyers. Of 69 videos screened, we left out 25 uploaded before 1 November 2025, 8 with no concrete cost or budget number, 3 about markets outside the US, UK, Canada and Australia, 1 without a transcript, 1 not in English, 1 that was unavailable, and 1 med spa budget video from the second search that we did not extract.

We read each transcript and logged every cost or budget number once, with its timestamp. Each is labelled as a result from a real ad account, a cited benchmark, a rule of thumb or a worked example (an illustration of the maths). Targets and break-even thresholds count as rules of thumb, and a figure restated as another number (a daily budget times 30, say) counts once. A case-study video is one built around results from specific ad accounts. For cost per lead we use one figure per video, and we leave out a figure the creator says is wrong.

Where auto-captions garbled a number we corrected it: $6.87 per install (captions "$687"; he says $6.87 twice), WordStream's $1.80 cost per click (captions "a$180"; the written report confirms $1.80), $200 to $300 per appointment (captions "$2 to $300"), $2,600 a month (captions "$2,6000") and $2,500 to win a client (captions "$25,000", against his own arithmetic).

These are creators who chose what to show, so the real results probably lean towards good campaigns. 12 of the 29 videos name a country for any number, so most dollar figures are unlabelled. The groups are small. None of this is our own ad account data. The CSV lists every number with a link to its video and timestamp.

Questions about Facebook ads cost

How much do Facebook ads cost?

There is no set price. Eight YouTube videos from the past year reported a real cost per lead from $1 to $2 up to about $50, with four of them between $7.72 and $21.80. WordStream and LocalIQ's 2026 report puts the median cost per lead at $27.39 across 452 US lead campaigns.

What is a good cost per lead on Facebook?

One you can afford after margin and close rate. WordStream's 2026 medians run from $13.74 for real estate to $61.56 for dentists. The real campaigns we logged ranged from $1 to $2 for a free B2B guide to $7.72 for real estate leads and $21.80 for a shower remodeler.

How much should I spend on Facebook ads per day?

The 7 channels that named a starting budget ranged from $10 to $100 a day, with a median of $35. David Parrottino sets it from cost per sale instead: at least twice your break-even cost per purchase each day.

What is the average Facebook CPM in 2026?

None of the 9 CPM figures in our videos came from a real account. TrueProfit cited an average of $8.15 from 2026 data and LYFE Marketing gave a typical $10 to $20+ range, but CPM depends on country, audience and season.

Are Facebook ads cheaper than Google Ads?

Per click, usually. WordStream and LocalIQ's 2026 report says the average Google Ads click costs more than double the average Meta click. Cost per customer is what matters, and that depends on how ready to buy each audience is.

How much do Facebook ads cost in the UK or Australia?

Daniel Dimsey suggests starting a martial arts gym at £25 to £50 a day in the UK and A$50 to A$100 in Australia. Nick Boddington puts UK leads at £1 to £5 for a free book and £50 to £100 for a mortgage lead.
