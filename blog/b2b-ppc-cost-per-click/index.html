B2B PPC cost per click: what software, insurance and finance keywords cost (2026)

We took the US keyword prices and the AI Overview checks from two of our November studies and pulled out the software, insurance and finance searches that B2B advertisers buy.

B2B PPC has a reputation for expensive clicks, and we wanted to know by how much. In early November we priced 339 US keywords in Semrush and then searched the same keywords on Google to see which ones showed an AI Overview. This page pulls out the searches a B2B advertiser is most likely to buy: software, business insurance, business finance and marketing services.

For one B2B niche close to home, see our PPC agency CPC study: what agencies pay for searches from businesses looking to hire one.

The short version: in our B2B PPC sample, the median CPC estimate for software keywords was more than twice the median of everything we priced, and every software search showed an AI Overview on the day we checked, in all four countries. Our view is that this changes how a B2B account should be judged. The figures, with the study each comes from, are free to download at the end.

What a software click costs

We priced 10 US software keywords. Their median Semrush CPC estimate was $28.87, against $12.32 for all 339 keywords in the study. Software came second of 11 industries by median, behind insurance. 6 of the 10 software keywords were at $20 or more, and 5 were at $30 or more.

Payroll software topped the list at $50.14 a click, with help desk software close behind at $47.52. The big platform searches were among the cheapest: crm software was $16.62 and erp software $11.96, the lowest of the ten. Our reading is that buyers searching for a whole category like CRM or ERP are early and often compare a few household names, while someone searching payroll or help desk software is closer to picking one. The data cannot prove that; it only shows the price gap.

Once you know the click price, the next choice is the offer; our B2B PPC offers study shows what 33 software companies ask for.

How B2B PPC compares across industries

The middle half of the software keywords sat between $17.32 and $36.28. Insurance was wider, from $18.67 to $61.55, and finance sat lower, from $5.94 to $18.44. Marketing services, the searches businesses use to find an agency, had a median of $22.49. Its most expensive keyword, google ads management, had a Semrush estimate of $228.10 a click, the second highest of all 339, and ppc management $95.27.

When we added the two keywords in the study that clearly name a business buyer, business insurance ($39.38) and small business loans ($20.57), to the ten software keywords, the median of that group of 12 stayed at $28.87, because one landed above the middle and one below it.

Insurance: a consumer price war with a business keyword inside

Insurance had the highest median of the 11 industries, $35.44, and its most expensive keyword was auto insurance quotes at $84.02. That keyword is a consumer search. The 4 quote searches had a median of $71.04, while the other insurance keywords had a median of $19.14.

For a B2B advertiser, the line that matters is business insurance at $39.38 a click, above the software median. Our view is that commercial insurance brokers and insurtech firms should not read the industry median as their price. Their own keyword sits above the software median, and the quote searches that sit at the top of the insurance list are mostly consumers.

Finance came in below the overall median

Finance came sixth of 11, with a median of $9.47, below the $12.32 median for all keywords. Mortgage rates was the cheapest finance keyword at $1.97. The most expensive was debt consolidation loans at $32.37.

The business keyword in the list, small business loans, was $20.57, more than double the finance median and with 1,220,000 US searches a month in the Semrush data. It is one keyword, so it proves little on its own. For a lender or a fintech selling to small firms, our reading is that the finance median can understate what business-facing searches cost.

Every software search had an AI Overview

When we searched the same keywords on Google, all 10 software keywords showed an AI Overview in the US. Across all 339 keywords the rate was 57.8%. Marketing services came next at 93.3%, insurance at 90% and finance at 70%. In the insurance and finance lists, the keywords without one were local searches: insurance agent near me, financial advisor near me and tax preparation near me, plus credit repair.

We then checked the same software keywords in the UK, Canada and Australia. All 10 showed an AI Overview in each country. That is 40 out of 40 software searches across the four markets we work in.

The answers drew on a mix of sources. In what our scraper captured from the 10 US software AI Overviews, reddit.com, forbes.com, oracle.com and en.wikipedia.org were each cited 3 times, alongside vendor sites such as salesforce.com, mailchimp.com and bamboohr.com, and the median AI Overview showed 3 sources. All 10 captures were flagged as possibly cut off, so the full answers may have cited more.

We did not measure ads in that study, so we cannot say whether AI Overviews lower ad clicks on these searches. What we can say is that on every software search in our sample, and on business insurance and small business loans too, an AI Overview was on the US results page that day.

Why we judge B2B PPC on cost per opportunity

Put the two findings together. At the software median, 100 clicks cost $2,887 on the Semrush estimate, against $1,232 at the median for all keywords. Those are plain multiplications of the median; we did not measure anyone's spend. They show the size of the bet before any lead arrives.

Our view is that cost per click and even cost per lead are the wrong scorecard at these prices. A B2B form fill can be a student, a competitor or a buyer two years away. The number that decides whether B2B PPC is working is cost per sales opportunity, and in the end cost per unit of pipeline: total spend divided by the deals your sales team accepts as real. That needs the CRM to send qualified stages back to Google Ads, which we cover in teaching the ad platforms what a real customer looks like.

Our view is that the AI Overview finding points the same way, though our data does not test it. If more of the early research happens in an AI answer, the clicks that reach your ad may come from people further along, and fewer of them. In our view that makes each click worth tracking all the way to revenue, and it makes broad research keywords such as crm software a weaker buy than the narrower, higher-priced searches where the buyer is choosing.

What we would do with these numbers

  • Price the keyword list before the budget. In our sample the software keywords ran from $11.96 to $50.14. Our view is that a budget that buys too few clicks a day for the bidding to learn is better spent on fewer keywords.
  • Measure cost per opportunity from week one. Import qualified stages from the CRM so bidding learns from real buyers, and report spend against pipeline.
  • Look past the industry median. Business insurance ($39.38) was about double the median of the non-quote insurance keywords ($19.14), and small business loans ($20.57) was more than double the finance median ($9.47).
  • Write for the reader who has already seen an AI answer. In our view the ad and landing page should answer what a summary cannot: price range, setup time, proof from customers like the searcher.
  • Test LinkedIn when search is thin. Where search volume is small, as with help desk software at 5,400 searches a month, our LinkedIn ads arithmetic shows how to check whether targeting by job title pays.

This page collects nothing new. It takes figures from two studies we published in November 2026. The cost per click figures come from our CPC by industry study: 339 US keywords priced in the Semrush Keyword Overview (US database, USD) on 3 November 2026. They are keyword-tool estimates of what advertisers bid, and an account can pay more or less. The AI Overview figures come from our AI Overviews study: the same keywords searched on 3 November 2026 (UTC) through Apify's Google search scraper, desktop, in English, with no city set; the UK, Canada and Australia runs used a subset of 118 keywords.

The software, insurance, finance and marketing services lists are the keywords those studies chose, 10 or 15 per industry, so they are a sample of each market. Most insurance and finance keywords are consumer searches; where we talk about B2B we name the keywords that point to a business buyer. Our "B2B group" of 12 keywords (the 10 software keywords plus business insurance and small business loans) is our own grouping, computed from the CPC study's keyword file.

Limits. Keyword tools estimate CPC from bid data and do not see any one account's invoices. AI Overviews vary by person, place and time, and our scraper can miss one that loads late. Each country was checked once, on one day, so we could not test how stable the results are. The scraper often captured only part of an AI Overview, so the source counts and cited domains describe what it captured. Neither source shows conversion rates or deal values, so nothing here says what a B2B lead or customer costs. The "100 clicks" figures are plain arithmetic on the median; we did not measure anyone's spend. Questions about B2B PPC costs

How much does B2B PPC cost per click?

In our sample of US Semrush estimates from 3 November 2026, the median software keyword was estimated at $28.87 a click, business insurance $39.38 and small business loans $20.57, against a median of $12.32 for all 339 keywords we priced. Your own account can pay more or less than these estimates.

Which B2B keywords are the most expensive?

Among our 10 software keywords, payroll software ($50.14) and help desk software ($47.52) were the most expensive. In marketing services, google ads management was estimated at $228.10 a click.

Do AI Overviews show on B2B searches?

On 3 November 2026, all 10 software keywords we checked showed a Google AI Overview in the US, the UK, Canada and Australia. Across all 339 US keywords the rate was 57.8%.

Should B2B PPC be judged on cost per lead?

Our view is that cost per lead is too early a measure at B2B click prices. We judge accounts on cost per sales opportunity and pipeline, using CRM stages sent back to the ad platform.
