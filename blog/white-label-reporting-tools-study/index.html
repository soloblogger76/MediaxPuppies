White label reporting tools compared: what 20 vendors promise, and where white label starts

We opened the white label reporting tools that come up in search and coded what each one says on its own pages. A logo swap is nearly universal. A custom domain, your own email sender and white label on the cheapest plan are not.

Key findings

  • Of 20 white label reporting tools we read on 21 November 2026, 17 (85%) offered a custom logo and 16 (80%) custom colours. Only 13 (65%) stated a custom domain, 10 (50%) said reports go out from the agency's own email, and 8 of 20 stated both.
  • White label sat on the cheapest paid plan at 10 of 20 tools (50%). At 7 (35%) it needed a higher tier, a paid add-on or a separate quote, and 3 (15%) did not say.
  • The median cheapest published plan was $49 a month across 15 tools with USD list prices (range $20 to $139). The cheapest stated price that includes white label had a median of $97 across 13 tools.
  • Half the tools (10 of 20) priced by a client-like unit: clients or accounts, locations, data sources or dashboards. 18 (90%) offered a free trial, and 11 of the 15 that gave a length said 14 days.
  • Connectors and automation were common but not universal: Google Analytics 75%, Google Ads 65%, Meta ads 65%, LinkedIn 50% (n=20). 15 (75%) stated scheduled email reports and 14 (70%) mentioned AI summaries or insights.

Most articles about white label reporting tools are written by the tool vendors, and each one ends with its own product at the top. We wanted the buyer's view without the ranking. On 21 November 2026 we ran seven searches an agency owner would type, opened the first 20 tools that sell white label reporting to agencies, and coded what each tool's own white label and pricing pages say.

The short answer: almost every tool lets you put your logo on a report, and far fewer take the vendor out of the rest of the experience. 17 of 20 (85%) offered a custom logo. 13 (65%) stated a custom domain, 10 (50%) said reports can go out from your own email address, and only 8 stated both. White label sat on the cheapest paid plan at half the tools. At 7 of 20 it needed a higher tier, an add-on or a quote.

If you want to know what belongs inside a client report, our white label reporting guide covers the content, the template and the monthly routine. This post is the tool side that sits next to it. We do not sell software and we did not rank anyone. Where we name a tool, it is to say what its page states.

The median tool stated 4.5 of the 7 features. 2 tools stated all 7 and 10 stated 5 or more. One tool, BrightLocal, said plainly that it does not offer one of them: its FAQ says "you can't host the reports on your own website's domain", and explains that shared report links carry no BrightLocal branding instead. We counted that as a no. It was the only no in the table. Everything else that is missing is a page that does not say.

Our view: the domain and the sender are the two that matter most. A report link on the vendor's domain, or an email from a vendor address, tells a curious client the name of the tool in one click.

Where white label starts: entry plan, higher tier or add-on

We expected white label to be a paid upgrade almost everywhere. It was less common than that. 10 of 20 tools (50%) put it on their cheapest paid plan, and several said so in plain words: Reporting Ninja's pricing page reads "Every feature, every integration, on every plan", and Swydo's reads "One Plan. All Features Included."

The other half is where the cost hides:

  • Higher tier only (3 tools). Synup lists white labeling as "Available on Premium and above." Cloud Campaign puts standard white labeling on its Team plan and advanced white labeling on Agency. Search Atlas adds client portals and branded dashboards on its Agency plan.
  • Paid add-on (3 tools). Databox sells branding and white labeling as an add-on to its agency plan, and calls it "the only add-on outside the base plan." Semrush puts branding and white labeling in a Pro Report add-on. ClicData lists "Optional White Label" under its Enterprise cloud plan.
  • Separate quote (1 tool). Zoho Analytics sends white label buyers to "Get a Price Quote".
  • Not stated (3 tools). The pages we read named white label features without saying which plan carries them.

Even an entry plan can hold back one piece. Whatagraph includes white label reports on its Max plan and keeps the custom report domain for its Prime plan or an add-on. AgencyAnalytics' pricing page lists custom domain and custom email in every tier, while its white label feature page still says "select plans". We followed the pricing page.

Our view: price it on your real client count a year from now. A per-client tool that looks cheap at 5 clients can cost more than a flat tier at 40. A per-data-source tool adds a charge each time a client adds Meta to Google Ads.

Trials, connectors, scheduling and AI

18 of 20 tools (90%) offered a free trial and 1 more had a free plan. Of the 15 that gave a length, 11 said 14 days. 2 said 15 days and 2 said 7.

Connectors were less complete than we expected for tools sold to agencies. 15 tools (75%) named Google Analytics, 13 (65%) Google Ads, 13 (65%) Meta ads and 10 (50%) LinkedIn. 10 named all four. Some of the gaps are tools built for one job: local SEO, social media or a single ad platform. 10 tools put a number on their integrations, from 12 to 500, with a median of 50.

15 tools (75%) stated scheduled email reports, and 14 (70%) mentioned AI summaries, insights or an AI assistant. AI is now a headline on most of these pages. Several offer to write the commentary for you.

Our view: an AI summary is a draft. The client is paying for someone who knows why the numbers moved, and a tool cannot know that a promotion ended or a competitor raised bids.

What a white label client report should contain (our view)

A tool decides how the report looks. What the report says is up to you. This is what we put in a client report, and what we would ask any tool, or any white label PPC partner, to support:

  1. The result the client pays for, first. Leads, sales or booked calls against the target, with cost per result. One number on the cover beats a page of charts.
  2. Spend against budget. What was spent, what was planned, and the pace for the rest of the month.
  3. What changed and why. Written by the person running the account, in plain words. This is the part an AI summary cannot do alone.
  4. What we did this period. Tests launched, keywords added or cut, creative changed, budgets moved.
  5. What we will do next. Two or three actions with dates, so the next report can check them.
  6. One page of channel detail. Search terms, top ads or audiences, for the client who wants to dig.
  7. Your brand end to end. Logo, colours, your domain on the link and your address on the email. The table above shows how often each is stated.

On 21 November 2026 we ran seven searches an agency owner would type, starting with "white label reporting tool", "white label marketing reports" and "agency reporting software white label". We took tools in the order they came up and counted each once, at its first appearance.

We skipped listicles and directories (The Digital Project Manager, SourceForge, G2 and similar), embedded analytics sold to SaaS companies rather than agencies (Luzmo, Toucan, Qrvey), guides with no tool, and two vendors whose pages gave us nothing to code: datapine now redirects to construction BI pages, and Improvado describes a data pipeline with one line on branded dashboards.

For each tool we saved its white label or product page and its pricing page as text, with curl and a pause between requests. Where the search result was the vendor's own blog post, we read its product page instead. We coded only what the vendor says about its own product.

A field is "not stated" when the pages we read are silent. The feature may still exist in the help centre, a demo or the app. Prices are the vendors' public list prices on the day, mixing monthly and annual billing and two currencies; the medians use USD list prices only.

Limits. 20 tools from one day of US search results, and search order is not market share. Several pages carried Black Friday offers. One person coded the pages, and where a vendor's own pages disagreed we noted it beside the quote and followed the pricing page or FAQ. We did not sign up for any trial or test any tool.

Questions about white label reporting tools

What is a white label reporting tool?

Software that pulls a client's marketing data into reports and dashboards and shows them under the agency's brand. In our sample of 20 tools from 21 November 2026, 85% offered a custom logo, 65% a custom domain and 50% reports sent from the agency's own email.

Is white label included on the cheapest plan?

At half the tools we read, yes. 10 of 20 put white label on the cheapest paid plan. 3 kept it for a higher tier, 3 sold it as an add-on, 1 asked for a quote and 3 did not say which plan carries it.

How much do white label reporting tools cost?

On the vendors' public pages, the median cheapest plan was $49 a month across 15 tools with USD prices, from $20 to $139. The cheapest stated price that includes white label had a median of $97 across 13 tools. Half the tools price by clients, locations, data sources or dashboards.

Do white label reporting tools connect to Google Ads and Meta?

Most do. 13 of 20 named Google Ads and 13 named Meta ads, 15 named Google Analytics and 10 named LinkedIn. Check the exact connector your client needs before you sign up, because several tools are built for one channel.

Do I still need to write the report if the tool has AI summaries?

Yes, in our view. 14 of 20 tools mentioned AI summaries or insights. They save time on the first draft, but the client is paying for someone who knows why the numbers moved and what happens next.
