Interior design leads in India: the offer decides everything

Interior design is the category where changing the offer beats changing the targeting by the widest margin we see.

Media Puppies · 14 July 2026 · Updated 11 October 2026

Interior design leads have a specific problem: the purchase is large, slow and emotional, and the standard industry offer — "book a free consultation" — asks for a big commitment in exchange for nothing specific.

That mismatch is why so many accounts in this category produce enquiries nobody can convert.

To fix interior design leads, change the offer first

Compare these:

StandardBetter
Book a free consultationGet a room-by-room quote in 24 hours, no site visit
Transform your home2BHK modular kitchen, ₹2.8L–₹4.2L, 6 weeks
Get a free designSee 8 kitchens we finished in Gurugram this quarter, with prices

The right-hand column asks for less and promises something specific. In this category that change routinely moves cost per qualified lead more than any campaign work — which is why we open with it rather than with targeting.

Price, publicly

The most common objection is fear of an open-ended bill. Most competitors hide the number, which makes the fear worse.

Publishing ranges — per room, per configuration — does three things: filters people who can't afford it, positions you as straightforward, and gets you a better conversation when someone does enquire.

Creative that works

  • Real projects, real homes. Stock renders are instantly recognisable and they signal that you have nothing of your own to show.
  • Before and after of a space — allowed here, unlike in clinics, and extremely effective.
  • The same flat layout the viewer lives in. "This is a 2BHK in a society like yours" beats a luxury villa nobody can map onto their life.
  • Timeline as the hook. Delays are the category's reputation problem. Promising and showing a schedule is a differentiator.

In this category the ad account is rarely the constraint. The offer and the quote turnaround are.

Follow-up: speed, then substance

An interior enquiry is comparing three firms by evening.

  1. Instant WhatsApp with a portfolio link and the price range.
  2. Human call within minutes to understand the flat and the timeline.
  3. The quote when you promised it. If you said 24 hours, 24 hours is the product demonstration.
  4. A sequence for the ones who go quiet — most aren't lost, they're comparing. Here's the version we run.

What to measure

  • Cost per qualified lead — flat type, locality and budget known.
  • Cost per quote sent, which is the real mid-funnel number.
  • Quote-to-booking rate. If quotes go out and nothing closes, the problem is pricing or presentation, not traffic.
  • Time from enquiry to quote. Track it weekly; it drifts.

If your enquiries arrive mostly by phone, the call-tracking side of this is on our home services page.

Projects of this size behave like any other considered purchase, so the high-ticket approach to lead generation — more qualification, fewer leads, deeper conversion events — is usually the right shape.

Questions people ask

Why are interior design leads so poor quality?

Because the standard offer asks for a large commitment in exchange for very little. "Book a free consultation" requires time, a home visit and a decision, while promising nothing specific. The enquiries that result are browsing rather than buying.

Should I publish prices?

Yes, at least a per-square-foot band or a starting package. It is the single most effective filter in the category: it removes people who were never going to proceed and it signals that you are not going to quote a number only after a two-hour meeting.

What creative works for interior design?

Finished work in homes that look like the buyer’s, not showrooms. Before-and-after of real projects, with the budget band stated. Renders convert worse than photographs because buyers have learned that renders flatter.

What should I measure?

Cost per site visit or per measured consultation, not cost per enquiry. The gap between the two numbers is where the entire category’s wasted spend lives.
