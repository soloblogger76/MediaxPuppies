How to choose an influencer marketing agency, and when to skip one

Influencer agencies sell access, management and measurement. Only the last one is hard to do yourself, so that is where to judge them.

Media Puppies · 20 October 2026

An influencer marketing agency finds creators, negotiates with them, manages the content and reports what it produced. For a brand, the value is time saved and, at its best, a measurement system that tells you which creators actually sell. Choosing an influencer marketing agency well comes down to checking that third part, because finding and briefing creators is work any organised team can learn.

This guide covers what the work involves, how agencies charge, the questions that separate good ones from list-sellers, and when you should not hire one at all.

What an influencer marketing agency does

  1. Creator discovery. Shortlisting creators whose audience matches your buyer by location, age, language and interest, and checking for fake followers.
  2. Negotiation and contracts. Rates, deliverables, posting dates, usage rights and exclusivity.
  3. Briefing and approvals. A brief that gives the creator the key message without killing their style, and a review step before anything goes live.
  4. Amplification. Running the best creator videos as paid ads through partnership ads on Meta, which often matters more than the organic post.
  5. Measurement. Tracking links, discount codes and post-campaign sales data tied to each creator.

How influencer agencies charge

Agencies usually work on one of three models: a fixed management fee on top of creator costs, a percentage of the total creator budget, or a bundled price per campaign where the creator fees are hidden inside. The bundled model makes it hard to see what each creator was paid, so ask for creator fees to be shown separately whichever model you choose. To sanity-check what a creator is asking, the influencer rate calculator gives a starting range from follower count and engagement.

Questions to ask an influencer marketing agency

  • How do you check audience quality? Look for answers about audience location, engagement patterns and follower growth spikes, rather than follower counts.
  • Who owns the content, and for how long? If you want to run creator videos as ads, usage rights must be in the contract.
  • How will you measure sales, not just views? Unique codes, tracked links and partnership ads with conversion tracking are the minimum.
  • Can I see creator fees line by line? A good agency has no reason to refuse.
  • What happens if a creator underdelivers? Reposts, replacements or credit should be written down before the campaign starts.

Red flags to walk away from

  • Guaranteed views or reach. Views are easy to inflate and say nothing about sales.
  • Reports made only of screenshots. You should get tracked links, code redemptions and, for paid amplification, access to the ad account data.
  • No direct contact with creators, ever. Some layering is normal, but a brand should be able to approve content and talk to a creator when something goes wrong.
  • Creator lists with no audience data. A name and a follower count is not a recommendation.
  • Refusing to put usage rights in writing. Without them, your best-performing content cannot legally become an ad.

A worked example of judging results

Say a brand works with ten micro creators through an agency, at a total cost of 3 lakh including fees. The posts get strong views, and the tracked codes record 150 orders. Cost per order is 2,000. If the brand's break-even cost per order is 900, the organic campaign lost money even though it looked busy.

Now the brand takes the three creators whose codes sold best and runs their videos as partnership ads. Those ads reach buyers at a far lower cost per order, because the creative is already proven. This is the pattern behind most profitable creator programmes: use influencers to find winning content, then scale it with paid media. When to skip the influencer marketing agency

If you plan to work with a handful of creators a month, a single coordinator inside the business can usually do it, with direct relationships and no middle layer. An agency makes sense when you need dozens of creators across languages or cities at once, or when nobody in-house has time to chase deliverables.

Questions people ask before hiring

What does an influencer marketing agency do?

It finds and vets creators, negotiates rates and rights, manages briefs and approvals, and reports results. Good agencies also help run creator content as paid ads.

How do influencer marketing agencies charge?

Usually a management fee, a percentage of creator spend, or a bundled campaign price. Ask for creator fees to be shown separately whichever model is used.

Is it better to hire an influencer agency or do it in-house?

For a few creators a month, in-house is usually cheaper and builds direct relationships. Agencies help when volume, languages or deadlines outgrow a small team.

How do you measure influencer marketing ROI?

Use unique codes, tracked links and partnership ads with conversion tracking, then compare cost per order with your break-even cost per order.
