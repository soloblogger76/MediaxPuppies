Google Partner agency claims: what 43 US, UK and Australian PPC agencies show, and what we could check

We read the websites of 43 US, UK and Australian PPC agencies for Google Partner, Premier Partner, Microsoft and Meta claims, then tried to check each one in Google's public directory. Two thirds claim a Google tier. None of the tiers could be confirmed without running Google's own scripts.

Almost every PPC agency pitch includes a Google badge somewhere. We wanted to know how common the claim really is, what it looks like, and whether a buyer can check it in a few minutes. On 20 November 2026 we read the homepages, about pages and PPC pages of 43 US, UK and Australian agencies and coded every Google Partner, Premier Partner, Microsoft and Meta claim we found.

The short answer: 29 of the 43 (67.44%) present themselves as a Google Partner agency, and 12 of those claim Premier Partner. 11 link to a Google profile. Google's public directory confirmed that 8 of those profiles exist, and it would not show the tier to a plain request for any of them. Our earlier piece on whether the Google Partner badge is worth anything gives our opinion of the badge. This study counts what agencies actually show.

The sample is the same 43 agencies we used for our study of published Google Ads management fees, so we could set partner claims beside fees, contracts and reporting. We name no agency here. The full coding, with the evidence string for each claim, is in the data file.

How many agencies claim to be a Google Partner agency

We expected nearly every agency to claim something. Two thirds did. The other 14 showed no Google tier anywhere we looked. Some of them publish detailed fees, terms and service lists, so a missing badge did not mean a thin website.

Australian agencies claimed a tier most often and US agencies least often. With 14 or 15 agencies per country, a difference of two or three agencies moves these shares a lot, so we would not read a national pattern into it.

How the claim appears varies. 19 of the 29 show a badge image on the homepage or about page. The other 10 make the claim only in words, sometimes in a strip of trust signals next to review scores and years in business. 2 sites show both tiers in different places: one says Premier in its text while its badge image reads Partner, and another shows a Partner logo and a Premier logo side by side. We coded both as Premier claims, since that is what a reader would take away.

What Google's published criteria require

Google publishes the rules for both tiers in its Google Ads Help article on becoming a Google Partner or Premier Partner, which we read on 20 November 2026. The program has three tiers: Member, Partner and Premier Partner. Any company can join as a Member with its manager account. The badge starts at Partner.

Tier and categoryWhat Google publishes
Partner: performanceOptimization score of at least 70% on the registered manager account
Partner: spendUS$10,000 of ad spend across managed accounts over 90 days
Partner: certificationsAt least 50% of account strategists certified in Google Ads (capped at 100 users), plus a certification in each product area with US$500 or more of spend in 90 days
Premier PartnerMeet the Partner requirements and rank in the top 3% of participating companies in the country, set once a year

Google says the Partner requirements are checked daily. For Premier, it lists 5 factors that include, among others, growth in spend from existing clients, growth from new clients, client retention year over year, the share of spend outside Search, and total annual spend. Google also notes that Premier is relative to other companies in the same country and may not be offered in every country.

Read plainly, these are tests of scale, spend growth, certification and adoption of Google's recommendations. None of them measures the cost per lead or return on spend in a client's account. That is the core of our view below.

Google Premier Partner claims in this sample

12 of 43 agencies (27.91%) claim Premier Partner. Google reserves the tier for the top 3% of participating companies in each country. Those two numbers measure different groups, so they do not contradict each other. Our 43 agencies are the ones that rank on page one for fee and pricing searches, which favours firms with large sites and marketing budgets. The 3% is counted across every company enrolled in the program, which includes thousands of small ones.

One more detail matters. Google assesses Premier once a year, so a badge from a past year can stay on a page after the status changes, and a current badge can be missing from an old page. A badge that names the year is easier to trust than one that does not.

One agency in the sample says outright that it is a Google Partner and not a Premier Partner, and explains the difference. We liked that. It is the clearest statement of status we found.

Can you verify a Google Partner badge?

Partly. 11 of the 29 claimants link to a profile in Google's Partners directory. The older link format opens an empty page shell. We tried the same profile id on Google's current directory with a plain request, and 8 returned a page titled with the agency's name. 3 returned "Not Found". That can mean an old id, a moved profile or a lapsed listing, so we do not read it as a false claim.

The tier itself is the part a buyer cares about, and it is the part we could not see. The directory page loads Partner or Premier status with JavaScript, and the search box works the same way. We did not run those scripts, log in or use any workaround, so the tier is verified for 0 of the 29 claims. The other 18 claimants give no profile link at all, and the directory cannot be searched without a browser.

For a buyer with a normal browser this takes two minutes: open Google's Partners directory, search the agency name, and read the tier on its profile. Do that before a sales call, and check that the company name on the profile matches the legal entity on the contract.

Microsoft and Meta partner badges

10 agencies claim Microsoft Advertising partner status and 9 claim Meta Business Partner status. Every one of them also claims a Google tier. No agency without a Google claim showed a Microsoft or Meta partner badge. 7 of the 10 Microsoft claims came from Premier claimants, and the Meta claims split 4 Premier and 5 Partner.

We counted only partner wording or a link to the partner directory. Individual certifications such as "Facebook Ads Certified" and plain platform logos did not count, and several sites use those in the same badge row. A Meta or Microsoft logo next to a Google badge says the agency runs those platforms. A partner badge says the agency is enrolled in that platform's program.

Three things stood out. First, a badge did not go with more price transparency. Agencies with no claim published their fees most often, and Premier claimants used hourly, hybrid or percentage pricing more often among those that publish. Second, Partner claimants stated contract and notice terms more often than either other group, and more of them offered rolling terms. Third, Premier claimants listed more services but stated a reporting cadence on fewer of their pages. We expected the reverse on reporting. A bigger firm may leave reporting to the proposal, so this is a question to ask, and no verdict on any agency.

What the Google Partner badge does and does not tell you (our view)

The badge is a real signal of a few things. It tells you the agency's manager account spends at least the published threshold each quarter, that most of its account staff have passed Google's exams, and that its accounts score well on Google's own optimization score. Premier adds that the agency grew spend and kept clients faster than most of its national peers last year. Those are fair signs of scale and continuity, and a Premier agency usually has a Google contact who can help with policy problems and suspended accounts.

It does not tell you who will run your account, how they handle your margins, or whether they push back on Google's suggestions. Optimization score rises when recommendations are applied, and some of those recommendations widen match types or move budget in ways a small account cannot afford. Spend growth across a portfolio is also a different thing from your cost per lead.

Our checklist, in order:

  1. Check the tier yourself. Search the agency in Google's Partners directory in your own browser and read the tier and company name on the profile.
  2. Ask which entity holds the status. A badge can belong to a parent company, a sister brand or a delivery partner. Ask who will actually log in to your account.
  3. Ask what they declined. Have them name a Google recommendation they dismissed recently and why. A good answer shows judgement that no badge measures.
  4. Get reporting in writing. In this sample the badge did not predict whether reporting was described at all. Ask how often, in what format and who writes it.
  5. Read the terms beside the badge. Contract length, notice and fee model shape your experience more than the tier does.

The 43 agencies are the sample from our study of published Google Ads management fees: US, UK and Australian agencies taken in search result order, minus directories, software vendors and agencies based elsewhere. On 20 November 2026 we fetched each homepage and its first about link with a plain HTTP client, and kept the visible text, image alt text, image file names and link targets. 2 homepages returned empty replies, so for those we used only the pages saved the day before.

A claim counts when the agency's own page names the tier in text, alt text, a file name or a link label. Advice such as "pick a Google Partner" does not count. 7 badge images had no readable text, so we opened them and read them by eye. Premier is coded when any page names Premier; 2 sites show both tiers in different places. Microsoft and Meta count only partner wording or a link to their partner directories. Individual certifications and plain platform logos do not count.

Verification. Older profile links on agency sites load an empty page shell. The same profile id on Google's current directory returns the company name without scripts, so we used that to confirm a listing exists. The Partner or Premier tier only appears after the page runs JavaScript, and directory search is JavaScript only. We ran no scripts, used no login and did nothing to get around any block, so no tier is verified. A link that did not resolve may be an old id or a moved profile, and we do not read it as a false claim.

Limits. 43 agencies is a small sample from one search tool, and the three groups (12, 17 and 14) are smaller still. The cross-tabs are counts with no adjustment for agency size or country, and they show association only. Pages change, and badges can be out of date in either direction. Contract and notice come from any page of the agency's site; services and reporting cover the 37 agencies with a PPC services page.

Questions about Google Partner agencies

How many PPC agencies are Google Partners?

Of the 43 US, UK and Australian agencies we checked on 20 November 2026, 29 claimed a Google tier on their own pages: 17 claimed Partner and 12 claimed Premier Partner. We could not verify any tier, because Google's directory shows it only with JavaScript.

What does an agency need to become a Google Partner?

Google's published criteria have three parts: an optimization score of at least 70% on the agency's manager account, US$10,000 of spend across managed accounts over 90 days, and at least 50% of account strategists certified in Google Ads.

What is the difference between Google Partner and Premier Partner?

Premier Partners meet the Partner requirements and rank in the top 3% of participating companies in their country, assessed once a year on client growth, retention, product mix and annual spend. It is a relative ranking within a country.

How do I check if an agency is really a Google Partner?

Search the agency's name in Google's Partners directory in your browser and read the tier on its profile. In our study 11 of 29 claimants linked a profile, and 8 of those links opened a listing with the agency's name.

Is a Google Premier Partner agency better than a Google Partner?

Not for every account. Premier reflects scale, spend growth and client retention across the agency's whole portfolio. It says nothing about who runs your account or the cost per lead you will get, so judge an audit of your own account first.
