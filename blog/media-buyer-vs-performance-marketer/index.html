Media buyer, performance marketer, growth manager: the real differences

Three titles, heavily overlapping job ads, and very different people behind them. Hiring the wrong one is an expensive and slow mistake to discover.

Media Puppies · 15 October 2026

Media buyer vs performance marketer vs growth manager is a question people ask after reading three job descriptions that sound identical. They are not identical, and the difference is mostly about how far from the ad account the job extends.

Media buyer: owns the account

The narrowest and deepest of the three. A media buyer owns campaign structure, budgets, optimisation events, audiences and the creative testing cycle inside the platforms. Depth is the point — someone who has run hundreds of accounts recognises a pattern in a week that a generalist takes a quarter to see.

What they typically do not own: the offer, the landing page, the email sequence, pricing, or what happens to a lead after it arrives.

Hire one when your spend is large enough that a few points of efficiency pays a salary, and someone else already owns the funnel around it.

Performance marketer: owns the funnel

Everything a media buyer does, plus the destination. Landing pages, forms, tracking, the measurement layer, and usually the argument with the sales team about lead quality. Broader, and in most Indian businesses more useful, because the expensive problems usually sit between the ad and the enquiry rather than inside the ad account.

The trade-off is depth. A performance marketer covering five responsibilities will not know any platform as well as a dedicated buyer does.

Hire one when you are the first marketing hire's manager and need one person who can see the whole paid funnel — which is most companies below a certain size.

Growth manager: owns the number

Paid is one input among several. A growth manager is accountable for a business outcome — new customers, activated users, revenue — and works across acquisition, onboarding, retention and pricing to move it. The job is as much experiment design and prioritisation as execution.

The risk is the one every broad role carries: a growth manager with no specialist underneath them tends to run shallow experiments across many areas and change nothing much in any of them.

Hire one when you already have working acquisition and the constraint has moved elsewhere — conversion, retention, or deciding what to build next.

Media buyer vs performance marketer: telling them apart

Titles are unreliable; the questions are not.

  • "Walk me through the last account you fixed." A media buyer describes structure, events and creative. A performance marketer describes the page and the tracking. A growth manager describes the funnel stage they found the leak in.
  • "Cost per lead doubled overnight. What do you check?" The order of their checks reveals the shape of their experience faster than any CV.
  • "What is outside your job?" Candidates who claim everything is inside it usually have not had to own a number.
  • "Show me a report you wrote." Buyers show platform metrics; performance marketers show funnel conversion; growth managers show a business number with a hypothesis attached.

The hiring mistake that costs the most

Hiring broad before the narrow job is done. A growth manager joining a company whose tracking is broken and whose landing page is a homepage will spend six months fixing things a performance marketer would have fixed in six weeks, and will be judged on a number that could not move until they did.

The reverse mistake is cheaper but real: hiring a deep specialist media buyer when the ad account is already competent and the actual constraint is that leads are not being called back.

Work out where the constraint is before you write the job ad. If it is inside the ad account, hire narrow. If it is between the click and the sale, hire broad. If it is after the sale, neither of these three is the right hire.

Or do not hire yet

Below a certain spend, none of these roles pays for itself, and an agency or a contractor covers the same ground for less. Questions people ask before hiring

What is the difference between a media buyer and a performance marketer?

A media buyer owns what happens inside the ad platforms — structure, budgets, events, audiences, creative testing. A performance marketer owns that plus the destination: landing pages, forms, tracking and the lead quality conversation with sales.

What does a growth manager do differently?

A growth manager is accountable for a business number rather than a channel, and works across acquisition, onboarding, retention and pricing to move it. Paid media is one input among several rather than the job itself.

Which role should I hire first?

Usually a performance marketer, because in most companies the expensive problems sit between the ad and the enquiry rather than inside the ad account. Hire a specialist buyer only when spend is large enough that efficiency alone pays the salary.

How do I tell which one a candidate really is?

Ask what they check first when cost per lead doubles. A buyer names account causes, a performance marketer names tracking and the page, a growth manager names the funnel stage. The order reveals their experience faster than the CV.
