What does a creative strategist do? The role in a paid social team

Targeting is now mostly automated, so the creative is the targeting. The creative strategist is the person who decides what that creative should say.

Media Puppies · 23 October 2026

A creative strategist decides what ads a brand should make next, and why. The role sits between the performance data and the people who make the content: it reads which ads are winning and losing, works out the reason, talks to customers, and turns all of that into briefs for designers, editors and creators.

The job has grown because ad platforms now handle most targeting automatically. When Meta's system chooses who sees an ad, the ad itself decides who responds to it. A creative strategist is the person accountable for that decision.

What a creative strategist does day to day

  • Reads the numbers. Hook rate, hold rate, click-through rate and cost per purchase for every live ad, looking for patterns rather than single winners.
  • Finds the insight. Customer reviews, support tickets, sales calls, comments and competitor ads, to understand what buyers doubt and what makes them say yes.
  • Writes briefs. One page per concept: the audience, the angle, three hook options, the claim to prove and the format.
  • Plans tests. Decides which variable each round tests (hook, angle, format or offer) so the results teach something.
  • Feeds back. Turns results into the next round of briefs and a running record of what has been learned.

Creative strategist vs designer vs copywriter

RoleMain questionOutput
Creative strategistWhat should we say, to whom, and why?Briefs and test plans
Designer or editorHow should it look and move?Finished visuals and video
CopywriterWhat are the exact words?Scripts, headlines, ad text

In small teams one person does two or three of these. The strategist part is the one most often missing, which is why many accounts make plenty of ads without learning much from them.

A worked example of the creative strategist's loop

Say a skincare brand runs eight ads. Two have a hook rate well above the rest, and both open with a customer admitting she doubted the product. The other six open with the product itself. The creative strategist reads the comments and finds the same doubt repeated: people think serums like this are overpriced.

The next brief keeps the scepticism angle and tests three versions of it: a customer, a dermatologist-style explainer and a side-by-side price comparison per use. Two weeks later the comparison version has the lowest cost per purchase. That becomes the new control, and the next round tests formats around it. This is the method described in our creative testing framework, and the hook ideas in ad creative hooks are a good starting bank.

Skills a creative strategist needs

  • Comfort reading ad metrics and spotting what a number does and does not prove.
  • Curiosity about customers, and the habit of reading reviews and comments in bulk.
  • Clear writing, because a vague brief produces a vague ad.
  • Taste in short-form video: what holds attention in the first three seconds on Reels and Shorts.
  • Working knowledge of how tools like AI image and script generators fit in; an AI ad creative workflow shows where they help and where they do not.

How a creative strategist is judged

The fair measure is not how many ads get made. It is how often new concepts beat the current best ad, and how quickly the team learns why. Useful signs are a rising share of spend on ads less than a month old, a stable or falling cost per purchase as budgets grow, and a written log of tested angles so the same failed idea is not tried twice. A creative strategist who makes fewer ads but finds a new winner every few weeks is worth more than one who fills the account with variations of the same idea. The creative volume calculator helps set a realistic monthly output for the spend level.

How to become a creative strategist

Most people come into the role from content creation, copywriting, design or media buying. The quickest way to show you can do it is a small portfolio of briefs: pick three brands, study their ads in the Meta Ad Library, write the brief you would send next and explain why. Pair that with any ads you have made yourself.

Questions people ask before hiring

What does a creative strategist do?

Decides what ads to make and why, using performance data and customer insight, then writes the briefs and test plans that designers, editors and creators work from.

Is a creative strategist the same as a creative director?

No. A creative director owns the look and quality of the work. A creative strategist owns the reasoning behind each ad and whether it performs.

What skills does a creative strategist need?

Reading ad metrics, studying customers, writing clear briefs, and a good instinct for what holds attention in short-form video.

How do I start a career as a creative strategist?

Build a portfolio of briefs based on real brands’ ads, explain the reasoning behind each, and apply for roles or internships that put you on live accounts.
