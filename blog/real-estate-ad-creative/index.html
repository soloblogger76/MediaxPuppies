Real estate ad creative: what gets site visits, not just enquiries

Every developer has a CPL target. Almost none has a site-visit target, which is why the creative is optimised for the wrong thing.

Media Puppies · 17 October 2026

Most real estate ad creative in India is built to one brief: lowest cost per lead. The result is predictable. A rendered tower, a starting price, a "limited units" line, and a lead form that collects phone numbers from people who will never pick up. The CPL looks fine on the report and the sales team quietly stops calling the leads.

Creative that produces site visits looks different, because it is written for the person who will visit, not for the one who taps out of curiosity. Here is what we have found works across the real estate accounts we run.

Why real estate ad creative keeps producing cold leads

Three reasons, and they compound. The ad asks for too little, so anyone will fill it in. The ad shows a render, which looks like every other render. And the ad is optimised for the lead event, which tells the platform to find people who fill forms rather than people who buy flats.

Fixing the creative without fixing the optimisation event only gets you part of the way. The two have to move together; we covered the measurement side in real estate lead generation in India.

What buyers respond to

Specificity over aspiration. "3 BHK, 1,650 sq ft carpet, possession December" outperforms "Luxury living redefined" every time we have tested it, because it lets the viewer rule themselves in or out. Ruling out is the point: it is what keeps cold leads off the form.

Site over render. A thirty-second walkthrough of the actual construction, the actual view from the eleventh floor, the actual approach road, converts to visits far better than a render. Buyers have been burned by renders; a shaky phone video of a real slab is a trust signal.

A reason to visit this month. Not a discount. Something that is true and time-bound: the sample flat is ready, the first tower has got its occupancy certificate, the bank tie-up has gone live. The hook is a fact with a date, and it gives the sales team something to say on the call.

Objection-first angles. The buyer's unspoken questions are about delays, builder reputation and the loan. Creative that answers one of them directly ("RERA number on screen, possession timeline, what happens if we miss it") pulls a smaller audience that is far more likely to turn up.

Formats that work, and the one that does not

Vertical video for Reels and Stories, shot on site, with a person talking, is the workhorse. Carousels that walk through the floor plan, the location, and the payment schedule in three cards do well for projects where the price band is the question. Static images only work when they carry one specific fact in large type.

The format that reliably underperforms is the agency-grade brand film with drone shots and a voiceover. It wins awards and produces enquiries from people who liked the music.

Asking more on the form

Add two questions: budget band and intended timeline. Lead volume drops. Site-visit rate rises more than enough to compensate, and the sales team stops dreading the call list. If the developer's instinct is to resist because the CPL will go up, show them cost per site visit instead; it is the number the business actually runs on, and we set it as the target from the first week. Why leads do not answer the phone covers the rest of that conversation.

Testing creative without burning the budget

Run three angles against each other with the same audience and the same offer, judged on cost per qualified lead, not cost per lead. Kill the losers in a week. Keep producing new versions of the winner. The creative testing framework we use is the same for property as for anything else; only the hooks differ.

Questions people ask before hiring

What is the best ad format for real estate in India?

Vertical video shot on the actual site, with a person speaking and one specific fact in the first three seconds. Carousels work for walking through floor plans and payment schedules. Brand films with drone footage produce enquiries from the wrong people.

Should real estate ads show the price?

Show the band, and the configuration it buys. Hiding the price produces leads who cannot afford the project and a sales team that spends its day disqualifying them. Stating it is the single biggest filter you have.

Why do my real estate leads never answer the call?

Usually because the form asked for too little and the ad promised too much. Adding a budget band and a timeline question, and optimising for site visits rather than form fills, changes who the platform finds.

How many creatives should a project run at once?

Three distinct angles per audience, refreshed as soon as one clearly wins. One ad running for months fatigues within weeks in a city market, and the cost per lead climbs quietly while the report still looks fine.
