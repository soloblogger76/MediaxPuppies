UGC ads: what converts, what looks fake, and how to brief them

Indian audiences are unusually good at spotting a paid actor pretending to be a customer. Here is what separates UGC that converts from UGC that gets scrolled.

Media Puppies · 26 August 2026 · Updated 20 October 2026

UGC ads are adverts built from user-generated content: short, phone-shot videos of a customer or a creator talking about a product in their own words, run as paid ads on Instagram, Facebook or YouTube. They work because they do not look like ads. The moment UGC ads look like ads in costume, they perform worse than a straightforward ad would have, because you have spent more to be trusted less.

Indian audiences are particularly quick at this. Here is what gives it away and what to do instead.

What makes UGC ads look fake

Too clean

Perfect lighting, colour-graded footage, a tripod that never moves. Real phone video has a slight wobble, uneven light and background noise. The imperfections are the credential.

The script is visible

Benefits listed in order, no hesitation, a clean sign-off. Nobody recommending something to a friend speaks in bullet points. Real recommendations meander, backtrack, and include one irrelevant detail.

The wrong person

A model who clearly does not use the product in a home that clearly is not theirs. If you sell a ₹400 skincare product, the setting should look like the home of someone who buys a ₹400 skincare product.

Language that does not match

This one is specific to India and it is the most common mistake. If your customer thinks in Hinglish, an ad in polished English creates distance instantly. Let people speak the way they actually speak — code-switching mid-sentence included.

The production value you remove is often worth more than the production value you add.

What makes UGC ads convert

Start with the objection, not the product

The strongest opening is usually the doubt the viewer already has. I thought this was another overpriced serum. Naming the scepticism earns you the next three seconds.

Show the thing working, not the thing existing

Unboxing is a category of content, not a sales argument. Hands using it, the result, the before state acknowledged honestly.

One claim, specific

Not it changed my skin. Something like three weeks, and the patch near my jaw is gone. Specific is believable; superlative is not. This is also what keeps you on the right side of platform policy — vague dramatic claims are what get accounts restricted.

Let it end abruptly

A tidy conclusion signals a script. Real clips end when the person stops talking.

How to brief UGC ads

A good brief fits on one page and leaves the words to the creator. It covers:

  1. The one person this is for, in a sentence: who they are and what they doubt.
  2. Three hook options for the first line, each naming a doubt or a moment. The creator picks one and says it their way.
  3. The single claim the video should prove, and anything that must not be said for compliance.
  4. What to show: the product in use, the result, the setting.
  5. Format: vertical, under 25 seconds, captions on, and only music the platform licenses for ads.

The production reality

  • Shot on a phone, in a real room, by the person speaking.
  • Vertical, captions burned in — most of your audience is watching on mute.
  • Under 25 seconds for cold audiences. You are not telling a story, you are earning a click.
  • Brief, do not script. Give the angle, the objection to answer and the hook. Let them use their own words.
  • Get usage rights, always. A post that performs organically should become a paid ad, not disappear after a day. That is the whole point of running creator work and paid media together.

How many UGC ads you need

More than you think and fewer than the agencies selling packages claim. Eight to twelve genuinely different pieces a month, from different people with different objections, is enough for most accounts to find two winners. Ten variations of one concept is one concept.

How to measure UGC ads

Judge on hold rate first — how many are still watching at three seconds and at fifteen. That predicts performance better than anything else, and it tells you whether the problem is the hook or the offer. Then judge on cost per customer. Views are not a number you can bank.

A worked example

Say a brand tests four UGC ads for two weeks with the same budget on each. Hook rate is 3-second views divided by impressions; "at 15s" is the share still watching at fifteen seconds. The numbers might look like this:

AdHook rateAt 15sCost per sale
A: objection first38%14%Lowest
B: unboxing31%6%2.1× A
C: slow start22%9%1.8× A
D: studio cut19%5%2.6× A

These numbers are made up to show the pattern, not benchmarks. The ad that wins the first three seconds usually wins on cost per purchase too, and the polished version loses. The next step is five new versions of ad A with different people and different first lines, rather than more budget for B, C and D.

Questions people ask

What makes UGC ads look fake?

Studio lighting, a tripod-steady frame, scripted phrasing read off a prompter, and a product that appears unopened. Any one of them tells the viewer this is an ad in costume, which performs worse than an ad that never pretended.

Do I need real customers?

Real customers are better, but a paid creator filming honestly in their own home works. What matters is that the room, the phone and the speech are genuine, not whether money changed hands.

How much should UGC ads cost in India?

Rates vary widely by creator and category, so treat any single number as a quote rather than a benchmark. Budget by volume instead: decide how many concepts you need each month, then find creators who fit that figure.

How many UGC ads do I need?

Enough to keep three to five live variants running while you test. Plan for a steady monthly intake rather than one large batch, because this format fatigues faster than polished creative.

What are UGC ads?

Ads made from user-generated content: short phone-shot videos of customers or creators talking about a product in their own words, run as paid social ads.

Where do UGC ads work best?

On Instagram Reels, Facebook and YouTube Shorts, where they sit naturally among creator videos. They are usually strongest with cold audiences who do not know the brand yet.
