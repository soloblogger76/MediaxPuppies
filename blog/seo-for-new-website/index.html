SEO for a brand new website: the first 90 days

A new site has no authority and no history. That changes what's worth doing, and most advice ignores it.

Media Puppies · 17 September 2026

Most SEO advice is written for sites that already rank. On a new domain the priorities are different, and doing them out of order wastes a quarter.

Weeks 1–2: make it findable

  1. Search Console set up as a domain property, sitemap submitted with the full URL.
  2. Check for stray noindex tags left from staging. This single mistake blocks entire launches.
  3. One canonical version — https, one hostname, redirects for the rest.
  4. Google Business Profile if you have a catchment area. For local businesses this outranks the website in the first months.

Weeks 3–6: build the commercial pages

Not the blog. The pages that would take money: your service pages, your location pages if relevant, your category pages if you sell products.

These are what people search with buying intent, and they're what everything else will eventually link to.

A new site's first ten pages should be pages that could close a sale. The blog can wait six weeks.

Weeks 7–12: content and proof

  • Answer the questions your sales team gets weekly. Those are pre-validated by people trying to give you money.
  • Get listed in the directories that already rank in your category.
  • Ask customers for reviews with sentences in them, not just stars.
  • Link internally from every article to the commercial page it supports.

What to skip for now

  • Link building campaigns. You have nothing worth linking to yet. Build an asset first.
  • Chasing competitive head terms. You won't win them this year.
  • Technical perfectionism. Past the basics, it's not your constraint.

Expectations

Three to six months before meaningful movement, longer in competitive categories. If you need customers this quarter, that's what paid is for — run both.

What not to do in the first 90 days

New sites waste their first quarter on work that only pays off later, or never.

  • Buying links. On a domain with no history this is the fastest route to a problem you then have to spend months undoing.
  • Publishing thirty thin articles. Volume on a new domain reads as exactly what it is. Ten substantial pages beat thirty shallow ones, and the shallow ones actively drag on how the site is assessed.
  • Chasing head terms. "Digital marketing agency" is not winnable in month two. "Performance marketing agency in Noida for clinics" might be.
  • Redesigning because rankings are slow. Three months is not slow; it is normal.
  • Obsessing over an audit score. A green score on a site nobody links to and nobody searches for is worth nothing.

What realistic progress looks like

Expectations are most of what goes wrong in the first quarter, so here is a rough shape for a genuinely new domain in a competitive Indian category.

MonthWhat to expect
1Indexing. Brand-name searches only. No meaningful traffic.
2Long-tail impressions appear in Search Console. Clicks still near zero.
3First non-brand clicks, usually on very specific queries.
4–6Commercial pages start ranking on page two or three. First enquiries from organic.
6–12Compounding, if the content and links kept coming.

If you need customers before month four — and most new businesses do — run paid alongside. SEO is the channel that makes paid cheaper later; it is not the one that pays this quarter's bills.

The unfair advantage a new site has

You get to build the structure correctly the first time. Established sites spend serious money untangling URL structures, duplicate pages and orphaned content accumulated over years. You can simply not create those problems: one canonical hostname, lowercase descriptive URLs, every page reachable in three clicks, internal links written as you publish.

None of it is glamorous and all of it is cheaper now than it will ever be again.

Common questions

Is there a sandbox for new domains? Not an official one, but new sites do rank slowly while Google gathers signals. The effect is real regardless of what it is called.

Should I buy an aged domain instead? Rarely worth it. You inherit the history, including whatever the previous owner did to it.

How many pages before launch? Enough to cover what you sell — usually five to ten. Launch and add; waiting for perfection delays the clock that only starts when you go live.
