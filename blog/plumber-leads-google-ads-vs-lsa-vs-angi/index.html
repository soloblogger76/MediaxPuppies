Plumber leads in 2026: what a Google Ads click, a Local Services Ads lead and an Angi lead cost

US plumbing owners usually buy leads three ways: Google search clicks, Local Services Ads leads and Angi leads. We put the prices from three of our studies side by side, and labelled where each one comes from.

If you run a plumbing company in the US, you can buy work from Google three ways: pay per click on search ads, pay per lead on Google LSA for plumbers (Local Services Ads), or pay for shared leads on Angi. To see how they compare, we pulled the plumbing numbers from three of our own studies and put them side by side.

The short version. A plumbing click costs a median $23.29 on Semrush's US estimates, and $40.98 for emergency searches. The head of one agency said on YouTube that Google LSA for plumbers now costs his clients $66 a lead, down from $70. A software vendor puts an Angi plumbing lead at $40 to $85, and that lead can go to up to five pros. Each number has a different source and a different strength, and we say which is which below.

Plumbing PPC: what a Google click costs

In our Google Ads cost study we priced 28 plumbing keywords in Semrush on 3 November 2026. The median was $23.29 a click and the search-weighted average $26.08. By median, plumbing came second of the 8 trades we priced, behind only water damage ($33.84), and above the $17.62 median for all home services.

Within plumbing, what the searcher wants moves the price a lot. The 4 emergency searches cost a median $40.98, 1.86 times the $22.04 for repair searches, a gap of $18.94 a click. "Emergency plumber" tied with "emergency plumbing" as the most expensive plumbing keyword at $43.65, with 246,000 US searches a month. At that estimate, 100 clicks would cost $4,365. At the other end, people researching prices are cheap to reach: "plumber cost" was $7.88 and "tankless water heater cost" $1.71.

Here is what surprised us. We expected "near me" searches to cost more, because they look like someone ready to call. In two of our three plumbing pairs they cost less. "Plumber" was $29.50 and "plumber near me" $23.72, $5.78 cheaper. "Water heater repair" was $33.11 and its near-me version $22.86, $10.25 cheaper. Drain cleaning went the other way: "drain cleaning near me" cost $9 more than "drain cleaning". Our view: price each plumbing keyword on its own, because the near-me rule of thumb did not hold for most of the plumbing pairs we checked.

Google LSA for plumbers: what one agency says a lead costs

Semrush cannot price Local Services Ads, because you pay for each lead and there is no click price to look up. So the best plumbing figure we have is a claim. In a video uploaded 28 October 2026 on the John Wilson | Owned and Operated channel, Sam Preston of the agency Service Scalers said his plumbing LSA leads cost $70 a year ago and $66 now. It appears to be his own clients' data, and he did not say how many accounts it covers or confirm that every figure is Local Services Ads alone.

That was the second surprise. Plumbing was the only one of the 5 trades he named where the lead got cheaper, down $4 or 5.7%. Roofing went from $74 to $107, HVAC from $51 to $60, electrical from $29 to $43 and appliance repair from $20 to $34. We expected plumbing to rise with the rest. One agency's book of clients is a thin base, so we read it as a hint until more data shows up.

Preston also said that about 90% of his agency's lead disputes used to be rejected by Google, and about 70% are now, so they win roughly 30%. Google's own help page describes automatic credits for some charged leads and a "Rate this lead" option, and describes no dispute process. We could not check how often credits are given.

Two official changes matter for the price. From 1 October 2026, Google charges for a missed call in business hours if the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds; a repeat contact from the same customer within 15 days is free. And from August 2026 Google began moving selected US LSA accounts, plumbing among the first categories, into Google Ads as a Performance Max campaign with pay-per-lead goals. Google says non-US accounts follow in 2027, which matters for readers in Canada, the UK and Australia. Our Local Services Ads guide covers the basics.

On budget, what we have are practitioners' rules of thumb. Building Blocks Digital recommended LSA to plumbing and HVAC firms doing at least $250,000 a year, with at least $1,000 to $2,000 a month, and, in a video about HVAC budgets, said message leads cost about 50% to 60% of a phone lead. PushLeads suggested a weekly budget of 10 times your cost per lead for about 4 weeks before you judge it.

Angi: a cheaper-looking lead you share

Angi publishes no lead prices. In a video uploaded February 2026, the software vendor LeadTruffle gave a plumbing range of $40 to $85 per lead, and said to add 20% to 25% in a major metro. That is a rule of thumb from a company that sells lead-response software that works with Angi, so read it with that in mind.

The firmest number came from Angi itself. On the Q1 2026 earnings call on 6 May 2026, the CEO said pros pay $50 a lead and win one in 7 or 8. That is $350 to $400 of leads for each job won, across all trades. Angi's help centre says a request can go to up to 5 pros, and its Q1 2026 release showed US monthly active pros down 22%, from 134,000 to 105,000.

Thumbtack works in a similar way, and the worst plumbing story in our sample came from there. In a video uploaded 16 October 2026, Imprint Digital read out an unnamed plumber's post: he paid $250 for one water heater lead, was one of five pros sent it, and the homeowner only wanted to pay $500. The speaker's math: five plumbers at $250 each is $1,250 spent on one $500 job. It is secondhand, so we cannot check it. Thumbtack's help centre says a customer can contact up to 5 pros in the first 4 hours, and refunds the lead if the customer replies to no one within 72 hours.

How many plumbing clicks one lead is worth

To compare a click with a lead we divided one by the other. At the $23.29 median click, a $66 LSA lead costs the same as 2.83 clicks, and Angi's $50 lead the same as 2.15 clicks. Turned around, search ads match a $66 LSA lead only if 35.3% of clicks become a lead. If you buy emergency clicks at $40.98, that bar rises to 62.1%.

We expected search ads to come out ahead on a price-per-lead basis. On these numbers they rarely do. To beat a $40 Angi lead, 58.2% of median-priced clicks would have to call or fill in a form. The lead price is not the whole story, though. An LSA call comes to your business directly, while an Angi request can be matched with up to five pros. Talking about lead marketplaces in general, PushLeads used $50 a lead and one close in 8 to get about $400 per booked job, the top of the range implied by the Angi CEO's figures. In our view the number to compare is cost per booked job, and you can only get that from your own call log.

Speed decides a lot of that. PushLeads said leads called back within 5 minutes convert far better than after 30, and after 2 hours the job is probably gone. Momentum 360 put it at an hour for LSA form leads. PushLeads also said contractors who do well on LSA answer live at least 75% of the time, and that booking fewer than 40% of LSA leads means the follow-up needs fixing before the budget does. These are practitioner rules; nobody in our sample measured them.

The plumbing searches Google answers itself

We also checked which plumbing searches showed a Google AI Overview on 3 November 2026. 15 of 28 did, a rate of 53.6%, above the 46.7% for all home services. "Emergency plumber" and "plumber near me" had no AI Overview, while "plumber" and "plumber cost" did. The "plumber cost" answer told searchers a standard service call runs $180 to $450, with a national average of $315. Our view: cost-research searches are cheap clicks that Google often answers on the page, so most plumbing budgets belong on emergency, repair and company searches. This is a one-day desktop snapshot with no city set, and a local search can look different.

What we would do with a plumbing lead budget

  • Run LSA first if you answer the phone. Our view is that a call that comes straight to you, at the price of 2.83 clicks, is the best starting point for most US plumbers who can pick up live.
  • Use search ads for emergency and high-value repair. They cost more per click, but you control the keywords and the hours. In our view, cut cost-research keywords and near-me terms that price above their plain version.
  • Keep Angi small and measured. LeadTruffle itself suggested Angi as 10% to 20% of a marketing mix. Our view: track jobs won per lead and compare your rate with the one in 7 or 8 that Angi's CEO described.
  • Track every call. Without call tracking you cannot compare cost per booked job across the three, and that is the only comparison that counts.

Questions about plumbing PPC, LSA and Angi

How much does Google LSA for plumbers cost per lead?

Google does not publish lead prices. The head of one agency said in a video uploaded 28 October 2026 that his clients' plumbing LSA leads cost $66, down from $70 a year earlier. Prices vary by city and season, so treat it as one agency's figure.

What does a plumbing click cost on Google Ads?

On Semrush's US estimates of 3 November 2026, the median across 28 plumbing keywords was $23.29 a click. Emergency searches had a median of $40.98, and "emergency plumber" was $43.65.

Is Angi cheaper than Local Services Ads for plumbers?

Per lead it can look cheaper: a vendor video put Angi plumbing leads at $40 to $85, and Angi's CEO said $50. But an Angi request can go to up to five pros, and the CEO said pros win one in 7 or 8, so the cost per job won is much higher.

Should a plumber run Google search ads and LSA together?

In our view, yes, if you can answer the phone. LSA calls come straight to your business, and search ads let you choose emergency and repair keywords and hours. Compare them on cost per booked job, which needs call tracking.

Are these numbers from your own clients?

No. Click prices are Semrush keyword-tool estimates. LSA and Angi prices are claims made in YouTube videos and on Angi's earnings call, and we name each source. None are client results.
