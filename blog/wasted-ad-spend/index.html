Ten places wasted ad spend hides

Before you ask for a bigger budget, find out how much of the current one is already buying nothing. It is usually more than anyone expects.

Media Puppies · 11 October 2026

Wasted ad spend is rarely dramatic. It is not one broken campaign; it is ten small things each taking a few percent, which together add up to a meaningful share of the monthly spend. The good news is that they are the same ten things in almost every account, and you can check them yourself in an afternoon.

1. Search terms you would never want

Open the search terms report and read it. Not the keywords — the actual queries that triggered your ads. In most accounts a visible share are jobs, free alternatives, competitor names, DIY intent, or the wrong country entirely. A negative keyword audit is the highest-return hour in paid search and the most commonly skipped.

2. Brand traffic you were going to get anyway

Paying for clicks from people searching your own name is sometimes defensible and often a way of buying traffic that would have arrived free. It also inflates every efficiency metric in the account, which hides problems elsewhere. Separate brand from non-brand so you can see both, and watch for brand terms hiding inside Performance Max, where they are invisible by default.

3. Placements nobody meant to buy

Display expansion, audience network, in-app inventory and automatically included partner placements all spend quietly. Check the placement report. The ones that account for a lot of impressions and no conversions are the ones to exclude.

4. Duplicate and overlapping audiences

When several ad sets target overlapping pools, you bid against yourself and raise your own costs. Consolidation usually improves both efficiency and learning, because each remaining ad set gathers conversions faster.

5. Geography you do not serve

Check the location report, and check the location setting itself — the default on both platforms can include people merely showing interest in a place rather than located in it. For any business with a service area, this is a reliable source of leads that can never be fulfilled.

6. Conversions being counted more than once

A conversion action firing on page load rather than on a thank-you page, a tag installed twice, or several conversion actions all counted as primary will all overstate results. The platform then optimises toward the overstatement. Verify in the platform's own debugging view rather than trusting the tag manager preview, and read what a correct GA4 setup looks like if the numbers disagree with your back end.

7. Ads that have been losing for months

Most accounts contain creative that stopped working and was never turned off, usually because it was once the winner. Sort by spend and look at the bottom of the conversion column. Fatigue shows up as rising frequency and falling response, and the fix is replacement rather than a bid change.

8. Budgets sitting on campaigns that cannot use them

Pacing problems go both ways: campaigns capped below their potential while others spend into diminishing returns. The pacing calculator shows which are which, and reallocating between them costs nothing.

9. Landing pages that undo the media

Slow pages, forms asking for more than the offer justifies, and a mismatch between ad promise and page headline all waste clicks you already paid for. This is the leak with the highest ceiling, because fixing it improves every channel at once. The landing page checklist is the short version.

10. Leads nobody follows up

The largest leak in most lead generation accounts is not in the ad platform at all. Enquiries that arrive outside working hours, sit in an inbox, or are called once and abandoned represent money already spent and then discarded. Response time is the single biggest variable in whether a paid lead becomes a customer.

Nine of these leaks live in the ad account. The tenth is usually bigger than the other nine together.

What to do about the wasted ad spend you find

Fix the leaks before increasing budget, because scaling an account with waste in it scales the waste proportionally. Then recalculate — a few percent recovered across ten places frequently means the existing budget can buy materially more, which is a cheaper way to grow than a budget request. Scaling properly starts here for exactly that reason.

If you would rather have someone else work through it, this list is roughly the first half of what we do in a free account audit, and auditing your own account covers the method in more detail.

Questions people ask before hiring

How much wasted ad spend does a typical account have?

It varies enormously by account, which is why a percentage quoted in a blog post is not worth much. What is consistent is where the waste sits: search terms, placements, geography, duplicated conversions and unfollowed leads.

What is the fastest way to reduce wasted ad spend?

The search terms report in Google Ads. Reading the actual queries that triggered your ads takes twenty minutes and almost always produces a list of negatives worth adding immediately.

Should I pay for ads on my own brand name?

Sometimes — if competitors bid on your name or your organic result is weak. The problem is that brand clicks inflate the efficiency of the whole account, so separate them out and judge non-brand performance on its own.

How do I know if conversions are double counting?

Compare platform-reported conversions with your own back-end numbers for the same period. A platform figure substantially higher than your actual orders or enquiries usually means a tag firing twice or on the wrong event.

Is a slow landing page really a budget leak?

Yes, and one of the larger ones. You pay for the click whether or not the page loads in time, so every abandonment is spend converted into nothing, across every channel at once.
