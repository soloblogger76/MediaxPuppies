Why nobody can guarantee you a ROAS

A guaranteed number before anyone has looked at your data is not confidence. It is a sales technique with a get-out already written in.

Media Puppies · 10 October 2026 · Updated 11 October 2026

A ROAS guarantee is an effective pitch because it answers the question every business owner is actually asking. It is also not a thing anyone can honestly promise, and understanding why protects you from a specific kind of waste.

What would have to be known

To promise a return, an agency would need to know your margins, your conversion rate, your average order value, your repeat rate, what a click costs in your category, how your landing page performs, how competitive your auction is this quarter, and how good your creative will turn out to be.

Before the engagement, they know none of it. A number offered at that point is a guess dressed as a commitment.

How a ROAS guarantee actually gets met

This is the part worth understanding, because guarantees are often technically honoured.

Spend on branded search. People typing your company name convert at excellent rates and would mostly have arrived anyway. A campaign made mostly of branded traffic hits almost any ROAS target.

Lean on remarketing. Same mechanism. People already deep in consideration convert cheaply, and the platform claims full credit.

Use generous attribution. Count view-through conversions, widen the window, and report platform-claimed revenue rather than what the business actually took.

All three produce a beautiful report and a flat bank balance. The blended ROAS calculator shows the gap between what the platforms claim and what the business received — it is usually the whole argument.

The guarantee is rarely broken. It is met using demand you already had, and the business does not grow.

The escape clause

Read what the guarantee is conditional on. There is always a list: a minimum spend, a minimum duration, creative supplied on time, landing page changes implemented, tracking configured a particular way. When the number is missed — and it usually is — one of those conditions was not met, and the guarantee lapses without a refund.

It was never a guarantee. It was a conditional statement with the conditions in smaller type.

What an honest answer sounds like

"Here is your break-even cost per customer based on your margins. Here is what similar accounts in your category have achieved. Here is what I would need to see in the first month to believe we can get there, and here is the point at which I would tell you this is not going to work."

That is less satisfying and considerably more useful.

What to ask instead

  • What is my break-even cost per customer, and how did you calculate it?
  • What will you tell me at week four if it is not working?
  • What would make you recommend I stop spending?
  • Can I leave in thirty days?

The last one matters most. An agency confident in its work does not need a lock-in, and month-to-month terms are a stronger commitment than any guarantee — because they have to earn the next month, every month.

We do not guarantee numbers, and we explain why on the pricing page. What we commit to is the work, a weekly report with decisions in it, and telling you early when it will not work.

The commission version of the same promise deserves the same scrutiny: pay-per-lead arrangements look like perfect alignment and quietly change what gets optimised. When the guarantee is backed by a case study, four questions will tell you how much of it is evidence.

Questions people ask

Can any agency really guarantee a ROAS?

Not honestly, before seeing your margins, your offer and your historical data. The number depends on variables the agency does not control and has not yet seen, so a confident figure on the first call is a sales device rather than a forecast.

What is wrong with a ROAS guarantee if they hit it?

How it gets hit is the problem. The reliable way to produce a high platform ROAS is to concentrate spend on branded search and remarketing — demand you already had. The number looks excellent and the business grows no faster.

What should I ask instead of asking for a guarantee?

Ask what return your margins can support, how they would find out, and what they would tell you to do if the honest answer is that paid media will not work here. The willingness to answer the last one is the signal.

Are performance-based fees the same thing?

Related, and with the same mechanics. Paying on outcomes pushes an agency toward whatever converts fastest and toward claiming credit for sales that were already coming.
