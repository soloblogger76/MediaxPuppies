Ad account disabled? The recovery playbook

Panic makes it worse. Here is the order of operations that actually gets accounts back, and the practices that stop it happening again.

Media Puppies · 25 August 2026 · Updated 11 October 2026

A Meta ad account restricted without warning is a special kind of emergency. Revenue stops the same hour, there is nobody to call, and the appeal form gives you a few hundred characters to fix it. We have run accounts for eight years without a ban, and we have also inherited plenty of restricted ones. Here is what actually helps.

Meta ad account restricted: what not to do in the first hour

Almost everyone makes the same three mistakes immediately:

  1. Creating a new ad account. Meta links accounts by payment method, device, business manager and IP. A new account created to dodge a restriction usually gets caught and escalates the problem from one account to your whole business asset.
  2. Appealing five times. Repeated identical appeals do not increase your odds. They queue behind each other and can close the case.
  3. Changing everything at once. If you delete the ads, edit the page and swap the payment method simultaneously, you have destroyed the evidence you need for the appeal.

What to do instead

1. Find out what was actually flagged

Check Account Quality. There is usually a specific policy named and often a specific ad. Restrictions are rarely mysterious — they are just badly explained. Read the named policy properly, not your memory of it.

2. Screenshot everything before changing anything

The flagged ads, the landing page, the account status. If the appeal goes to a human, you may need to show what was actually running.

3. Fix the underlying cause honestly

Most restrictions in Indian accounts come from a short list:

  • Health and body claims. Before-and-after images, weight loss promises, anything implying a personal attribute.
  • Income claims. Earnings, guaranteed returns, get-rich framing — including in the testimonial you did not write.
  • Landing page mismatch. The ad promises one thing, the page sells another. This is judged, not just the ad.
  • Personal attributes. Copy that addresses the reader as though you know something about them — struggling with hair fall? — is a policy violation even when it is true.
  • Business verification gaps. Unverified business, mismatched details, a page with no real presence.

4. Write one clear appeal

Short, specific, unemotional. Name the policy, state what you changed, confirm it will not recur. Do not argue that the rule is unfair, do not mention revenue, do not write a paragraph about your family business. The reviewer has seconds.

An appeal is not a defence. It is evidence that you understood the rule and fixed the thing.

If it stays restricted

Options narrow, but they exist. Complete business verification if you have not. Try support chat through a different Business Manager admin. If you work with an agency that has a Meta partner channel, use it — that route reaches humans more reliably than the public form.

What does not work: buying an aged account from a reseller. Those come with somebody else's history, and you inherit all of it.

How to not be here again

  • Two ad accounts under one verified Business Manager from the start. Not to evade anything — for continuity if one is reviewed.
  • Every ad reviewed against policy before launch, including creator content. The creator does not know your compliance rules.
  • Landing page matches the ad. Same promise, same claim, same offer.
  • Business verification done early, while nothing is on fire.
  • Never reuse a payment method that was attached to a previously banned account.

The honest framing

Most bans are not bad luck. They are a claim someone made because it converted, on a platform that enforces inconsistently — so it worked for three months before it did not. Writing ads that pass review is a skill, and it is cheaper than losing the account.

Recovery is much simpler when the account is yours rather than your agency’s. Checking who owns it takes five minutes and is best done before you need the answer.

Questions people ask

What should I do first when a Meta ad account is restricted?

Nothing hasty. Do not create a new account, do not add new payment methods, and do not appeal repeatedly from different profiles. Each of those looks like evasion and makes the original restriction harder to reverse.

How do I write an appeal that works?

Short, specific and unemotional. Name the policy, state what was in the ad or on the landing page, say what you have changed, and ask for review. Appeals that argue about fairness are read by a system that does not weigh fairness.

How long does a Meta appeal take?

Anywhere from hours to weeks, and the second appeal is usually slower than the first. Plan for revenue being down for at least a few days rather than assuming same-day reinstatement.

How do I avoid it happening again?

Keep the Business Manager owned by the business with two admins, keep the landing page consistent with the ad, avoid personal health and financial claims, and treat policy review as part of creative sign-off rather than something that happens to you.
