SEO in tier-2 and tier-3 Indian cities: where the competition isn't

Everyone fights for Mumbai. Almost nobody builds properly for Indore — and the enquiry is worth the same.

Media Puppies · 24 September 2026

Search volume for “interior designer Mumbai” is enormous and so is the competition. Search volume for “interior designer Indore” is a fraction of it — and so is the competition, usually by a much larger factor. The enquiry that arrives is worth roughly the same money.

That asymmetry is the whole argument for tier-2 and tier-3 SEO, and most agencies ignore it because the keyword tool shows a small number and small numbers do not win pitches.

The maths that makes it work

Ranking is not about volume, it is about volume divided by difficulty. A metro keyword with 8,000 monthly searches contested by forty well-funded sites produces fewer enquiries for you than a city keyword with 300 searches where the top result is an unmaintained Justdial listing.

Metro keywordTier-2 keyword
Monthly searchesHighLow
Competitors investing in SEOMany, continuouslyFew, often none
Time to page one12–24 months3–6 months
Value per enquirySimilarSimilar
Cost to competeVery highModest

Ten tier-2 cities at 300 searches each is 3,000 searches you can realistically win, against one metro keyword at 8,000 you probably cannot.

The trap: templated city pages

The obvious move is to generate forty city pages from one template with the name swapped. Do not. That is a doorway pattern, it is explicitly against search guidelines, and the risk is not that the weak pages fail — it is that they drag on how your whole domain is judged.

If the page still reads correctly after you find-and-replace the city name, it was never about that city.

What makes a city page genuinely local is information a person outside that city could not write:

  • Named localities. Not “Indore” but Vijay Nagar, Rau, Bhawarkuan. That is how people actually search and speak.
  • Local price reality. What the work costs there, which is frequently 30–40% below metro rates and is exactly what the searcher wants to know.
  • Delivery honesty. Whether you serve it in person or remotely. Pretending to a local office you do not have gets found out on the first call.
  • Local proof. One real project in that city beats five paragraphs of description.

Where to start, and how to choose cities

Do not pick by population. Pick by three things: whether your product is bought there at a price that works, whether you can actually service it, and whether the current page-one results are weak. That last check takes two minutes — search the term and look. If page one is Justdial, IndiaMART and two abandoned WordPress sites, that city is open.

Start with three cities, not thirty. Build them properly, measure enquiries for a quarter, and only then extend. A portfolio of three good city pages that produce leads is a template you can trust; forty thin ones is a cleanup job.

What changes in the content itself

Buyers in smaller cities behave differently in ways that affect the page:

  • Phone and WhatsApp beat forms by a wider margin than in metros. Make the number impossible to miss.
  • Price sensitivity is higher and stated more openly. A visible range converts better than “request a quote”.
  • Local trust signals carry more weight — a recognisable landmark, a local client name, a GST number.
  • Mobile is close to everything. Test the page on a mid-range Android on mobile data, not on your laptop.

Google Business Profile does the heavy lifting

In smaller cities the map pack frequently outranks every website on the page. If you have any physical presence — even a registered address — a properly completed Business Profile with real photographs and a steady trickle of reviews will out-perform months of on-page work. Do that first; it is free and it moves fastest.

Common questions

Is the search volume even worth it? Judge it in enquiries, not searches. Thirty searches a month at 20% click-through and 5% conversion is not nothing when one customer is worth a lakh.

Should I build a page for every city I can ship to? No — only where you can genuinely service and say something specific. Everything else belongs on one honest “where we work” page.

Do I need a local phone number? It helps but it is not decisive. Being reachable quickly matters more than the STD code.
