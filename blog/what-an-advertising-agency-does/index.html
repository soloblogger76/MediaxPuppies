What does an advertising agency do? The four types, how they charge and which you need

The word covers four very different businesses. Hire the wrong type and you get excellent work that does not solve your problem.

Media Puppies · 17 October 2026 · Updated 27 October 2026

An advertising agency is a business that plans, creates and places advertising for other companies. Depending on the type, it may come up with the creative idea, make the ads, decide where they should run, buy the media space, run digital campaigns, or measure the results. The term covers at least four quite different businesses today, so the useful question is less "what does an advertising agency do?" and more "which kind of advertising agency does what my business needs?"

Knowing the difference before you start looking avoids the most common agency mistake: hiring a type that is excellent at something you did not ask for. This guide explains what each type of advertising agency does, what a media agency is, how agencies work day to day, how they charge, and how to choose.

What does an advertising agency do?

At its simplest, an advertising agency helps a business reach people who might buy, with a message that makes them more likely to. That work splits into four jobs:

  • Strategy: who the audience is, what the message should be and what the campaign should achieve.
  • Creative: the idea, the words, the design, the video.
  • Media: where and when the ads run, and buying that space at a good price.
  • Measurement: whether the ads worked, and what to change.

Large agencies do all four. Most agencies specialise in one or two, which is why the label alone tells you little.

The four types of advertising agency

The creative agency makes the ads: the idea, the film, the campaign line, the visual identity. It is judged on whether the work is distinctive and remembered. It charges by project or retainer, and usually does not buy the media or measure sales. Creative agency versus performance agency goes deeper on this type.

The media agency plans where the ads run and buys the space, across TV, radio, outdoor, online video, social and search. It is judged on reach, frequency and cost per thousand impressions, and usually charges a percentage of the media it buys or a planning fee.

The performance agency runs digital campaigns to a business result such as cost per lead, cost per customer or return on ad spend. Because it cannot hit the number otherwise, it wants control of creative testing, landing pages and tracking. It is judged on customers, and usually charges a monthly fee. What a media buyer does describes the day-to-day work.

The full-service agency offers all three, often with PR, social media and web development, under one contract. It is convenient and usually expensive, and the quality depends on which internal team gets your account. Full-service agency versus specialists covers when the bundle is worth it.

What is a media agency?

A media agency is the part of advertising most people never see. It decides which channels, publications, programmes and placements will reach the target audience most efficiently, negotiates prices, books the space and checks that the ads ran as planned. Large media agencies buy across many clients, which gives them negotiating power on TV and outdoor.

For digital channels the picture has changed. Google, Meta and LinkedIn sell through automated auctions, so the skill is less about negotiating rates and more about targeting, bidding, creative testing and measurement. That is why many businesses that advertise mainly online hire a performance agency instead of a traditional media agency.

How an advertising agency works, step by step

  1. Brief. You explain the goal, the audience, the budget and the deadline. A good brief is the best predictor of good work. How to brief an agency has a template.
  2. Strategy. The agency proposes the message, the channels and a plan for how the budget will be spent.
  3. Creative. Ads are written, designed or filmed, then approved by you.
  4. Launch and buying. Media is booked or campaigns are set live on the ad platforms.
  5. Measurement. Results are reported against the goal, and the plan is adjusted.

For brand campaigns, this cycle may run once or twice a year. For performance campaigns it runs continuously: ads are tested every few weeks and budgets move daily.

Brand advertising versus performance advertising

Most confusion about what an advertising agency does comes from mixing up two kinds of advertising with different goals and different ways of measuring success.

Brand advertising builds memory and preference over time. It reaches large audiences, often people who are not buying today, so that when they do buy, your name comes to mind first. It is measured in reach, recall, brand searches and, over months, sales growth. TV, outdoor, online video and sponsorships are typical channels.

Performance advertising captures people who are ready to act now: searching for a service, comparing options, or responding to an offer. It is measured in leads, sales, cost per customer and return on ad spend, usually within days or weeks. Search ads, social ads with a clear offer, and shopping ads are typical channels.

The two work together. Brand advertising makes performance advertising cheaper over time, because people who already know you click and convert more readily. But a small business with limited budget usually needs performance advertising first, because it pays back quickly enough to fund everything else.

Matching the type of agency to your situation

If your problem is that nobody knows who you are, and you have a budget to change that over years, you want a creative agency and a media agency, or a full-service agency strong in both.

If your problem is that you need customers at a cost you can afford this quarter, you want a performance agency, and you should expect it to want control of creative testing, landing pages and tracking.

If your ads look amateurish but the account is working, you want a creative partner alongside your current buyer, not a replacement.

Most small and mid-sized businesses have the second problem and hire for the first, because brand work is more visible and easier to feel good about. The result is a beautiful campaign and no change in the number of enquiries.

Advertising agency examples by business type

  • A local service business, such as a dentist or an HVAC company, usually needs a performance agency running search ads and local listings, with call tracking.
  • An ecommerce brand needs a performance agency strong in Shopping ads, Meta ads and product feeds, plus a creative partner producing new ads every month.
  • A B2B software company needs a performance agency that understands LinkedIn, long sales cycles and CRM integration.
  • A national consumer brand launching a new product needs creative and media agencies for reach, and a performance agency to capture the demand the campaign creates.

What a good advertising agency report shows

An agency's report should answer three questions in plain language: did the money work, what changed, and what happens next. For performance work, that means spend, leads or sales, cost per result against target, and the trend over the last few months. For brand work, it means reach and frequency against plan, plus any brand-lift or search-volume evidence. Every report should end with decisions, not just charts. If you finish reading a report and cannot say what the agency will do differently next month, ask. How to read an ads report shows what to look for.

How long advertising takes to show results

Performance campaigns usually produce leads or sales within days, but they need 60 to 90 days of testing and refinement before the cost per result settles. Brand campaigns take months to show up in sales, and their effect builds with repetition. Be wary of any agency that promises fast brand results or guaranteed performance numbers before it has seen your data. A realistic agency will tell you what the first month is for, usually fixing tracking and learning which audiences respond, and when to judge the work.

Working with more than one advertising agency

Larger businesses often use several agencies at once: a creative agency for brand campaigns, a media agency for TV and outdoor, and a performance agency for search and social. This works when one person in the business owns the overall plan and the agencies share a single brief and a single set of results. It fails when each agency optimises its own channel and nobody owns the combined number. Full-service agency versus specialists covers how to run that setup.

Advertising agency versus in-house team

Some businesses build their own advertising team instead. In-house teams know the business deeply and move fast on small changes. Agencies bring experience across many accounts, specialist tools and cover when people are away. Many companies combine the two: an in-house marketing lead owns strategy and the brand, and agencies handle specialist execution such as paid media or video production. What to ask an advertising agency before hiring

  • Which of the four types are you, really, and what share of your work is each?
  • Who will work on our account every week?
  • How will you measure success, and what will you report?
  • Will the ad accounts and creative files belong to us?
  • What are the contract length and notice period?
  • Can we speak to a current client with a similar goal?

More questions, with what good answers sound like, are in questions to ask a performance marketing agency.

Red flags when choosing an advertising agency

  • Reports full of impressions and reach when your goal is enquiries or sales.
  • Ad accounts opened in the agency's name instead of yours.
  • Long lock-in contracts before any results have been seen.
  • Guaranteed results, such as a promised return on ad spend, before the agency has seen your data.
  • A senior team at the pitch and a junior team on the account.

Advertising terms an agency will use

  • CPM: cost per thousand impressions, the standard price unit for brand and display advertising.
  • CPC: cost per click, the main price unit for search ads.
  • CPA or CPL: cost per acquisition or per lead, what each customer or enquiry cost.
  • ROAS: return on ad spend, revenue divided by ad spend.
  • Reach and frequency: how many people saw an ad, and how many times on average.
  • Impression share: the share of eligible searches where your ad appeared.

How to switch advertising agencies without losing ground

Before you give notice, make sure every ad account, analytics property, pixel and creative file is in your company's name, with the agency as a user. Ask the outgoing agency for a written handover: active campaigns, tests in progress, audiences, conversion settings and anything scheduled. Give the new agency a month of overlap if you can, and expect results to wobble for a few weeks while it learns the account. Leaving an agency cleanly has the full checklist.

Advertising agencies in the US, UK and Australia

The four types exist in every market. In the UK, ad copy must follow the CAP Code enforced by the Advertising Standards Authority. In Australia, the AANA codes and Australian Consumer Law apply. In the US, the Federal Trade Commission rules on truthful advertising and endorsements apply. A good agency knows the rules in your market and builds compliance into the work rather than fixing it after a complaint.

Questions people ask before hiring

What is a media agency?

A media agency plans where advertising should run and buys the space, across TV, radio, outdoor, online video, social and search. It is judged on reach and efficiency, and usually charges a percentage of the media it buys.

What does an advertising agency do for a small business?

For most small businesses the useful version is a performance agency: it runs search and social ads, writes the ads, sets up tracking and reports cost per lead or customer. Brand campaigns usually come later.

What is the difference between an ad agency and a digital marketing agency?

An ad agency traditionally focuses on making and placing ads. A digital marketing agency also covers SEO, email, content and websites. In practice the labels overlap, so ask what the agency actually does every week.

What is the difference between an advertising agency and a marketing agency?

In practice the terms overlap. "Advertising agency" historically meant creative and media buying; "marketing agency" is broader and can include SEO, content, email and strategy. The useful distinction is by what the agency is judged on: ideas, impressions or customers.

What does a full-service advertising agency do?

Creative, media buying, digital campaigns and often PR and social under one contract. The convenience is real; the quality depends on which internal team is assigned to you, so ask who specifically will run each part.

How do advertising agencies make money?

Project fees for creative work, a percentage of spend or a planning fee for media buying, and monthly retainers, sometimes with a performance bonus, for digital performance work. Each model carries a different incentive.

Which type of agency does a small business need?

Usually a performance agency, because the problem is customers at an affordable cost rather than awareness. Brand-led creative work makes sense once the customer acquisition engine is proven and there is budget to build on it.
