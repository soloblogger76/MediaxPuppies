HVAC contractor PPC: what a Google click costs

In our Google Ads cost study we priced 28 HVAC keywords in Semrush on 3 November 2026. The median was $19.60 a click and the search-weighted average $22.64. That put HVAC 5th of the 8 trades we priced, below plumbing ($23.29) and just above the $17.62 median for all home services.

What the searcher wants moves the price more than the trade does. The 4 emergency searches had a median of $35.71, and "emergency ac repair" was the most expensive HVAC keyword at $41.45. Repair searches had a median of $27.85, 1.95 times the $14.30 for install and replacement searches. "Ac repair" was $27.85 with 201,000 US searches a month, and "furnace repair" $20.76, which puts summer repair $7.09 a click above winter repair. At the bottom, "heat pump cost" was $2.94.

This was our first surprise. We expected install and replacement searches to cost the most, because a new system is the biggest ticket in HVAC. They were among the cheapest. A replacement job can be worth many repair calls, so on these estimates replacement keywords look underpriced compared with repair. Our view: give install and replacement searches their own campaign and budget, and judge them on booked jobs. If they share a campaign with repair, the dearer repair clicks will take most of the spend.

Near-me searches were mixed. "Ac repair near me" cost $1.82 more than "ac repair", "furnace repair near me" $1.07 less than "furnace repair", and "hvac company near me" $7.37 more than "hvac company".

HVAC Local Services Ads: what agencies say a lead costs

Google does not publish LSA prices, and keyword tools cannot estimate them. So every HVAC LSA price we have is a claim from someone who sells LSA management or software. Here they are, with sources.

  • $51 to $60 in a year. In a video uploaded 28 October 2026 on the John Wilson | Owned and Operated channel, Sam Preston of the agency Service Scalers said HVAC LSA leads went from $51 to $60, up 17.6%. HVAC was one of 4 of the 5 trades he named that rose. Plumbing went the other way, to $66. He put the average across all LSA categories at $53.
  • $45 to $55 in peak season, $70 to $80 when it is slow. Building Blocks Digital said this in a video on LSA budgets for HVAC and plumbing (May 2026). The same video said message leads cost about 50% to 60% of a phone lead, and showed one HVAC and plumbing client with a $4,000 weekly budget.
  • $40 to $150. Netrocket HVAC Marketing gave this range in an April 2026 video, depending on competition and area.
  • $90 in a worked example. PushLeads used $90 a lead for a mid-sized market: $720 a week, 8 leads, 3 booked jobs at $2,200 each, so $240 per booked job.

The second surprise was the season. We expected HVAC leads to cost the most in peak season, when everyone is bidding. Building Blocks Digital described the opposite: the high end of its slow-season range is $25 above the high end of its peak range. Our view is that when demand drops, fewer homeowners call, and the same budget is chasing fewer leads. It is one agency's observation, and nobody in our sample measured it.

Two of Google's own rules change what a lead costs. Since 1 October 2026, a missed call in business hours is charged if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. A repeat contact from the same customer within 15 days is free. And from August 2026, Google has been moving US LSA accounts into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. Google says non-US accounts follow in 2027, which matters for readers in Canada, the UK and Australia. Our Local Services Ads guide covers the setup.

How many HVAC clicks one LSA lead is worth

To compare a click with a lead, we divided one by the other. At the $19.60 median click price, a $60 LSA lead costs the same as 3.06 clicks. A $45 peak-season lead costs the same as 2.3 clicks, and an $80 slow-season lead 4.08 clicks.

Turned around, search ads match a $60 LSA lead on cost per lead only if 32.7% of median-priced clicks become leads. For emergency clicks at $35.71, the bar rises to 59.5%. Against the $80 slow-season lead it drops to 24.5%. We expected search ads to come out ahead on price per lead. For emergency work they almost never will. For install searches, where clicks cost $14.30, the picture is different.

Lead price is only half the sum. PushLeads' example shows why: with 3 of 8 leads booked, the cost per booked job was $240. If missed afternoon calls cut that to 1 of 8, the same $720 a week buys a $720 job, and the return falls from 9 to 1 to 3 to 1. For comparison, a Canadian HVAC client of Stephen Baker's agency paid CAD 80 a lead on Angi, got 60 leads and closed 3, which is 5% and CAD 1,600 a job. Our view: compare channels on cost per booked job, and you can only get that from your own call log.

What 196 HVAC contractors are running on Facebook

Search data shows prices. It does not show what HVAC firms say to homeowners. So on 17 November 2026 we pulled active US ads from the Meta Ad Library for 8 HVAC searches. The library returned 477 ads. Once we removed brands, online stores, home warranties, utilities, auto air-conditioning shops, hiring ads and unrelated pages, 196 ads from 165 local HVAC contractors were left. 20% of those contractors also name plumbing, electrical or another trade in their page name.

Offers are common. 55.6% of the ads had some offer: a dollar figure, a discount, financing, a rebate or something free. Tune-ups led, which fits the start of heating season. 26.5% of ads pushed a tune-up or maintenance visit, and 7 advertisers named a price. Those prices ran from $59 to $150, with a median of $69. 11.2% mentioned financing and 6.6% a rebate or tax credit.

The third surprise was emergency wording. Emergency searches are the most expensive HVAC clicks on Google, yet only 16.3% of these Facebook ads mentioned 24/7, same-day or emergency service. Our view is that this is sensible: nobody with a dead furnace scrolls Instagram for help. Facebook is better at selling the planned job (tune-ups, replacements, heat pumps) before it becomes an emergency.

How the ads are built:

  • Where they send people. 63.3% went to a website or landing page. 12.8% used a Call now button, 10.2% an instant form, and the rest stayed on Facebook, Instagram or WhatsApp.
  • Format. 48.5% were single images, 19.9% video and 19.9% dynamic creative.
  • Buttons. Learn more was the most common at 24.0%, then Contact us at 13.3%.
  • How long they run. The median ad had been live 119 days, and 16.8% had run for a year or more. Single images had the longest median, at 142 days. Days live is not performance; an ad can run for months because nobody checked it.
  • How many each. 87.9% of contractors had just one active ad in our sample, and the busiest had 6. On Facebook, most HVAC firms run one ad and leave it alone.

The HVAC searches Google answers itself

We also checked which of the 28 HVAC searches showed a Google AI Overview on 3 November 2026. 12 did, a rate of 42.9%, a little below the 46.7% for all home services. "Ac repair", "ac repair near me", "furnace repair" and "hvac company" had none. "Emergency ac repair", "heat pump cost" and "ac replacement cost" did. Cost-research clicks are already cheap, and Google often answers them on the page, so our view is that they deserve little HVAC PPC budget.

What we would do with an HVAC lead budget

  • Start with HVAC Local Services Ads if you answer the phone live. Our view: at about 3 clicks' worth per lead, a call that comes straight to you is the cheapest place for most US HVAC firms to start. The missed-call rule makes answering more important than ever.
  • Use search ads for install and replacement. Install clicks cost $14.30 at the median, cheap for what the job is worth. Cut cost-research keywords.
  • Use Facebook for planned work. Tune-ups, maintenance plans, heat pumps and replacements are what HVAC firms advertise there, and our view is that this is the right use.
  • Track every call. Without call tracking you cannot compare cost per booked job, and that is the number that decides the budget.

Questions about HVAC LSA and HVAC PPC

How much do HVAC Local Services Ads cost per lead?

Google does not publish LSA prices. The head of one agency said in October 2026 that his clients' HVAC leads cost $60, up from $51 a year earlier. Another agency put peak-season phone leads at $45 to $55 and slow-season leads at $70 to $80. These are claims, not measurements.

What does an HVAC click cost on Google Ads?

On Semrush's US estimates of 3 November 2026, the median across 28 HVAC keywords was $19.60 a click. Emergency searches had a median of $35.71, repair $27.85 and install or replacement $14.30.

Is LSA better than Google search ads for HVAC contractors?

On cost per lead, usually yes for repair and emergency work: a $60 LSA lead costs the same as 3.06 median clicks. Search ads give you more control over install and replacement keywords and hours. In our view, most firms that answer the phone live should run both and compare cost per booked job.

What do HVAC companies advertise on Facebook?

In 196 live US HVAC contractor ads we read on 17 November 2026, 55.6% had an offer, 26.5% pushed a tune-up or maintenance visit, and only 16.3% mentioned 24/7 or emergency service. 63.3% sent people to a website.

Are these numbers from your own clients?

No. Click prices are Semrush estimates, LSA prices are claims made in YouTube videos, and the Facebook data is what we observed in the public Meta Ad Library. None are client results.
