What SEO costs in the UAE, and how to judge an agency

The quote matters far less than what is behind it. Here is how to read one.

Media Puppies · 23 September 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026

SEO costs in Dubai vary by a factor of ten for work described in almost identical language. That is not because the market is irrational — it is because “SEO” covers everything from a monthly rank report to rebuilding a site's information architecture, and the proposals rarely say which.

The three pricing models

ModelSuitsWatch for
Monthly retainerOngoing programmes; most businessesVague deliverables — “4 blogs and 20 backlinks” is a quota, not a strategy
Fixed projectA technical fix, a migration, a one-off auditNo follow-through; the report lands and nothing changes
Performance-basedRarely anyone, honestlyPaying for ranking positions nobody searches for

Retainers dominate for a reason: SEO is maintenance as much as construction. But a retainer with no defined scope is where most of the money in this market is wasted.

What actually drives SEO costs in Dubai

  • Competition in your category. Dubai real estate and legal services are among the most expensive content markets anywhere, because a single transaction justifies enormous investment. A B2B niche with fifty monthly searches costs a fraction.
  • Whether content is included. Strategy without writing is cheap and produces nothing. Confirm who writes, and whether they know your sector.
  • Technical debt. A site that needs its URL structure rebuilt is a different job from one that needs pages added.
  • Languages. Adding Arabic properly is closer to a second programme than a ten per cent uplift.
  • Whether they can implement. An agency that hands you a list of changes costs less than one that makes them. The list is worth nothing if your developer is busy for three months.

The cheapest proposal is usually a report. The expensive part of SEO is doing the work the report describes.

Questions that separate the serious from the rest

  1. “What would you not do for us?” Anyone who says every service is right for you is selling a package, not a diagnosis.
  2. “Show me a client where it did not work, and why.” An honest answer exists for every agency that has been going more than two years.
  3. “Who writes the content and have they worked in this sector?” In Dubai real estate specifically, a writer who does not know what a service charge is will produce pages that rank for nothing.
  4. “How do you handle Trakheesi permit numbers on listing pages?” If they do not know what you mean, they have not done Dubai property SEO.
  5. “What will you report, and will it include enquiries?” Rankings and traffic are inputs. If the report stops there, nobody is accountable for the outcome.
  6. “Who owns the content and the accounts if we leave?” The answer should be you, in writing.

Warning signs

Guaranteed rankings, in any form — nobody controls the algorithm, and the guarantees are always written around keywords with no demand. A fixed monthly quantity of backlinks, which means they are being bought. Reporting that leads with impressions. Reluctance to name the specific pages they will build in the first ninety days. And an agency unwilling to say plainly that results take six to twelve months in a competitive UAE category, because the ones who promise faster are describing paid ads or nothing.

What to expect for the money

Whatever the number, the first ninety days should produce a technical fix list that is actually implemented, a keyword and page plan you can read and disagree with, a Google Business Profile properly set up for each location, and the first pages published. If a quarter passes and you have only received reports, the engagement is not working regardless of the invoice.

Judge the whole thing on one question at the twelve-month mark: has the cost of acquiring a customer fallen? Organic is the only channel where it should, and that is the entire reason to spend on it.

Questions people ask

Why do SEO quotes in Dubai vary so much?

Because the word covers everything from a monthly rank report to rebuilding a site's information architecture, and most proposals never say which. Two quotes ten times apart are usually describing genuinely different amounts of work.

Which pricing model is best?

A monthly retainer suits ongoing content and technical work; a project fee suits a defined migration or rebuild; performance-based pricing usually suits the seller. Pick the one that matches the shape of the work, not the one with the smallest number.

What questions should I ask an agency?

Who does the work and where they sit, what they will publish in the first ninety days, how they build links and from where, what happens to the content if you leave, and which previous client you can speak to directly.

What are the warning signs?

Guaranteed rankings, a fixed number of links a month, no named people on the account, reporting that shows only traffic and never enquiries, and an unwillingness to say which sites the links come from.
