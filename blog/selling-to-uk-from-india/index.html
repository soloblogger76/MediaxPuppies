Selling to the UK from India: the four things buyers check

The UK is the easiest Tier-1 market to serve from India and the easiest one to get quietly wrong, because the failures are all in trust rather than in traffic.

Media Puppies team · 10 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026

Selling to the UK is the most approachable of the Tier-1 moves from India. Four and a half hours of time difference means a shared working day, the language is the same, and the buying culture is not unfamiliar.

Which is exactly why brands underestimate it. The traffic is easy to buy. The conversion is where it goes wrong.

What a UK buyer actually checks

A total price, early. Revealing mandatory charges late is now an enforceable consumer protection matter in the UK, not merely bad manners. More practically, a buyer who finds an unexpected fee at checkout does not complain — they leave and do not return.

Where you are. Not a PO box. A UK buyer dealing with an overseas supplier wants to know that plainly, up front, rather than discovering it from a tracking email. Stating it openly converts better than hiding it, which surprises people.

Returns, written in English a person can follow. Who pays for the return, how long it takes, what happens to the refund.

Reviews that look real. UK buyers are unusually suspicious of review widgets. Fake or incentivised reviews presented as spontaneous are also now an enforcement matter.

None of these are in the ad account. All of them decide whether the traffic you bought does anything.

The rules that stop campaigns

UK advertising sits under the CAP Code, policed by the ASA, and the standard is that you hold evidence for a claim before it runs. Not when challenged. "Up to" figures, best-price claims and testimonials all have specific requirements, and rulings are published with your brand name attached.

If you are promoting anything financial — an investment, a credit product, crypto — it must be issued or approved by an FCA-authorised firm. That is statute, the platforms check it before letting you spend, and no amount of media buying skill routes around it.

Invoicing and VAT

A UK business buying services from a supplier outside the UK normally accounts for the VAT itself under the reverse charge, so an Indian invoice does not carry UK VAT. For consumer sales, the import and duty position depends on what you ship and at what value, and it is worth getting right before a customer discovers a charge at the door.

What works in your favour

The time difference is the best of any Tier-1 market. A 9-to-6 Indian day covers roughly 9:30am to 5:30pm in London, so same-day is normal rather than exceptional. You can hold a daily call, answer questions inside the working day, and fix a live problem while the client is still at a desk.

That matters more than it sounds. Most offshore relationships fail on response latency rather than on quality of work, and the UK simply does not have that problem.

A sensible first test for selling to the UK

One product line, one clearly written landing page with the full price on it, a returns policy a human can read, and a budget sized for UK auction costs rather than Indian ones. Ninety days before judging.

Questions people ask

What do UK buyers check before buying?

A UK address and a reachable phone number, clear pricing including VAT, a returns and delivery policy they can read before buying, and independent reviews. Absence of any one of them is treated as a reason to leave rather than a detail to overlook.

Do I need a UK company?

Not to advertise or to sell, but you may need to register for VAT depending on how and what you sell. The commercial argument for a UK presence is trust rather than law, and a UK address and number does much of that work.

Which advertising rules catch Indian advertisers out?

The CAP Code applies to anyone advertising to UK consumers, prices must include mandatory charges, testimonials must be genuine and typical, and consent rules apply to how you track and email. Platforms enforce much of this before the regulator does.

What is a sensible first test?

One product, one clearly defined audience, a landing page written for British buyers with sterling pricing and UK contact details, and enough budget to read the result. Judge it on cost per sale, not on how it compares with your Indian numbers.
