Law firm PPC by country: what lawyer clicks cost on Google Ads in the US, UK, Canada and Australia

We took the top 50 lawyer searches in each of four countries from Ahrefs and priced them side by side. Australia came out dearest at the median, and the US numbers turned out to depend heavily on which tool you ask.

Key findings

  • On Ahrefs estimates of 17 November 2026, the median buyer-intent lawyer CPC was $4.50 in Australia, $2.75 in the US, $2.75 in Canada and $2.50 in the UK. Australia's median was 163.64% of the US figure.
  • The US had the longest tail: 11 of its 40 priced buyer keywords (27.5%) cost $10 or more, topped by "motorcycle accident lawyer" at $70. In the UK it was 3 of 31 (9.68%).
  • On the 20 US legal keywords both tools priced, Ahrefs was lower than Semrush on all 20. The medians were $3.50 against $40.90, and on 6 of the 11 injury keywords Ahrefs came in under 1% of the Semrush price.
  • On the 10 lawyer keywords found in all four countries, the median UK price was 119.84% of the US price, Australia 175.71% and Canada 85.71%. The US was the dearest market on only 1 of the 10.
  • Injury and compensation searches had a median of $8 in Australia against $4 for other practice areas, 2.0 times as much. In the US, Ahrefs priced 12 of 24 injury keywords under $1.50, which pulled that median down to $1.85.

Law firm PPC is priced very differently across the four markets our readers advertise in. We took the top 50 lawyer searches by volume in Ahrefs for the US, the UK, Canada and Australia, collected on 17 November 2026, and kept the 144 that were priced searches by someone looking for a lawyer. On those Ahrefs estimates, the median click cost $4.50 in Australia, $2.75 in the US, $2.75 in Canada and $2.50 in the UK.

The short answer for anyone budgeting law firm PPC is that Australia is dearest at the middle of the market and the US is dearest at the top. Eleven US keywords were priced at $10 or more, led by motorcycle, truck and rideshare accident searches at $65 to $70. The second answer matters as much: on the 20 US legal keywords both Ahrefs and Semrush priced, Semrush's median was $40.90 and Ahrefs' was $3.50. So treat every figure here as one tool's estimate. If you would rather hand the bidding to someone else, that is what our Google Ads agency team does for firms in all four countries.

Law firm PPC by country: the medians

We expected the US to lead. It did not, at the median. Australia's median was 163.64% of the US figure, Canada matched the US and the UK sat at 90.91% of it.

The median hides the shape. The US had the widest spread of the four: its middle half ran from $0.72 to $12.25, and 32.5% of US keywords were priced under $1. The UK was the tightest at the top, with an upper quartile of $4.50. Australia had the fewest cheap keywords, with only 4 of 37 under $1.

Lincoln Lawyer TV searches crowd every list. They took 13 of the UK's top 50 rows, 10 in Canada, 9 in Australia and 6 in the US. That is why the UK has only 31 priced buyer keywords to work from.

The UK is the odd one out. After its $35 injury term, its dearest searches were conveyancing and employment settlement agreements, and the rest of its top 10 sat between $4 and $6. Canada stood out for criminal work: "dui lawyer" at $14, "criminal lawyer brampton" at $14 and "impaired driving lawyer" at $12, for a criminal median of $9 across 7 keywords. In the other three countries we found 1 to 3 criminal keywords each, too few to compare.

Australia showed the clearest injury premium. Its 15 injury and compensation keywords had a median of $8 against $4 for everything else, and injury, compensation and negligence searches filled all of its top 10. Canada had a smaller gap, $3 against $2.50.

The US and UK went the other way, and we do not think that reflects the market. In the US, Ahrefs put 12 of 24 injury keywords under $1.50 and 9 at $13 or more, with only 3 in between. "Personal injury lawyer" was $0.45 and "car accident lawyer" $0.45, while "truck accident lawyer" was $65. In the UK, the injury median was $0.20. Our view: when one tool prices the biggest injury head terms below a family law search, the head-term estimate is the thing to distrust.

Lawyer Google Ads cost: Ahrefs and Semrush on the same 20 US keywords

That suspicion is why we checked the US rows against the Semrush data from our law firm marketing study. Ahrefs was lower on all 20 keywords both tools priced, and its median figure was 36.52% of Semrush's.

The gap split cleanly by practice area. On the 9 keywords outside injury work, the median Ahrefs price was 39.61% of Semrush's, a steady discount: divorce, family, immigration and employment terms all landed between about a third and a half. On the 11 injury keywords, the median Semrush price was $133.15 and the median Ahrefs price was $1.20, and 6 of them came in under 1% of the Semrush figure. Narrower injury terms such as truck, motorcycle and dog bite searches were closer, at 25.71% to 44.87%.

We cannot tell you which tool is right. Both build their CPC from their own models and neither sees Google's auctions. What we can say is that a firm planning personal injury spend from Ahrefs alone would plan for clicks two orders of magnitude cheaper than a firm planning from Semrush. Our broader CPC by country study found Ahrefs lower than Semrush across nine trades, with the largest single-keyword gaps in legal.

The same keyword in four countries

Ten priced lawyer keywords appeared in the top 50 of all four countries. On that like-for-like set, the median UK price was 119.84% of the US price, Australia 175.71% and Canada 85.71%. Australia was dearer than the US on 8 of the 10, the UK on 6 and Canada on 4.

The US was the dearest of the four on only 1 of the 10, truck accident lawyer, and that keyword was priced at under 25 cents everywhere else. Attorney PPC by keyword shape: near me, city and practice terms

Near-me searches were cheap in the US on Ahrefs, with a median of $0.77 across 4 keywords, three of them injury terms. In the UK and Australia they ran at $5.50 and $5.25, though each country had only 2 near-me keywords in its top 50. Canada had the most, 6, with a median of $2.50.

City searches appeared only in Australia and Canada. Australia's 6 city keywords, all Brisbane, Melbourne or Sydney, had a median of $6. Canada's 5, from Toronto and Brampton, had a median of $3.50, with the two Brampton terms at $14 and $15. Neither the US nor the UK top 50 held a single city search with a price. Our guess is that national practice-area terms simply have far more volume there.

What we would change in a law firm budget

  • Price injury work in Google's own tools first. Our view: with a $133.15 Semrush median against $1.20 from Ahrefs on the same injury searches, neither tool is a safe basis. Keyword Planner, set to your exact city or region, gives Google's own top-of-page range.
  • Use the steadier practice areas to compare countries. Family, divorce, employment and immigration searches priced within a few dollars of each other in all four markets. They make a fairer test of a new country than injury terms.
  • Do not assume the US is the expensive market. On Ahrefs, Australia had the highest median and was dearer than the US on 8 of 10 shared keywords. Firms adding Australia should budget at least as much per click as they do at home.
  • Build exclusions before launch. Non-buyer searches such as the TV show, salaries and brand names made up 7 to 18 of each country's top 50 rows. In our view, a negative keyword list for these belongs in the account on day one.
  • Convert to local currency. Ahrefs shows every country in US dollars, so UK, Canadian and Australian plans need converting.

These rows come from the 1,800-keyword pull behind our Google Ads CPC by country study. For the seed "lawyer" we kept the top 50 keywords by monthly search volume from the Ahrefs US, UK, Australian and Canadian databases, 200 rows in all, and collected no new data. 23 rows had no CPC and are left out of every median.

We flagged searches that are not someone looking for a lawyer: the Lincoln Lawyer TV series, salary searches, named brands and a trade magazine. Checking the list by eye, we also overrode three rows the shared rule had kept: "lawyer x" (an Australian TV series), a spam query naming a website, and "justia ask a lawyer" (a directory brand). Only the first had a price. That leaves 144 priced buyer searches: 40 in the US, 31 in the UK, 37 in Australia and 36 in Canada.

Each keyword was tagged with a practice area and a keyword type (near me, named city, generic, practice area) by written rules in compile.py. Many practice-area cells hold only 1 to 3 keywords per country, so we treat them as examples. Lawyer top 50s contain no emergency or 24-hour searches, so the emergency comparison we run for trades does not apply here.

For the tool comparison we matched Ahrefs US keywords to the Semrush US CPCs we collected on 3 November 2026 for our law firm marketing study. 20 keywords had a price in both. One Semrush row priced at $0 (bankruptcy lawyer) was left out.

Limits. Both tools publish modelled estimates. Neither is Google Keyword Planner and neither is an auction price, so we cannot say which is closer to what a firm pays. The pulls were 14 days apart. The top 50 is a different list in each country, which is why the like-for-like comparison uses only the 10 keywords found in all four.

Questions about lawyer Google Ads costs by country

How much does a lawyer click cost on Google Ads?

On Ahrefs estimates of 17 November 2026, the median buyer-intent lawyer CPC was $4.50 in Australia, $2.75 in the US, $2.75 in Canada and $2.50 in the UK. The US had the longest tail, with 11 of 40 keywords at $10 or more and a top price of $70. Semrush prices many of the same US keywords far higher.

Which country is most expensive for law firm PPC?

Australia had the highest median of the four on Ahrefs data, and on the 10 lawyer keywords found in all four countries it was dearer than the US on 8. The US had the most very expensive keywords, mostly in accident and injury work.

Why are personal injury keywords so cheap in Ahrefs?

We do not know how Ahrefs models these terms. On the same US injury keywords, Semrush's median was $133.15 and Ahrefs' was $1.20, and 6 of 11 came in under 1% of the Semrush price. We treat the Ahrefs head-term figures for injury work as unreliable and check Keyword Planner instead.

Are UK lawyer clicks cheaper than US ones?

At the median, slightly: $2.50 against $2.75 on Ahrefs. On the 10 shared keywords, though, the UK median was 119.84% of the US price, because family, employment and near-me searches cost more in the UK. The UK had the fewest keywords at $10 or more.

Are these prices in local currency?

No. Ahrefs shows CPC in US dollars for all four countries, so every figure on this page is in USD.
