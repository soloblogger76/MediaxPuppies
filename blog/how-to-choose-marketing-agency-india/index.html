How to choose a marketing agency in India (and the 6 red flags)

Most agency relationships fail in the first call, not the third month. Here are the questions that separate the ones worth hiring.

Media Puppies · 11 July 2026 · Updated 11 October 2026

Most founders work out how to choose a marketing agency while they are already sitting in the pitch, which is the worst possible moment to start. The deck looks the same across every agency in the country, so it tells you nothing.

Here's what actually separates them.

Six red flags

1. A guaranteed number before seeing your account

Anyone promising a specific ROAS or lead cost before looking at your margins, your offer and your historical data is guessing — and building in the excuse they'll use later.

2. A six or twelve month lock-in

Lock-ins protect the agency from the consequences of poor performance. If their work is good, you'll stay anyway. Ours are month-to-month for exactly this reason.

3. Reporting that leads with impressions and reach

Ask to see a real client report with the names removed. If the first page is impressions, reach and engagement rate, they are managing to metrics that don't pay salaries.

4. No opinion about your offer or your landing page

An agency that says "just send traffic to your website" will happily spend your budget on a page that converts at half what it should. The good ones argue with you about the offer before touching the ad account.

5. You never meet the person doing the work

The pitch is by the founder; the account goes to whoever joined last month. Ask directly: who touches my account daily, and can I meet them now?

6. They've never turned anyone away

Ask when they last told a prospect not to run ads. An agency that takes everyone has no standard, and you'll be funding the accounts that shouldn't exist.

How to choose a marketing agency: the questions worth asking

  1. "What would make you tell me to stop spending?" A real answer means they have a threshold. No answer means the retainer is the product.
  2. "Show me an account that didn't work and tell me why." Everyone has them. Willingness to discuss one is the strongest signal available.
  3. "What do you need from me for this to work?" If the answer is "nothing, we handle everything," they don't understand that your offer, your speed of response and your sales process decide half the result.
  4. "How do you define a qualified lead for my business?" Watch whether they ask you questions back.
  5. "Who owns the ad account and the data?" It should be you, always. Agencies that keep the account hold you hostage.

The best question is the one that invites bad news: what would make you tell me to stop?

Structure the first three months so you can judge

  • Agree the number that matters before starting — cost per qualified lead, or cost per customer. Not cost per click.
  • Expect month one to be setup and learning, not results. Anyone promising results in week two is spending your money to look busy.
  • Ask for a written plan for months one to three with what ships when. Then hold them to it.
  • Keep ownership of the ad accounts, the pixel, the domain and the creative files.

The thing nobody tells you

Agencies fail more often because of a mismatch in stage than in skill. A business doing ₹2 lakh a month in revenue with no repeat purchase does not have an ads problem — it has an offer or margin problem, and no amount of media buying fixes that.

A good agency will say so on the first call. That conversation costs you nothing and saves you a quarter.

Questions people ask

How do I compare quotes from different agencies?

Convert each one into the same shape first: what you pay per month, exactly what is in scope, and what happens if you stop. Agencies quote in retainers, percentages of spend and per-lead deals, and those three are not comparable until you restate them.

Should I hire a local agency or does it not matter?

For most paid media it does not matter, because the work happens inside platforms that are the same everywhere. It matters when the work needs on-ground knowledge — local SEO, regional language creative, or a category where the buyer behaves differently city to city.

What should I be suspicious of in an agency pitch?

A guaranteed return before anyone has seen your margins, a case study with a percentage and no starting number, a refusal to name who will run the account, and any contract that keeps the ad accounts on their side.

How long should I commit to for the first engagement?

Three months is the shortest period in which setup, learning and a first honest read can all happen. That is different from a twelve-month lock-in, which protects the agency rather than the work.

Is a cheaper agency worse?

Not necessarily, but a low fee usually buys fewer hours or a more junior buyer, and both become visible within a quarter. Ask what the fee assumes in terms of time and seniority rather than comparing headline numbers.
