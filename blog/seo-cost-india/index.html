What SEO actually costs in India, and what drives the number

Two quotes for "monthly SEO" can be an order of magnitude apart. The word covers everything from a rank report to rebuilding a site, and proposals rarely say which.

Media Puppies · 15 October 2026

SEO cost in India spans a range so wide that the number on its own tells you almost nothing. The same two words cover a monthly ranking report emailed from a template and a programme that rebuilds a site's structure, publishes properly researched content and earns links from real publications. Both are sold as SEO.

So rather than a price list, here is what actually drives the number, and how to tell which kind of work a proposal is describing.

What drives SEO cost in India

How much content gets produced, and by whom. This is usually the largest line. Content written by someone who understands the category, edited, and built around genuine search demand costs materially more per piece than content assembled from the top ten results. The cheap version is the reason most content programmes produce traffic and no enquiries.

Whether technical work is included. Fixing crawl and index problems, site speed on Indian mobile connections, internal linking and schema is project work. On an older site it can take months. Some proposals include it, some quietly assume the site is fine.

How links are earned. Digital PR, original data, and relationships with publications take time and show up in the fee. Link packages do not, because they are bought from a vendor, and that difference explains a large share of the price gap between two otherwise similar proposals.

Competition and language. Ranking a clinic in a tier-2 city is a different job from ranking a fintech nationally. Adding a second language is close to adding a second site.

Who is doing it. A named strategist who knows your category costs more than an execution team following a checklist, for the same reason it does in any professional service.

The three pricing models, and who each suits

Monthly retainer. The default, and the right shape for ongoing content and technical work where the job is never finished. The risk is paying for months in which little happened, which is why the deliverables should be specific.

Project fee. Right for a defined piece of work: a migration, a technical overhaul, a content build for a new section. Clear scope, clear end, easy to evaluate.

Performance pricing. Pay on rankings or traffic. Attractive and usually a poor fit, because the agency's incentive becomes ranking for whatever is easiest rather than whatever produces customers, and because attribution arguments start the moment it works.

What the money should buy each month

Whatever the model, a month of real work should leave behind artefacts you can look at: pages published or substantially rewritten, technical fixes shipped, links earned with the URLs listed, and a report that explains what moved and what is next. If the month's output is a rankings screenshot, the work did not happen.

It is also worth knowing what SEO cannot do on its own. If enquiries are needed this quarter, paid is the faster route, and the two work better together than separately — the search terms report from your ad account is the best content plan most businesses will ever get. Warning signs in an SEO proposal

  • Guaranteed rankings. Nobody controls the result page. A guarantee means either easy terms nobody searches, or a refund clause that is cheaper than the work.
  • A fixed number of links per month. Links that arrive on a schedule were bought.
  • No named people. Ask who writes, who does technical work, and where they sit.
  • Reporting that stops at traffic. Traffic is an input. Enquiries are the output, and the report should connect the two.
  • No audit before the quote. A number produced without looking at the site is a template.

Questions that separate serious proposals

Ask what they would do in the first ninety days, in order. Ask which pages they expect to rank first and why those. Ask how links will be earned and from what kind of site. Ask what happens to the content if you leave. And ask for one client you can speak to directly.

The answers sort proposals faster than the prices do. If you want a view of your own site before you commit to anyone, our SEO work starts with that audit rather than with a retainer.

Questions people ask before hiring

How much does SEO cost in India?

The range is too wide for a single figure to be useful, because the same term covers a monthly rank report and a full content and technical programme. Judge a quote by what it produces each month — pages published, fixes shipped, links earned — rather than by the number alone.

Is a retainer or a project fee better?

A project fee suits defined work such as a migration or a technical overhaul. A retainer suits ongoing content and maintenance, where the work is never finished. Pick the shape that matches the job rather than the smaller number.

Why do some agencies charge ten times more than others?

Usually content quality, whether technical work is included, and how links are earned. Written-to-order content from someone who knows the category and links earned through real outreach both cost real money; template content and bought links do not.

How long before SEO produces enquiries?

Months rather than weeks for a new site, and longer in competitive categories. If the business needs enquiries this quarter, fund paid media for that and treat SEO as the channel that reduces your cost per enquiry later.
