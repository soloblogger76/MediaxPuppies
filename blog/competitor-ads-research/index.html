How to read your competitors' ads (legally and usefully)

Every competitor's active ads are public. Most businesses never look, and the ones that do usually look at the wrong thing.

Media Puppies · 9 September 2026

The Meta Ad Library shows every ad any page is currently running, free, no login. Google has an equivalent transparency centre. This is the cheapest competitive research available and almost nobody uses it properly.

What to look for

  1. How long an ad has been running. This is the signal. An ad live for three months is working — nobody pays to run a loser that long. Sort by start date, not by how clever the ad looks.
  2. How many variations of one concept. Heavy variation on a single idea means they found something and are scaling it.
  3. The offer, not the design. What are they promising? Free consultation, fixed-price quote, 24-hour turnaround? Offers are copyable; their designer's taste is not worth copying.
  4. Where the ad lands. Click through. The landing page tells you more than the ad — their form length, their proof, their price transparency.

What to ignore

  • Ad count. Hundreds of active ads usually means poor structure, not a big budget.
  • Production value. Expensive-looking rarely correlates with performing.
  • Brands in other markets. What works in the US for a US buyer is not evidence about Indian buyers.

The only real signal in a competitor's ad library is time. Everything else is decoration.

What to do with it

Don't copy the ad. Copy the angle, then do it better with your own proof. If three competitors all lead with price and none address delivery time, that gap is your opening.

Do this once a quarter, not weekly. It's research, not a habit.

A repeatable monthly routine

Ad libraries are useful in proportion to how regularly you look. A single visit tells you what is running today; monthly snapshots tell you what survived.

  1. List five to eight genuine competitors. Businesses fighting for the same buyer in the same city, not the biggest names in your industry.
  2. Screenshot every ad running for each, with its start date. Keep them in a dated folder.
  3. Next month, compare. Anything still live is your shortlist. Anything gone was a test that failed, and knowing what failed is free information.
  4. Write down the offers, not the visuals. After three months you have a table of what your market is actually promising buyers, and where the gap is.

Reading landing pages, not just ads

The ad is the cheap half. Clicking through is where the real intelligence is, and almost nobody bothers.

  • How many form fields? A short form means they are optimising for volume and qualifying by phone. A long one means they are filtering hard.
  • Do they show price? If every competitor hides it, publishing a range is an immediate differentiator.
  • What proof do they lead with? Years, client count, certifications, named case studies — this tells you what your market finds credible.
  • What do they promise about response time? If everyone says "we'll get back to you", saying "reply within two hours, or tell us and we'll fix it" is a positioning move that costs nothing to make and something real to keep.

The gap you are looking for

The point of all this is not imitation. If you run the same offer with the same creative as four competitors, you are competing purely on budget, and the biggest budget wins that fight.

What you want is the thing nobody in the set is saying. In most Indian service markets we look at, the whole category advertises on speed and discount, and nobody advertises on certainty — fixed price, fixed timeline, written scope. That is a gap, and gaps are cheap to occupy because no one else is bidding on being the trustworthy option.

Where the libraries stop being useful

They show you the ad. They do not show you spend, results, or whether the business is profitable. An ad running six months proves someone keeps paying for it — that is all, and it is usually enough. Resist reconstructing a competitor's strategy from what is visible; you will confidently infer a plan that does not exist.

Common questions

Is this legal? Yes. Both libraries are public transparency tools published by the platforms themselves.

Can competitors see my ads too? Yes. Assume everything you run is public, because it is.

Should I copy an ad that has run for a year? Copy the offer structure and the angle. Copying the execution puts you second in a comparison the buyer makes instantly.
