B2B PPC: how to run Google and LinkedIn ads that build qualified pipeline

A B2B buyer clicks an ad, reads for weeks, involves four colleagues and signs a quarter later. PPC built for that cycle looks very different from PPC built for a shop.

Media Puppies · 27 October 2026

B2B PPC is pay-per-click advertising aimed at business buyers, usually on Google Search, LinkedIn and Microsoft Advertising. The goal is qualified sales pipeline: meetings, opportunities and closed deals from companies that fit the ideal customer profile. Cheap leads are a poor goal in B2B, because most of them never reach a sales conversation.

B2B PPC works when it follows the sales cycle. That means search ads on high-intent problem and solution terms, LinkedIn for reaching named accounts and job titles, offers that suit each stage, and CRM data fed back into the ad platforms so bidding learns which leads became revenue. Without that feedback loop, the ad platforms optimise for form fills, and form fills are the easiest thing for a B2B account to get wrong.

What makes B2B PPC different from B2C

  • Long cycles. Weeks or months pass between the first click and the signed contract. Platform attribution windows often close before the deal does.
  • Several decision makers. A user, a manager, finance and sometimes procurement or IT security. The person who clicks is often not the person who signs.
  • Small audiences. A company selling to mid-size logistics firms in the US may have a few thousand target accounts in total. Broad, cheap reach is mostly waste.
  • High deal values. One closed deal can pay for months of spend, so cost per lead matters much less than cost per qualified opportunity.
  • Lead quality varies widely. Students, job seekers, competitors and very small companies all fill in forms. Sales has to qualify, and that qualification data has to come back to marketing.

Every decision below follows from those five points.

Keyword strategy for B2B PPC

B2B search volume is small and spread across many phrasings, so the keyword list matters more than in most consumer accounts.

  • Solution terms. "Fleet management software", "warehouse automation consultant", "SOC 2 audit firm". These are the core.
  • Problem terms. "Reduce freight costs", "automate invoice approvals". Lower intent but earlier in the cycle; good for content offers.
  • Competitor and alternative terms. "[Competitor] alternative" and "[competitor] pricing". Often among the best converting terms. Do not use competitor trademarks in ad text.
  • Integration and category terms. "[Your category] for Salesforce", "[category] for healthcare". Small volume, strong fit.
  • Negatives. "Free", "jobs", "salary", "course", "certification", "template", "examples", "what is", "definition", "open source" (if you are not), plus student and personal-use terms. B2B accounts leak money to researchers and job seekers faster than almost any other type.

Use phrase and exact match while the account is learning. Broad match with Smart Bidding can work later, once the account has a steady flow of qualified conversions to learn from. Some of the best keyword ideas come from your own search terms data; keyword research from ads shows how to mine it.

LinkedIn targeting and account-based lists

LinkedIn is the only major platform that targets reliably by company and job title, which makes it the natural home of account-based marketing (ABM) in paid media.

  1. Build the account list. Export target companies from the CRM or a data provider and upload them as a company list through Matched Audiences.
  2. Layer roles. Add job functions and seniority to the company list. Avoid stacking too many filters; LinkedIn audiences shrink fast.
  3. Match the offer to the stage. Cold accounts get thought-leadership content, a short video or a useful guide. Engaged accounts get a case study or a webinar. Accounts already in a sales conversation get proof: reviews, security documents, an ROI model.
  4. Choose the form. LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms convert well because they prefill profile data, but the leads can be lower intent. Test them against a landing page and judge both on qualified leads.
  5. Connect the CRM. Sync form leads straight to the CRM and use LinkedIn's Conversions API so offline stages like "meeting booked" flow back.

On Google, Customer Match lists of existing contacts and lookalike-style segments help, but Google cannot target by job title the way LinkedIn can. That is why most B2B PPC programmes use Google to capture demand and LinkedIn to create it within a defined list of accounts.

Measuring qualified pipeline with offline conversion import

If the ad platforms only see form fills, they will find more form fills, many of them unqualified. The fix is to send later funnel stages back to them.

  1. Capture click IDs. Store the Google click ID (gclid), Microsoft click ID and UTM parameters in hidden form fields and save them on the CRM record.
  2. Define stages. Marketing qualified lead, sales qualified lead, opportunity, closed won. Agree the definitions with sales in writing.
  3. Import the stages. Upload or sync each stage back to Google Ads as an offline conversion, with a value if you have one. HubSpot and Salesforce both have native Google Ads integrations that do this.
  4. Change the bidding goal. Once a stage like SQL has enough volume, make it the primary conversion and move the raw form fill to secondary.

Landing pages and offers that suit B2B buyers

  • One page per offer. A demo request page, a pricing page and a guide download page each do one job.
  • Short forms for high intent, longer for qualification. A demo form can ask company size and role so sales can route it; a content download should ask less.
  • Proof above the fold. Customer logos you have permission to use, security and compliance badges you actually hold, and a clear statement of who the product is for.
  • Book the meeting on the page. An embedded calendar after the form removes days of email back-and-forth.
  • Say who it is not for. A line such as "built for teams of 50 or more" filters out leads sales would reject anyway.

Budget split and bidding by funnel stage

B2B PPC budgets go wrong when every campaign is asked to produce demos. A clearer approach is to give each stage of the funnel its own budget and its own measure of success.

StageCampaignsJudge it onBidding
CaptureGoogle and Microsoft Search on solution, competitor and brand termsCost per SQL and opportunityMaximise conversions, then target CPA on SQLs once volume allows
CreateLinkedIn to target account lists with content and videoEngagement from target accounts, account-level pipeline laterManual or cost cap, so a small audience is not overspent
ConvertRemarketing on LinkedIn, Google and Meta to engaged visitors and leadsMeetings booked from engaged accountsSmall budgets, frequency watched closely

Smart Bidding needs volume. A campaign producing three qualified leads a month cannot run target CPA on qualified leads without wild swings. Until volume builds, bid on a slightly earlier stage that still correlates with quality, for example "demo request from a business email domain", and keep importing the later stages so the data is ready. When the account reaches a steady flow of SQLs each month, move bidding to that stage.

Keep the capture budget protected. It harvests demand that already exists, so it is usually the most efficient spend in the account. Create and convert budgets can flex with results and with sales capacity.

Ad copy and creative for B2B PPC

B2B buyers skim ads the same way consumers do. What they look for is different.

  • Say who it is for. "Payroll software for US companies with 50 to 500 staff" filters out the wrong clicks before you pay for them.
  • Lead with the outcome and the proof. A specific benefit, plus a credible signal such as a compliance standard you hold or a well-known integration.
  • Match the stage. A search ad on "[competitor] alternative" should compare directly. A cold LinkedIn ad should teach something, not ask for a demo.
  • Use people. On LinkedIn, posts from a founder or subject expert, promoted as thought leader ads, often read as more credible than a logo and a headline.
  • Refresh on a schedule. Small LinkedIn audiences see the same ad often. Plan new creative every few weeks and watch frequency.

Common B2B PPC mistakes

  • Optimising for form fills. Bidding learns to find cheap, unqualified leads.
  • No negatives for jobs and education. "Software engineer jobs" and "course" searches eat budget.
  • LinkedIn audiences that are too broad or too narrow. Broad job-function targeting without a company list reaches the wrong firms; five stacked filters leave an audience too small to deliver.
  • Demo request as the only offer. Most visitors are not ready. Give earlier-stage buyers something useful and remarket.
  • Judging inside the attribution window. A 30-day window misses deals that close in month three. Look at pipeline by first-touch source in the CRM.
  • Sales never reports back. Without lead status from sales, marketing cannot fix lead quality.

Running B2B PPC in the US, UK and Australia

  • United States. The largest LinkedIn and Google B2B audiences, and the most competitive auctions. Several state privacy laws affect tracking and data sharing; US state privacy and conversions explains what changes.
  • United Kingdom. UK GDPR and PECR mean cookie consent is required before most tracking, so expect lower measured conversions and use Consent Mode. Business contact data still falls under data protection rules; UK consent mode and conversion loss covers it.
  • Australia. Smaller LinkedIn audiences in most industries, so account lists run out faster and creative fatigue arrives sooner. Many Australian B2B buyers also search for US vendors, so local proof and local support hours are a real advantage in ad copy.

Run each country as its own campaign with its own budget and schedule, so a large US audience does not swallow a smaller UK or Australian one.

How to judge a B2B PPC agency

Ask three questions. How will you get pipeline data back into Google and LinkedIn? What will you report each month beyond cost per lead? How do you decide LinkedIn audiences and offers by funnel stage? An agency that answers with dashboards and click-through rates is selling B2C habits to a B2B account.

Questions people ask before hiring

What is B2B PPC?

Pay-per-click advertising aimed at business buyers, mostly on Google Search, LinkedIn and Microsoft Advertising. The goal is qualified sales pipeline, not the largest number of leads.

Is Google or LinkedIn better for B2B PPC?

They do different jobs. Google captures people already searching for a solution; LinkedIn reaches named companies and job titles before they search. Most B2B programmes use both.

How do you measure B2B PPC results?

By cost per sales qualified lead, cost per opportunity and pipeline value, using CRM stages imported back into the ad platforms as offline conversions.

Why are my B2B PPC leads low quality?

Usually because bidding optimises for form fills, negatives are missing for jobs and education searches, or the offer attracts researchers. Import qualified stages and tighten the keyword list.

What is offline conversion import?

Sending later sales stages, such as a qualified lead or closed deal, from your CRM back into Google Ads or LinkedIn, matched by click ID, so bidding learns which clicks became revenue.

How long does B2B PPC take to show results?

Leads can arrive in the first weeks, but pipeline and revenue follow the sales cycle, often one to two quarters. Judge early months on qualified leads and opportunities.

Should B2B ads use LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms?

Test them. They convert well because they prefill profile data, but lead intent can be lower, so compare them with a landing page on qualified leads.
