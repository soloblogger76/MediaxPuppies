What a good first 90 days with an agency actually looks like

If you can't tell whether your agency is working, it's usually because nobody agreed what month one was supposed to produce.

Media Puppies · 12 July 2026 · Updated 11 October 2026

The first 90 days with an agency should end with you able to say whether it is working. The most common complaint we hear about a previous agency is not that results were bad — it is that the client could not tell. Three months in, money had moved, reports had arrived, and nobody could say.

That's an expectations failure, and it's preventable. Here's what the ninety days should look like.

Weeks 1–2: audit and plumbing

Almost no results. That's correct.

  • Full account audit, including what the previous setup got wrong.
  • Conversion tracking rebuilt and verified — pixel, server-side events, the lot. Someone should submit a test form and show you the event arriving.
  • The offer interrogated. Expect uncomfortable questions about price, guarantees and what happens after a lead comes in.
  • Agreement on the one number that defines success.

Red flag: ads live on day two with no tracking check. That's activity theatre.

Weeks 3–6: first tests, first read

  • Four or so genuinely different creative angles running — not four versions of one idea.
  • A dedicated landing page if paid traffic was going to a homepage.
  • Volatile cost per result. This is normal during learning.
  • First honest read at around week four: which angle works, which is dead.

Red flag: weekly reports with no decisions in them. A report should end with what changes next week and why.

Weeks 7–12: scale what proved itself

  • Budget shifts toward winners, gradually — not in one jump.
  • New creative keeps entering so the account doesn't fatigue.
  • Cost per qualified lead starts stabilising enough to plan around.
  • Conversations move from "is this working" to "how much can we spend at this efficiency".

Red flag: still no stable number by week ten, and no clear explanation why.

A weekly report without a decision in it is a status update, not management.

What they should be asking you for

A good agency creates work for you, in a useful way:

  • Access to real customer conversations — call recordings, WhatsApp threads, reviews. That's where ad copy comes from.
  • Which leads actually qualified, fed back weekly. Without it the algorithm optimises toward form-fillers.
  • Honest margin numbers, so the target cost per customer is real.
  • Fast approval on creative. A week of waiting is a week not testing.

What the first 90 days with an agency should leave you able to say

  1. What a customer costs you, and whether that's under your ceiling.
  2. Which creative angle works and why.
  3. What's shipping next month.
  4. What would make them tell you to stop.

If you can't answer those four, the problem is usually communication rather than performance — but you won't be able to tell the difference, which is its own problem.

Questions people ask

What should happen in the first two weeks?

Access, a tracking audit and whatever rebuilding it needs, account structure, and a written note of what is being tested first. Not a cost per lead — anything reported that early is noise.

When can I judge the results?

Around the end of the third month, once the account has gathered enough conversions for bidding to stabilise. Earlier than that you can judge the setup, the communication and the thinking, which are still worth judging.

What should the agency be asking me for?

Sales outcomes. Which leads were qualified, which closed, and what they were worth. An agency that never asks is optimising toward form fills because that is all it can see.

What if nothing seems to be happening in month one?

Ask for the change log and the test plan. Quiet setup work is normal and invisible; quiet nothing looks identical from outside. The change log settles it in a minute.
