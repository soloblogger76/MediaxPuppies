Do Google Ads work for small business? We coded 89 owners' own reports

We read what small business owners write about their own Google Ads in public forums and coded 89 reports. Half said it did not work. The reasons they gave, and what the winners did, tell you more than the headline.

Key findings

  • We coded 89 reports from owners describing their own Google Ads (57 on Hacker News, 32 on the Shopify Community). 16 (18%) said it worked, 19 (21%) were mixed, 45 (51%) said it did not work and 9 (10%) could not tell.
  • These are self-reported and skewed toward problems: people post when something breaks, and most Shopify threads are requests for help. Read the 18% as a floor for this sample, and do not read it as a success rate.
  • Of the 64 reports that did not work or were mixed, the most named cause was junk traffic from Display, partner sites, apps or bots (10, 16%), then the agency or Google rep (8, 12.5%) and too few people searching (8, 12.5%). 13 of the 45 failures named no cause at all.
  • Conversion tracking problems came up in 13 of 89 reports (15%), and in 6 of the 9 where the owner could not say whether ads worked.
  • Short and small tests mostly failed: 5 of the 6 runs under a month did not work, and 7 of the 8 owners spending under $500 a month said it did not work.
  • Performance Max came up in 9 reports, and 8 of them were mixed. Most described a campaign that had been working and then dropped.

Ask a forum whether Google Ads is worth it and you get two loud camps. We wanted a count instead of a vibe. On 21 November 2026 we read what small business owners have written in public about their own Google Ads campaigns and coded 89 reports, one per owner, for outcome, spend, campaign type, what they blamed and what they fixed.

So, do Google Ads work for small business owners, by their own account? In this sample, 18% said yes, 21% said it was mixed, 51% said no, and 10% could not tell. That sounds damning, and it is skewed: people post when something breaks. The more useful part is the pattern. The failures cluster around short tests, tiny budgets, junk traffic and missing tracking, and the wins share a few habits.

This study sits beside our guide to Google Ads for small business, which explains how to set up a first campaign and judge the first 90 days. Read that for the how. This post is the field report: what happened to the owners who tried it. We run Google Ads accounts ourselves, so we have opinions, and we label them. None of our own client data is in here.

Where these reports come from, and why they lean negative

We planned to use Reddit. On the day, Reddit returned a block page and a login redirect, so we stopped and did not work around it. Several owner forums in the UK, Canada and Australia blocked us too. We used two public sources that load without a login: Hacker News, where many founders and small software and service businesses post, and the Shopify Community, where store owners ask for help with Google Ads.

From 2,734 Hacker News comments and 637 Shopify topics, 89 reports passed our rules: an owner describing their own campaign and saying how it went. We dropped agencies, vendors, pure questions, suspensions and set-up questions with no result. Account suspensions get their own study: we coded 162 first-hand reports of suspended accounts, the reasons Google gave and how the appeals ended, in what advertisers report about Google Ads suspensions. 57 reports came from Hacker News and 32 from Shopify.

Two biases matter. First, the Shopify threads are mostly help requests, which means something already went wrong. Second, Hacker News skews to software founders, and many of those comments are from the AdWords years. Of the 89 reports, 50 date from 2020 or later. We expected a negative lean. We did not expect how few winners explained what they did.

"Mixed" mostly means one of two things: the ads made some sales but not a profit, or they worked for a while and then fell away. Shopify owners landed there more often (31%), and that is largely a Performance Max story, which we come to below.

The type of business mattered, with small groups. 3 of 5 service businesses and 2 of 5 local businesses said Google Ads worked. Only 6 of 34 software businesses and 4 of 35 online stores said the same. That fits what we see in accounts: when someone searches "plumber near me" or "IT security audit", the search itself is the demand. A new app or a niche product often has nobody searching for it yet. One online business owner on Hacker News put it plainly: "I went through about $5K (without so much as a single sale!) on adwords."

Junk traffic led. Owners described clicks from apps, foreign sites and bots that never became customers. Someone running ads for a local gym checked a campaign that looked tightly local and wrote that "when I investigated where they were all coming from it was 95% international". Several of these campaigns reached beyond Google Search into Display, app installs and partner sites. Others suspected bots or click fraud on Search itself.

Agencies and Google reps came next. 8 reports blamed someone who was supposed to help, often a Google rep who changed settings or set up an automated campaign that spent without reporting search terms.

The quiet finding is what owners did not blame. Only 1 report named broad match or wasted search terms, and only 3 named the landing page. 13 of the 45 owners who said it did not work named no cause at all. In our experience those are two of the most common reasons a small account loses money. Owners mostly cannot see them, which is why they do not name them. Our negative keywords audit shows how to check the search terms yourself.

Tracking: the owners who could not tell

Conversion tracking problems came up in 13 of 89 reports (15%), across every outcome. In 6 of the 9 reports where the owner could not say whether ads worked, the reason was tracking: sales showing in the store but not in Google Ads, a tag on the cart page counting visits as purchases, or one fake conversion that blew up the reported return.

One brick and mortar shop ran Shopping ads for a month and could not tell how much of its Google sales came from the ads and how much from free listings. That is the most common Shopify thread we read. If you cannot see which clicks became customers, every other decision is a guess, and automated bidding guesses for you. Our post on how to audit your own ad account starts with this check.

The same tracking trouble runs through our Microsoft Ads owner study: every Shopify owner in it from 2024 and 2025 could not match Microsoft Ads conversions to real sales.

Small tests, short tests, and Performance Max

Only 19 of 89 reports (21%) gave a monthly spend, and 32 gave a time run, so these groups are small. The direction is still clear. 7 of the 8 owners who spent under $500 a month said it did not work. 5 of the 6 who ran it for under a month said the same, and none of those 6 said it worked. One Shopify owner summed up a common path: "I have tried Google Ads a number of times with absolutely zero sales."

Longer runs did better but were no guarantee: 8 of the 17 that ran six months or more still did not work. Owners who mentioned Search campaigns reported the lowest failure share, 7 of 23 (30%).

Performance Max came up in 9 reports, all of them from the Shopify Community, and 8 of the 9 were mixed. Most were campaigns that had been working and then dropped: after Google moved Smart Shopping campaigns to Performance Max, after a store migration broke the feed, or after a tag stopped firing. One store owner wrote: "Since Google changed the ads we run our sales have plummeted." Our view is that Performance Max works best on top of clean tracking and a clean feed, and fails silently when either breaks. You need a weekly check, not a set-and-forget campaign. We followed this up with a study of 39 advertisers' own Performance Max reports: 18 said it did not work, and 7 of the 10 mixed campaigns had worked before they dropped.

The checks that separate the "worked" threads from the rest (our view)

The 16 owners who said Google Ads worked were not a uniform group, and only 5 of them named what they fixed. But compared with the 45 who said it did not, a few things stand out: 5 of 16 winners named a fix, against 2 of 45 failures; 8 of 16 mentioned Search or Local Services Ads; and all 6 winners who gave a time had run it for at least a month. One B2C owner wrote that "it took about $3k burned over 60 days to really understand how to build a profitable acquisition model".

From that, and from what we see in accounts, these are the checks we would run before deciding Google Ads does not work for your business:

  1. Do people search for what you sell? Look up your main terms in Keyword Planner before you spend. 8 failures came down to nobody searching. No bid fixes that.
  2. Is tracking counting real sales or leads? Compare Google Ads conversions with your own orders or call log for the same week. Fix this first. Our Google Ads audit checklist covers it.
  3. Is the money going to Search? Check the network split and the placements report. Turn off Display and search partners at the start, and add app and placement exclusions if you run Performance Max.
  4. Have you read the search terms? Download them weekly and add negatives. Owners rarely mentioned this, and it is where we find the most waste.
  5. Is the budget big enough to learn? If your budget buys a handful of clicks a day, you will not get enough conversions to judge. Narrow the keywords or the area so the budget can cover them.
  6. Have you given it a fair window? Plan to run at least a month, and judge on cost per customer, not clicks.
  7. Who changed what? Keep a change log, and do not accept settings changes from a rep without reading them.

If you are comparing help, our Google Ads management fees study shows how agencies price this work, and our PPC management page explains how we run accounts.

What we would change

Our view: most small businesses that "tried Google Ads" ran a test that could not answer the question. A few weeks, a few hundred dollars, no tracking anyone checked, and traffic from places the owner never chose. When that fails, the owner decides Google Ads does not work for them. In most of the threads we read, the honest answer was that they did not know yet. Set up tracking you trust, keep the money on Search, read the search terms, and run long enough to learn.

We planned to use Reddit's public JSON for r/smallbusiness, r/PPC, r/googleads and r/Entrepreneur. On 21 November 2026 it returned a block page and a login redirect, so we stopped there and did not work around it. Several owner forums in the UK, Canada and Australia also blocked plain requests. We used two sources that are readable without a login: Hacker News, through its public search API, and the Shopify Community, through its public JSON.

On Hacker News we ran 20 searches for Google Ads and AdWords experience phrases, kept the 2,734 comments they returned, and filtered to 153 that name Google Ads near a first-person phrase about spending or results. On the Shopify Community we ran 10 searches, found 637 topics and downloaded the 261 with Google Ads words in the title. We read every candidate by hand.

A report counts when a person describes a Google Ads campaign for their own business, or one they ran unpaid for a family member's or friend's business, and says something about the result. We excluded agencies and employees describing client accounts, tool vendors, pure questions, account suspensions with no result, tracking set-up questions with no result, publisher (AdSense) posts, repeat posts by the same person, and anyone identifiably outside the US, UK, Canada and Australia. Each report was coded once, by one person.

Limits. Everything here is self-reported and unchecked. People post when something has gone wrong, so failures are over-represented. Hacker News leans toward software founders, and 39 of its reports predate 2020, when the product was called AdWords. The groups are small. No usernames are stored; the data file holds thread and post links only.

Questions about Google Ads for small business

Do Google Ads work for small business?

They can. In 89 owner reports we coded, 18% said Google Ads worked, 21% had mixed results, and 51% said they did not work. These are self-reported and lean toward problems. The failures clustered around tests under a month, budgets under $500 a month, junk traffic and missing tracking.

Is Google Ads worth it for a small business with a small budget?

It can be, if the budget is focused. 7 of the 8 owners spending under $500 a month said it did not work, usually because the budget was spread too thin to learn. Narrow the keywords or the area so a small budget buys enough clicks to judge.

Why do Google Ads fail for small businesses?

The owners blamed junk traffic from Display, apps and bots (16% of problem reports), agencies or Google reps (12.5%), too few people searching (12.5%) and conversion tracking (11%). 13 of 45 failures named no cause, which usually means the owner could not see what went wrong.

How long should a small business run Google Ads before judging it?

In our view at least a month, judged on cost per customer. 5 of the 6 owners who ran ads for under a month said it did not work, and none said it worked. All 6 winners who gave a time had run it for a month or more.

Is Performance Max good for small businesses?

Owners were split. Performance Max came up in 9 reports, and 8 were mixed, mostly campaigns that worked and then dropped after a migration, a feed problem or a tracking break. It needs clean tracking and a clean feed, and a weekly check.
