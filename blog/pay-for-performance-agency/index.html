Pay for performance marketing agencies: what the model actually means

"You only pay when it works" is a sentence that deserves a second read. Someone is still carrying the risk; the question is who, and for what.

Media Puppies · 17 October 2026

The appeal of a pay for performance agency is obvious. No retainer, no paying for effort, only paying for results. For a business that has been burned by a year of fees and nothing to show, it sounds like the only sane arrangement. It can be. It is also the model most often used to make a bad deal look like a safe one, so it pays to understand exactly what "performance" is being paid for.

What a pay for performance agency is actually selling

There are three versions in the market, and they are very different businesses.

Pay per lead. The agency runs ads from its own account, owns the data, and sells you leads at a fixed price each. You never see the ad account. We have written separately about pay per lead agencies; the short version is that the leads are often sold to several buyers and the quality is whatever clears the price.

Fee plus a performance bonus. A lower base retainer with a bonus tied to an agreed target, such as cost per qualified lead or revenue at a set return on spend. The ad account is yours. This is the version that aligns incentives without inviting the problems below.

Pure revenue share. The agency takes a percentage of sales it drives, usually tracked through its own links or codes. Common in ecommerce. It works when attribution is clean and the agency controls enough of the funnel to deserve the share.

Where the risk goes

An agency that only gets paid on results protects itself in ways that are rarely stated in the pitch.

It picks clients where results are easy: established brands, proven offers, categories it already runs. If you are being offered pure performance pricing on an unproven offer, ask why.

It defines "result" generously. A lead is a form fill, not a conversation. A sale may be any order, including ones that were coming anyway through branded search. Who sets the definition decides whether the model is fair.

It controls the asset. When the ads run from the agency's account, the pixel data, the audiences and the creative learnings leave with the agency. A year later you have paid for leads and own nothing. Insist on running from your own ad account whatever the fee model; this checklist covers what that means in practice.

And it walks away faster. An agency with no retainer has no reason to stay through a bad month, which is precisely the month you most need somebody in the account.

The fair version

A base fee that covers the work of running the account properly, plus an upside tied to a number you both agreed in writing, measured in your own analytics, with the ad account in your name. The base fee keeps the agency in the account during the hard months. The bonus makes the good months better for both sides. The definition of success is written down before the first rupee is spent.

That is close to how we structure our own engagements, and it is why we talk about customer value and a target cost per customer on the first call rather than quoting a package. Agency fee models explained compares the alternatives side by side.

Questions to ask before signing a performance deal

  1. What exactly counts as a result, and who verifies it?
  2. Whose ad account does the spend run through, and who keeps the data if we part?
  3. What happens in a month where the target is missed: do you stay, and what changes?
  4. How is branded search and repeat business excluded from "performance"?
  5. What did you decline to take on this model, and why?

An agency with good answers to all five is offering something worth having. An agency that answers with "don't worry, you only pay for results" has told you where the risk sits.

Questions people ask before hiring

Is a pay for performance agency cheaper?

Often not. The per-result price has to cover the agency's risk of months with no payment, so it is usually higher than the equivalent cost under a fee-plus-bonus model. The saving is in bad months, and the premium is paid in good ones.

Do performance-only agencies run ads from my account?

Pay-per-lead agencies usually run from their own accounts and sell you the output. Fee-plus-bonus agencies run from yours. Always ask, because the account holds the pixel data and learnings that make the next year cheaper.

What is a fair performance bonus structure?

A base fee that covers the work, plus a bonus when an agreed number is beaten: cost per qualified lead, or revenue at an agreed return on spend, measured in your own analytics, with branded and repeat traffic excluded.

Why would an agency refuse a performance-only deal?

Because it cannot control the offer, the landing page, the sales follow-up or the product, all of which decide whether ads produce results. An agency that refuses is often being more honest than one that accepts and then defines "result" loosely.
