How long until paid ads work, realistically

The honest answer depends on three things, none of which is the agency. Here is what each stage needs, and what it means if you are stuck in one.

Media Puppies · 11 October 2026

How long until paid ads work is the first question on almost every agency call, and almost every answer is either a sales number or a shrug. The useful answer is a sequence of conditions rather than a date, because the clock is driven by how fast your account gathers conversions — not by effort.

Weeks one and two: nothing you can judge

Tracking is verified or rebuilt, account structure is set, the first creative and keyword sets go live. Spend in this window buys data, not results. Judging cost per acquisition here is like judging a recipe by the taste of raw flour, and an agency that reports triumphantly in week two is managing you rather than the account.

What should exist by day fourteen: conversions firing correctly and verifiably, a clean campaign structure, and a first set of ads actually serving rather than stuck in review.

Weeks three to six: the learning phase decides the pace

Both platforms need a volume of conversion events per ad set or campaign before their bidding stabilises. Below that threshold, performance swings wildly and every reading is noise. This is the real constraint on how fast anything can work, and it is arithmetic rather than opinion: conversions needed, divided by your conversion rate, times your cost per click, gives the spend required. The learning phase calculator does it in one screen, and the longer explanation covers why resets hurt.

Two consequences follow. A high-ticket business with few conversions a month will take longer than a low-ticket one at the same spend. And an account that is restructured every fortnight never finishes learning at all.

Weeks six to twelve: the first honest read

By now you should have enough conversions to say something true about which audiences, keywords and creative work. This is the first point where cost per lead or ROAS means anything. It is also the point where the decision gets made: scale what works, cut what does not, or conclude that the offer rather than the media is the problem.

Three months is the first point at which an agency can be fairly judged. One month is a judgement about whether they set it up competently, which is a different and still useful question.

Month three onward: compounding, if it is going to

Working accounts get cheaper over time — the platform knows your buyer, creative testing has a winner to beat, and negative keyword and audience exclusion work has removed the obvious waste. Accounts that are not going to work plateau here instead, and the plateau is informative. Scaling past it has its own rules, covered in scaling ad spend without losing ROAS.

What makes paid ads take longer to work

  • Low conversion volume. The dominant factor. Few conversions means slow learning, however much is spent per click.
  • Broken or partial tracking. If the platform cannot see outcomes, it optimises toward the wrong thing from day one.
  • A weak landing page. Media cannot fix a page that does not convert; it only pays more to discover that. Run the landing page checklist before launch rather than in month two.
  • Constant changes. Every significant edit restarts learning. Patience is a technical requirement, not a personality trait.
  • Long sales cycles. In B2B, the feedback loop is the sales cycle. If deals take ninety days, you will not know in thirty.

How long until paid ads work: what to commit to

Three months is the shortest window in which a competent agency can set up, learn and show you a defensible read — which is why pilots shorter than that mostly test onboarding rather than media. It should not require a twelve-month lock-in to get there. We work month to month for exactly this reason, and say so on the pricing page; if the work is not worth paying for in month four, a contract should not be what keeps us there. Before any of it starts, a free account audit will usually tell you which of the slowdowns above you are about to hit.

Questions people ask before hiring

How long does it take for Google Ads to work?

Expect two weeks of setup and data gathering, four to six weeks for bidding to stabilise, and a first trustworthy read somewhere between week six and week twelve. Accounts with few monthly conversions sit at the slower end.

Why do my results swing so much in the first month?

Because the platform has not yet seen enough conversions to bid consistently. Early swings are usually noise rather than signal, and reacting to them by changing budgets or targeting restarts the learning each time.

Is one month long enough to judge an agency?

It is long enough to judge their setup — tracking, structure, creative, speed — but not their results. Results need the conversion volume that one month rarely provides.

Does a bigger budget make ads work faster?

Up to a point, yes, because it gathers conversions faster. It does not help if the conversion rate or the offer is the constraint, and spending faster on a page that does not convert simply reaches the wrong conclusion sooner.

What should I see in the first two weeks?

Verified conversion tracking, a clean account structure, ads live and out of review, and a written note of what is being tested first. Not a cost per lead you can rely on.
