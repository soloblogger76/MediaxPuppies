White label PPC vs winning clients yourself: what agencies pay per click (2026 data)

We pulled the 15 agency searches out of our 339-keyword CPC study, checked which of them Google answers with an AI Overview, and set them next to 150 live Facebook ads that say "marketing agency".

Key findings

  • In Semrush US estimates from 3 November 2026, "google ads management" was $228.10 a click, the second highest of the 339 keywords we priced, and "ppc management" was $95.27.

Agency owners who want to add PPC usually weigh white label PPC services against building the service and selling it themselves. This post looks at one side of that sum: what it costs to win a PPC client in the first place, if you go out and buy the click.

Winning those clients is a cost of its own. Our PPC agency CPC study shows what agencies pay per click for searches like "ppc agency" in the US, UK, Canada and Australia.

So we went back to three studies we ran this month and pulled out every figure about agencies. Our CPC study priced 339 US keywords in Semrush, 15 of them searches people make when they want an agency. Our AI Overviews study checked those same searches on Google. Our marketing agency ads study read 150 live Facebook ads. We expected the white label PPC search to be expensive. It sat in the middle of the study, while the searches a business makes to find PPC help sat near the top of the price list.

The clicks that win a PPC client

In Semrush's US estimates from 3 November, "google ads management" came in at $228.10 a click. Of all 339 keywords we priced, across law, insurance, software, home services, dental and the rest, only one was higher: "truck accident lawyer" at $252.78. We did not expect an agency search to sit next to personal injury law.

The other ways a business looks for PPC help were cheaper but still high. "ppc management" was estimated at $95.27, "facebook ads agency" at $67.73, "ppc agency" at $46.56 and "google ads agency" at $45.44. These are the searches an agency would bid on to win PPC clients directly, and every one of them was above the $22.49 median for marketing services.

That median put marketing services 3rd of the 11 industries we priced, behind insurance ($35.44) and software ($28.87) and ahead of home services ($17.62) and legal ($13.68). Legal has the higher peaks: 11 of its 37 keywords were estimated at $100 or more, and its mean was $60.92, but its median was only $13.68. Across all 339 keywords the median was $12.32. 53.3% of the marketing services keywords were estimated at $20 or more a click.

A Semrush CPC is an estimate of what advertisers bid. It is no invoice. Your real cost depends on match type, location, schedule and Quality Score, and the figure says nothing about how many of those clicks become clients. In our view it is still a useful public guide to how much advertisers bid for the same click.

Where white label PPC sits in the same data

"white label ppc" was estimated at $14.28 a click. That put it 164th of the 339 keywords and 11th of the 15 marketing services keywords, just above the $12.32 median for the whole study. "white label seo" was $18.94 and "white label marketing" $10.11. Semrush gave "white label ppc" 1,900 US searches a month and a keyword difficulty of 18, the lowest difficulty of the 15 marketing services keywords.

Put side by side, the "google ads management" estimate is 16.0 times the "white label ppc" estimate. "ppc management" is 6.7 times, "facebook ads agency" 4.7 times, "ppc agency" 3.3 times and "google ads agency" 3.2 times.

The "100 clicks" column is our arithmetic on the estimates, and it stops at the click. We have no conversion data for these searches, so we cannot say how many clicks it takes to sign a client. The table shows only the entry fee for being in the auction.

One caution on reading the lower number. Our reading is that the person typing "white label ppc" is most often an agency looking for a partner, so that $14.28 estimate mostly reflects what partners bid to reach agencies. A CPC estimate does not count bidders, and it is no measure of what an agency pays for white label work; for how partners bill, see our guide to white label PPC pricing.

Google answers most agency searches before the first link

If paid clicks are dear, the free route is ranking organically for those searches. Our AI Overviews study checked the same 339 searches on Google in the US on 3 November. 14 of the 15 marketing services searches showed an AI Overview, 93.3%, second only to software (100%) and far above the 57.8% rate across all 339 searches. The one marketing search without an AI Overview was "lead generation companies".

The marketing searches we checked outside the US looked similar. Ten of the marketing services searches were in our UK, Canada and Australia subset of 118 searches. All 10 showed an AI Overview in the UK and in Canada, and 8 of 10 did in Australia. "google ads management" was the highest-CPC search in the whole study to show an AI Overview.

"white label ppc" had one too. Its AI Overview cited 3 sources: reddit.com, clicksgeek.com and ppc.co. That study did not measure ads, so we cannot say where the paid results sat on these pages. In our view, an agency hoping to win PPC clients through organic search alone is competing with an AI answer as well as with every other agency, on the same searches where paid clicks are most expensive.

The Facebook route: crowded with people selling to agencies

Meta is another place agencies look for clients. When we read the first 150 live US ads in the Ad Library that contain the phrase "marketing agency", 34 of them (22.7%) were selling to agency owners or people who want to start an agency: coaching, communities, templates, platforms and outsourcing. They came from 12 pages, and one coaching page alone had 14 ads. We expected that phrase to bring up agencies looking for clients. More than one ad in five was looking for agencies instead.

52 of the 93 pages were agencies selling to businesses, with 81 ads between them. 36 of the 52 (69.2%) ran at least one ad with a video player. Half (50%) named the trade they serve, such as roofing, dentists or car dealers. Yet 31 of the 52 (59.6%) had no offer, no figure and no guarantee that our word search could find in any of their ads. 25% gave a dollar figure, a ROAS or a count of leads or clients (a dollar figure can be a price as well as a result); 11.5% offered something free or an audit; 11.5% offered a guarantee, pay for results or no contract. The full breakdown is in our marketing agency ads study.

Our reading: on Facebook, an agency that wants PPC clients is bidding for attention next to other agencies, most of which gave no offer, figure or guarantee we could find, and beside sellers aiming at agency owners themselves. The surprise for us was how few of those agencies gave a business any number to check.

What the numbers mean for an agency adding white label PPC

None of the three studies measures profit, so this part is our view, built on the figures above.

  • The expensive step is winning the client. The searches that bring a business looking for PPC help were estimated at $45.44 to $228.10 a click in the US. If you already have clients for SEO, web design or social, you have already paid to win them. Our view: offering those clients PPC through a partner skips the most expensive clicks in this data.
  • Selling direct means competing on two fronts. On Google, 14 of 15 agency searches had an AI Overview. On Facebook, more than one ad in five for "marketing agency" was selling to agencies. In our view, a new PPC offer that relies only on cold acquisition starts against both.
  • If you do advertise for clients, be specific. Half the agencies on Facebook named a trade, and 59.6% gave no offer, proof or risk reversal we could find. Our view is that a named trade plus one checkable number would set an agency ad apart from most of that list.
  • Check the partner side with the same care. Our view: a lower estimated CPC on "white label ppc" suggests partners bid less to reach agencies than agencies bid to reach clients. It says nothing about which partner is any good. Our list of questions to ask a white label partner and our guide to white label reporting cover what to check before you hand over a client.

This page collects nothing new. It takes the agency figures from three studies we published in November 2026, each with its own method page. CPC figures come from our CPC study: Semrush Keyword Overview bulk exports, US database, in US dollars, pulled on 3 November 2026 for 339 keywords in 11 industries. Semrush CPC is an estimate of what advertisers bid for a click in the US; it is no record of what any advertiser paid, and the real price moves with match type, location, time of day and Quality Score. AI Overview figures come from our AI Overviews study: Google results pages for the same 339 keywords fetched with the Apify Google Search Scraper on 3 November 2026 (UTC), desktop, English, no city set, plus a 118-keyword subset in the UK, Canada and Australia. Ten of the marketing services keywords were in that subset; "white label ppc" was not. That study did not measure ads, and an AI Overview that loads late can be missed.

Facebook figures come from our marketing agency ads study: we searched the Meta Ad Library on 10 November 2026 (IST) for the exact phrase "marketing agency", US, active ads, default order, and read the first 150 of about 5,200 results. Advertiser types are our hand labels; offers, proof and guarantees come from word patterns in the main ad text only.

Ratios and "cost of 100 clicks" are our own arithmetic on the Semrush estimates, made in compile.py and marked as computed in the dataset. None of these sources shows conversion rates, lead costs or client revenue, so nothing here says what a client is worth or which route makes more money.

Questions about white label PPC economics

What does a click cost when a business searches for a PPC agency?

In Semrush US estimates from 3 November 2026, "google ads management" was $228.10 a click, "ppc management" $95.27, "ppc agency" $46.56 and "google ads agency" $45.44. These are estimates of advertiser bids, so a real account can pay more or less.

What is the CPC for white label PPC?

Semrush estimated "white label ppc" at $14.28 a click in the US on 3 November 2026, with 1,900 US searches a month and a keyword difficulty of 18. In our view it mostly reflects what partners bid to reach agencies, so it does not tell you what a partner charges.

Do Google AI Overviews show on searches for PPC agencies?

Mostly yes. On 3 November 2026, 14 of the 15 US marketing services searches we checked showed an AI Overview, including "white label ppc" and "google ads management". All 10 marketing searches in our UK and Canada checks showed one, and 8 of 10 in Australia.

Is white label PPC cheaper than winning PPC clients yourself?

Our data covers the cost of clicks. It has no figures for the cost of a client or for a partner's fee, so it cannot answer that on its own. It does show that the searches businesses use to find PPC help were among the most expensive in our 339-keyword study. In our view, that favours offering PPC to clients you already have.
