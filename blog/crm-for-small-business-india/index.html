Do you need a CRM, or just a better spreadsheet?

Most small businesses buy a CRM, use 4% of it, and go back to WhatsApp. Here's the honest threshold.

Media Puppies · 24 July 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026

A CRM for small business is sold as a growth requirement. For a lot of Indian small businesses it becomes an expensive place where leads go to be forgotten, while the actual work continues on WhatsApp.

Here's the honest threshold.

You don't need one yet if

  • Fewer than roughly 30 leads a month.
  • One or two people handle everything.
  • Nobody has ever lost a lead because it wasn't written down.

At this size, WhatsApp Business labels plus a shared sheet genuinely works. Labels give you a pipeline, the sheet gives you a record.

You need one when

  • More than one person talks to the same leads and they're stepping on each other.
  • You can't answer "how many leads did we get last month and what happened to them".
  • Follow-up depends on memory rather than a system.
  • You want to send qualified-lead data back to ad platforms — this one alone often justifies it, because it improves every campaign you run.

The moment a lead can be forgotten without anyone noticing, you have outgrown the spreadsheet.

What to look for in a CRM for small business

  1. WhatsApp integration that actually works. If conversations don't land in the CRM, your team will keep working outside it and the data will be fiction. This is the first filter, not a nice-to-have.
  2. Mobile app your team will use. Field sales won't open a laptop.
  3. Simple pipeline stages. Five, not fifteen.
  4. Automation triggers — assign, remind, escalate.
  5. Export and API access, so you can send conversion data back to ad platforms.

Notice what isn't on the list: forecasting, territory management, lead scoring models. Those are enterprise features that mostly generate configuration work.

The implementation that fails

Buying the tool, importing everything, designing a twelve-stage pipeline, training everyone once — and discovering a month later that the team is back on WhatsApp because the CRM adds work without removing any.

The version that sticks:

  1. Start with one pipeline and five stages.
  2. Connect the lead source so entries appear automatically — nobody should type a lead in by hand.
  3. Connect WhatsApp so conversations are captured without extra effort.
  4. Add one automation that visibly saves time — instant acknowledgement, or auto-assignment.
  5. Only then add fields, reports and stages, driven by questions you actually asked.

The test after 30 days

Ask your team whether the CRM saves them time or costs them time. If it costs, they will quietly stop using it and your reports will slowly become wrong — which is worse than having no CRM, because you'll trust the numbers.

Questions people ask

When does a small business actually need a CRM?

When more than one person touches the same lead, when enquiries arrive faster than anyone can remember them, or when you cannot answer what happened to last month's leads. Before that, a shared sheet with a status column is genuinely enough.

What should I look for in a CRM?

In order: does it capture WhatsApp conversations, can your team actually use it on a phone, does it accept leads from your website form automatically, and can you get your data out. Feature lists matter far less than whether it gets used daily.

Why do CRM rollouts fail?

Because the real conversation stays on WhatsApp and the CRM becomes a form someone fills in afterwards, badly. If logging an update takes longer than the follow-up itself, the record will be wrong within a fortnight.

Can I just use a spreadsheet?

For a while, yes — one row per lead, a status column, an owner and a next-action date. It fails when two people edit at once, when you need reminders, or when you want to know which source produced revenue rather than just enquiries.
