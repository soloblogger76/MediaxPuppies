Plumber Google Ads cost in 2026: click prices in the US, UK, Canada and Australia

We sorted 200 plumber keywords from Ahrefs across four countries by what the searcher typed. Australia had the dearest median click. Each country also hid its dearest clicks in a different place: urgency in the US, city names in Canada, and "emergency" plus a London borough in the UK.

Key findings

  • On Ahrefs estimates of 17 November 2026, the median plumber buyer search cost $8 a click in Australia, $7 in the US, $7 in Canada and $4 in the UK. 100 clicks at the median: $800, $700, $700 and $400.
  • US plumber clicks are priced on urgency. Emergency, 24 hour and same day searches had a median of $12.50, 4.17 times the $3 median of other US plumber searches. In Canada urgent searches came in below the rest, at 0.64 times.
  • Canada prices the city name. "plumber" and "plumber near me" were $2.50 each, while the 26 Canadian city searches had a median of $8, and all 26 cost more than "plumber".
  • The UK had the cheapest head term in our data, $1.90 for "plumber", and two of the dearest UK clicks: "emergency plumber london" and "emergency plumber croydon" at $20 each.
  • On 4 US plumber keywords in both tools, Ahrefs priced clicks at a median 39.14% of Semrush. Semrush put "emergency plumber" at $43.65 and Ahrefs at $20.

Plumber Google Ads clicks cost the most in Australia, on the median. We took the top 50 plumber keywords in Ahrefs for the US, the UK, Canada and Australia on 17 November 2026, 200 rows in all, and kept the 147 that are someone looking to hire a plumber and carry a price. Every figure here is an Ahrefs estimate in US dollars. These are modelled numbers; only Google sees the real auction.

The short answer. The median plumber buyer search cost $8 a click in Australia, $7 in the US, $7 in Canada and $4 in the UK. That is $800, $700, $700 and $400 per 100 clicks. The more useful finding is where each country puts its dear clicks. In the US it is urgency. In Canada it is the city name. In the UK the plain searches are cheap, and the price jumps when "emergency" meets a London borough.

We have already compared Google Ads, Local Services Ads and Angi for US plumbers, and our plumbing PPC guide covers account setup. This page adds what those two do not have: the click price by country and by search type, with the keyword count beside every median.

Plumber Google Ads cost by country

We expected the US to lead, as it does for most trades. Australia came out on top instead, and it was also the steadiest market: its middle half of keywords ran from $6 to $9.75, and its cheapest buyer keyword was $3.

The volume-weighted column tells a second story. In the US it rises to $9.51, because "plumber near me" ($7) and "plumber" ($10) carry 65.03% of all US buyer search volume in the list, and the urgent terms behind them are dear too. In Canada it falls to $4.20, because the two head terms carry 51.4% of volume and Ahrefs prices both at $2.50. So a Canadian plumber who bids only on the head terms pays far less than the median suggests, and one who targets city names pays more.

The US spread is the widest. The middle half runs from $2.50 to $12, and the list tops out at $30 for "plumber phoenix". The UK has only 2 buyer keywords at $10 or more, against 10 or 11 in each of the other three countries.

One note on cleaning. Before we removed 18 rows by hand (named firms such as "len the plumber", products such as "plumber putty", a salary question), the parent study had the US at $5.25 and Canada at $5. Those rows were cheap and pulled the medians down. If you read a plumber CPC figure elsewhere, it is worth asking whether someone checked the list by eye.

Where each country hides the dear plumber clicks

Across all the home-service trades in the parent study, emergency searches cost 3.33 times other searches in the US. For plumbers the US gap is wider still. Urgent searches (emergency, 24 hour, 24/7, same day) had a median of $12.50 against $3 for every other US plumber search, a premium of 4.17 times. "Same day plumber" was $25 and "emergency plumber" $20.

The pattern weakens as you move away from the US. In the UK urgent searches were 2 times the rest, in Australia 1.29 times, and in Canada they came in below the rest, at 0.64 times. The plain pair "emergency plumber" and "plumber" shows the same thing on two keywords that exist in every country:

Canada is the odd one, and the reason is in the city searches. Canada's urgent terms carry real prices of their own ("emergency plumber toronto" was $12). They look cheap only because the rest of the Canadian list is city names, and those are expensive.

Canada: the city name is the premium

"Plumber" and "plumber near me" were $2.50 each in Canada. The 26 Canadian searches for "plumber" plus a city had a median of $8, 3.2 times the head term, and every one of the 26 cost more than "plumber". Surrey, Langley, Toronto, Calgary, Hamilton and Guelph all sat at $11 to $12.

The UK follows the same direction on a smaller scale: 18 city searches with a median of $3.50, all 18 above the $1.90 for "plumber". Australia is flat. Its 24 city and suburb searches had the same $7 median as "plumber", though Sydney's "inner west" reached $17 and "plumber newcastle" $14. The US list had only one city search in its top 50, so we cannot say much about US cities from this pull.

Our view on why Canada looks like this: most Canadian plumbing firms serve one metro and bid on its name, so the competition stacks up on "plumber toronto" and "plumber calgary". "Plumber near me" relies on Google's location signal and spreads the bidding over more advertisers and a wider area. This is the same pattern we saw in our locksmith click study in three of four countries.

The UK: cheap head terms, dear London emergencies

At $1.90, "plumber" in the UK was the cheapest head term in our four-country data, and "plumber near me" was $2. Add "emergency" and a London place and the price jumps. "Emergency plumber london" and "emergency plumber croydon" were $20 each, the two dearest UK clicks, against $5 for "plumber london". Outside London the jump was smaller: "emergency plumber leeds" $7, "emergency plumber manchester" $4.50.

What we would change in a plumber Google Ads account

  • In the US, split urgent searches into their own campaign. At 4.17 times the price of other US plumber searches, they need their own budget, and ad text that promises a time. Our view is that an urgent click that lands on a general services page is the most expensive mistake in a US plumbing account.
  • In Canada, build city ad groups and keep "plumber near me" running. The head terms were the cheapest Canadian clicks we saw at $2.50. City terms cost more and usually bring a searcher who already knows the area they want served.
  • In the UK, bid on the head terms and be careful with London emergencies. A $20 click on "emergency plumber london" needs a firm that can really reach the borough fast. Outside London the emergency premium is modest.
  • In Australia, use job-named ad groups. Blocked drain, hot water and gas searches had real volume and sat close to the national median, which makes them good places to start.
  • Clean the search terms report every week. 10 of our 200 rows were searches for a named plumbing firm, and others were product searches. Our negative keywords audit covers the routine, and our call tracking guide covers measuring the calls that urgent clicks bring in.

These rows are the "plumber" part of our Google Ads CPC by country study. On 17 November 2026 we opened the Ahrefs "Matching terms" report for "plumber" in the US, UK, Australian and Canadian databases and kept the top 50 keywords by monthly search volume. Ahrefs shows CPC in US dollars for every country. 14 rows had no CPC and are left out; 9 of them were US strings such as "plumber glenwillow ohio" that look like small-place variants.

The parent study flags non-buyer searches with a written rule (salary, jobs, apprenticeships, tools, insurance). It caught 22 plumber rows. We read the rest by eye and set 18 more to non-buyer for this page: 10 searches for one named plumbing business, product searches such as "plumber putty", a salary question, a licence term, one adult term and one marketing term. That moves the US median from $5.25 to $7 and the Canadian median from $5 to $7. The list is in compile.py.

Each buyer keyword sits in one group, first match wins: urgent with a place name, urgent with no place name (emergency, 24 hour, 24/7, same day, open now), cost question, job or specialty (blocked drain, gas, hot water, roof, commercial, residential), near me, generic ("plumber", "local plumber", "find a plumber") and city or suburb.

Questions about plumber Google Ads costs

How much do plumber Google Ads cost per click?

On Ahrefs estimates of 17 November 2026, the median plumber buyer search cost $8 a click in Australia, $7 in the US, $7 in Canada and $4 in the UK. Semrush puts US plumbing higher, with a median of $23.29 across 28 keywords. Your own price depends on your bids, ads, quality and area.

Are emergency plumber keywords more expensive?

In the US, yes, by a wide margin: emergency, 24 hour and same day searches had a median 4.17 times other US plumber searches. The premium was 2 times in the UK and 1.29 times in Australia. In Canada urgent searches came in below the rest, because Canadian city searches were dearer.

Why are Australian plumber clicks so expensive?

We can only describe it from the data. Australia's head terms were $7 and its suburb and job searches were priced high and evenly: the middle half of its keywords ran from $6 to $9.75, and none was below $3. The US is still dearer on the shared head terms.

How much do 100 plumber clicks cost?

At the Ahrefs median buyer CPC, 100 clicks come to about $800 in Australia, $700 in the US, $700 in Canada and $400 in the UK, in US dollars. US urgent searches run much higher, so check Keyword Planner for your area and run a short test.

Should a plumber bid on city keywords or near me keywords?

It depends on the country. In Canada and the UK every city search we saw cost more than the word "plumber", and "plumber near me" was among the cheapest clicks. Our view is to run both and judge them on cost per booked job.
