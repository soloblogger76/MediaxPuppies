Blog or service pages? Where to put your effort first

Six months of blogging produced traffic and no revenue. It usually means the effort went to the wrong page type.

Media Puppies · 18 September 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026

Blog or service pages: the mistake is almost always the same — a business writes twenty articles about its industry while its actual service pages are three sentences and a form.

The test: blog or service pages

Look at the search term and ask what the person wants right now.

QueryWantsPage type
"modular kitchen price gurgaon"To buy soonService / location page
"modular vs carpenter kitchen"To decideComparison article
"how to clean a chimney"To fix somethingArticle, low commercial value

Writing a blog post for the first row is the expensive mistake. A buying query deserves a page built to sell.

Why service pages win first

  • They match the highest-intent searches.
  • They convert, so ranking them produces revenue not traffic.
  • They're the natural destination for internal links from everything else.
  • There are fewer of them, so you can make each genuinely good.

Rank a service page and you get customers. Rank a blog post and you get readers you then have to convert.

Where the blog earns its place

Three jobs, all real:

  1. Research-stage questions that precede a purchase — you build familiarity before the decision.
  2. A retargeting audience. Article readers are cheap to reach again with an offer two weeks later.
  3. Proof of competence. Someone checking you out after an ad reads two articles and decides you know the work.

The order

Service pages, then location pages if relevant, then category pages, then the blog. Most businesses do this exactly backwards.

A sequence that works

For a business starting from nothing, the order matters more than the volume.

  1. One service page per thing you sell. Not a combined page with seven sections — one page per service, each able to rank on its own and convert on its own.
  2. Location pages, but only where you genuinely operate. Two honest ones beat eleven templated ones.
  3. Three to five comparison and cost articles. "X vs Y", "what does X cost", "how to choose an X". These sit closest to the money and they are what people actually search before buying.
  4. Then the wider blog. Once the commercial pages exist and are linked, articles have somewhere useful to send authority.

Most businesses do this in reverse and wonder why forty articles produced traffic and no enquiries.

What a service page needs that a blog post does not

  • A price signal. A range, a starting point, or an explicit minimum. Silence on price is the single biggest reason a good service page fails to convert.
  • Proof specific to that service. Not a general testimonial wall — evidence about this exact thing.
  • The objection answered on the page. Whatever the prospect is worried about, answer it before the form, not in an FAQ nobody opens.
  • One action. A blog post can afford several exits. A service page should have one.

How to tell which one a page should be

Search the term yourself and look at what already ranks. Google has already decided what kind of page satisfies that query, and it is not going to make an exception for you. If the first page is all service pages, writing an article is choosing to lose. If it is all guides and listicles, a sales page will not break in.

This one check saves more wasted writing than any keyword tool, and it takes thirty seconds per topic.

When the blog genuinely comes first

There is one case for writing before selling: when nobody is searching for your service yet because the category is new. Then articles are how you create the demand your service page will later capture. If you are selling something with existing search volume — and almost everyone is — the service page comes first.

Questions people ask

Should I write a blog post or a service page?

Look at what already ranks for the term. If the first page is mostly guides, Google has decided the intent is informational and a service page will not break in. If it is mostly service and product pages, an article will not either. Match the format first.

Why do service pages come first?

Because they target people who are ready to buy, and most sites have far fewer of them than they need. One properly built service page per thing you sell usually produces more enquiries than twenty articles about your industry.

How many service pages should a business have?

One per service you would genuinely take an enquiry for, plus a location page per place you actively sell in. Not one per keyword variation — near-identical pages compete with each other and dilute the one that should rank.

When does the blog genuinely come first?

When the service pages already exist and are good, when your buyers research for months before they buy, or when you need the authority and internal links that a body of content provides. In that order.
