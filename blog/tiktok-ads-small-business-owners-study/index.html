Do TikTok ads work for small business? We coded 120 owners' own reports

We read every public post we could find from small business owners describing TikTok ads they ran themselves. 120 qualified. Almost all were stores that bought cheap clicks and sold nothing, and the reasons they found say more about the store than the platform.

Do TikTok ads work for small business? In the 120 first-hand reports we found in public forums, 1 owner said TikTok ads worked, 12 had mixed results, 90 said they did not work and 17 could not tell. Of the 103 who gave a clear verdict, 90 (87%) said TikTok ads did not work for them.

That number needs context before anyone acts on it. 115 of the 120 reports come from the Shopify Community, a help forum where owners post when the sales do not come, and 48 came from stores that had just opened. Read this study as a map of how TikTok ads go wrong for small stores. It cannot tell you how often they pay, because owners whose ads work rarely write in.

This is the sixth owner study in our series, after Google Ads, Facebook ads, Microsoft Ads, LinkedIn Ads and Performance Max, with the same sources and rules. We aimed for at least 40 reports and found 120. We run TikTok ads for clients and label our opinions. None of our own client or account data is in here.

Where the 120 reports come from

On 25 November 2026 we searched the Shopify Community and Hacker News, two public forums that load without a login. Reddit blocked plain requests four days earlier, so we did not try it.

On the Shopify Community, 38 searches returned 894 topics. We read 451 posts in 396 of them that named TikTok and described running ads or a result. Most of what we read was something else: TikTok Shop sync errors, pixel set-up questions, organic posting, and app makers and agencies offering help. Stores that priced in euros or Swiss francs, or that sold into Germany, France, Poland, Spain and other markets outside the US, UK, Canada and Australia, were left out. On Hacker News, 27 comment searches returned 4,930 comments. We read the 178 that paired a TikTok ad phrase with a first-person word, and found 5 reports.

We expected a negative sample. What surprised us was how little of it was about TikTok. Most owners described a store that got cheap clicks and sold nothing, often on every ad platform they tried at the same time. The problems agencies talk about most, such as account bans, bot traffic and tired creative, came up only a handful of times.

The one positive report came from an in-house marketer at a group of women's health clinics, who wrote that "Many of our users come from Instagram and TikTok ads." Friends in the clinics' target markets knew the brand from those ads, the marketer added, and never from search or billboards.

The 12 mixed reports were stores that made some sales and could not hold them. One owner had profitable days followed by empty ones and wrote that "some days the results are simply fantastic, while the following days wreck the profitability". Another had a profitable first half-day and then very little: "after the first 13-14 hours it almost seems TikTok ditched me."

In our view, 1 happy report out of 120 says more about who posts in a help forum than about TikTok. What the sample does show clearly is the shape of the failure, and it is the same story repeated dozens of times.

Clicks without sales was the main story

76 of the 120 reports (63.3%) described clicks or store visits with no sales, and 20 (16.7%) got add-to-carts or checkouts that never turned into purchases. One store that limited its ads to the US and Canada still wrote: "I have only gotten 4 sales out of 24k visitors".

The clicks were cheap. The four owners who gave a cost per click reported $0.02, $0.05, $0.21 and $0.25. Many described click-through rates and video views they considered excellent. 8 reports (6.7%) found much of that traffic came from the wrong country or audience, and 4 (3.3%) suspected bots.

In our view, cheap TikTok clicks are the trap in this sample. Ads Manager shows a strong click-through rate and a low cost per click, and the owner reads that as a winning product. The people clicking were mid-scroll, many were outside the store's market, and the store had nothing yet (reviews, a known name, a checkout that felt safe) to turn a curious tap into an order. Our landing page checklist covers the gaps we saw most, and our landing page study shows how often ad landing pages miss basics such as a working pixel or the ad's offer.

TikTok was often one of several channels that failed

In 49 reports (40.8%) the owner ran Meta, Google, Pinterest, Snapchat or Reddit ads at the same time as TikTok. 41 of those 49 said nothing sold on any of them. In our view, when every paid channel fails on the same store in the same weeks, the store, the product or the price is the likelier cause, and switching platforms will not fix it.

Only 3 owners compared TikTok with another platform directly. A founder selling to businesses wrote: "I have tried Google, Reddit, TikTok ads for B2B and they all performed worse per dollar." He meant worse than Meta. A store owner who had run Google Ads for other shops found far fewer TikTok checkouts turning into orders. A third store got its only sales from Pinterest and none from Meta or TikTok. Two owners said their only sales came from organic or influencer posts.

That matches our Facebook ads owner study, where most Shopify Community owners, many of them running new stores, said Meta ads had not worked either. Our guide to choosing Google or Meta first explains when to start where buyers are already searching.

New stores and short tests

48 of the 120 reports (40%) came from stores that had just opened. 45 said TikTok ads did not work, 3 were mixed and none said they worked. The 69 other online stores did a little better, and still no store in either group said TikTok ads worked: 9 were mixed, 44 said they did not work and 16 could not tell.

The tests were short. 48 owners said how long they had run the ads, and 29 of them had run for a week or less. 22 of those 29 had already decided it did not work. Several posted after 1 or 2 days and a budget of $10 to $50.

In our view, a 2-day test on a store with no reviews and no sales history measures the store more than the channel. TikTok needs purchase data to find buyers, and a new store sends it none. A test needs a budget and an end date set in advance, and it should be judged on add-to-carts and purchases.

When TikTok and Shopify disagree

23 of the 120 reports (19.2%) found TikTok and Shopify telling different stories. 16 said the TikTok pixel counted conversions that were not real orders, or missed ones that were. 9 said TikTok reported clicks that never showed up as store visits. 14 of the 17 owners who could not tell whether their ads paid were in this group.

The clearest case came from a clothing store: "In TikTok it says 142 conversions on the conversion campaign but Shopify shows 0 sales." TikTok support told the owner to trust Ads Manager. The owner found the gap only by comparing the two dashboards, and doubted TikTok's explanation.

In our view, this is the cheapest problem in the sample to fix. Place a test order, check that it appears once in TikTok with the right value, and compare TikTok clicks with Shopify sessions from the same day. Our guides to Shopify customer events tracking and server-side tracking cover the checks.

What owners rarely complained about

Account flags, ad review delays, billing and platform errors came up in 6 reports (5%). Results that swung or fell away after a good start came up in 5 (4.2%), and clicks that cost too much in 5 (4.2%). Creative fatigue, the usual agency explanation for a TikTok account that stops working, barely appears, because most owners in the sample never had a working ad to tire. Our note on ad frequency and fatigue explains what it looks like when you do.

What we would change (our view)

In our view, TikTok ads can work for a small business with a product people buy on impulse, a store that converts and tracking that matches real orders. Most owners in this sample were missing at least two of those. These are the checks we would run first:

  1. Does the store sell without ads? If friends, organic posts or another channel have produced no sales, fix the offer, price and trust signals first. In 41 of these reports every paid channel failed at once.
  2. Does a test order show up once, with the right value? Place one from a phone, then check TikTok Events Manager and Shopify the next day. Compare TikTok clicks with Shopify sessions too.
  3. Are the clicks from people you can ship to? Set location targeting to your markets and check the country report in Shopify after the first day.
  4. Is the test long enough to mean anything? Set a budget and an end date before launch, and judge on add-to-carts and purchases. A cheap click and a high click-through rate prove interest in the video, which is a different thing from interest in the product.
  5. Is TikTok the right first channel? A new store with no reviews may do better starting where buyers already search, and adding TikTok once it has sales to learn from.

If you want a second pair of eyes, our TikTok ads agency page explains how we run TikTok accounts, and our free ad account audit starts with the tracking and targeting checks above.

For agencies serving small stores, the lesson from these 120 owners is to audit the store before the ad account. The ads usually delivered what they promised, which was clicks. The stores were not ready to turn them into orders.

This study copies the method of our owner studies on Google Ads, Facebook ads, Microsoft Ads, LinkedIn Ads and Performance Max from 21 to 24 November 2026. Reddit blocked plain requests on 21 November, so we did not try it, and we did not work around any block, CAPTCHA or login. We used two sources that are readable without a login: the Shopify Community, through its public JSON, and Hacker News, through its public search API.

On the Shopify Community we ran 38 searches such as "tiktok ads", "tiktok ads no sales", "tiktok pixel", "tiktok ad account banned" and "tiktok vs facebook ads". They returned 894 topics. We downloaded the first page of posts in the 574 topics whose title names TikTok or an advertising word, and read every original post that names TikTok together with a result, spend or campaign word, plus replies where another owner describes running ads: 451 posts in 396 topics. On Hacker News we ran 27 comment searches and 5 post searches, kept 4,930 comments and 494 posts, and read the 178 comments that pair a TikTok ad phrase with a first-person word.

A report counts when the writer ran paid TikTok ads (In-Feed ads, Spark Ads or Promote) for their own business, or for their employer as an in-house marketer, and says something about the result. We excluded TikTok Shop selling and sync questions, which were a large share of the threads, organic posting only, agencies, freelancers, consultants and app vendors, set-up questions where ads had not run, shoppers who bought from an ad, non-English threads, and stores identifiably outside the US, UK, Canada and Australia, including stores pricing in euros, Swiss francs or Polish zloty. People who posted more than once were found by matching text and by comparing authors in memory, and count once. Each report was coded once, by one person.

Limits. Everything is self-reported and unchecked. 115 of the 120 reports come from the Shopify Community, a help forum where owners post when sales do not come, and several of our searches used "no sales" wording, so the sample leans negative by design. 48 reports are from stores that had just opened, and 49 owners were running other paid ads at the same time. Only 17 reports gave any spend, ROAS or cost figure. No usernames are stored; the data file holds thread and post links only.

Questions about TikTok ads for small business

Do TikTok ads work for small business?

In the 120 first-hand reports we found, 1 owner said TikTok ads worked, 12 had mixed results, 90 said they did not work and 17 could not tell. The sample comes mostly from a Shopify help forum, where owners post when sales do not come, and 48 of the reports were from stores that had just opened.

Why do my TikTok ads get clicks but no sales?

That was the most common problem in our sample: 76 of 120 owners described clicks or visits with no sales, and 20 got add-to-carts or checkouts with no purchases. The usual causes were a store that had no reviews or trust signals yet, clicks from countries the store did not ship to, and tests that ran for only a day or two.

Why does TikTok show conversions that Shopify does not?

23 of the 120 owners found TikTok and Shopify disagreeing. Some had a pixel event counting as a conversion that was not a purchase, and others had clicks that never appeared as store sessions. Place a test order, confirm it appears once in TikTok with the right value, and compare clicks with sessions for the same day.

Should a new store start with TikTok ads?

In our sample, 45 of the 48 stores that had just opened said TikTok ads did not work, and none said they worked. In our view, a new store should first get a few sales from any source and confirm its tracking, then test TikTok with a set budget and end date.

Are TikTok ads better than Facebook ads for a small business?

Only 3 owners in our sample compared them directly, which is too few to say. One founder selling to businesses found TikTok worse per dollar than Meta. In 49 reports the owner ran Meta, Google or other ads at the same time, and 41 of those said nothing sold on any platform.
