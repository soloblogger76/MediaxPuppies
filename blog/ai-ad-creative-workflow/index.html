We ship 40 ad creatives a week with AI. Here's the actual workflow

Not a prompt list. The actual pipeline we run, including the two steps where AI makes things worse and a human has to take over.

Media Puppies · 17 August 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026

An AI ad creative workflow earns its place for one reason: creative volume is the single biggest lever in paid social right now. Targeting is largely automated; the creative is what decides your cost per customer. The problem has always been that producing thirty genuinely different concepts a month is expensive.

AI changed the economics of that, but not in the way most posts claim. Here is the workflow we actually run, including the parts where it fails.

Step 1 — Mine the raw material (human)

Before anything is generated, someone collects:

  • Every objection from the last 100 sales conversations or WhatsApp threads.
  • Review text — yours and your competitors'. Especially the three-star ones, which are the most honest.
  • Comments under the client's organic posts and under competitor ads.
  • The actual words customers use. Not the words the brand uses.

This step cannot be automated and it determines everything downstream. AI given generic input produces generic ads, quickly and in bulk.

Step 2 — Angles, not headlines (AI-assisted)

The common mistake is asking for fifty headlines. You get fifty rephrasings of one idea. Instead, ask for distinct angles — different reasons a person might care:

  • Price and risk reversal
  • Speed of result
  • A specific objection answered head-on
  • Social proof from a lookalike customer
  • The cost of doing nothing

Feed it the raw material from step one and ask for angles grounded in those exact quotes. Ten strong angles beat fifty headline variants every time.

Step 3 — Scripts and statics (AI draft, human edit)

This is where the time is actually saved. A first-draft script for a 20-second UGC video used to take a strategist 30 minutes. Now it takes four, and the strategist spends the other 26 minutes making it sound like a human being.

The edit is not optional. AI drafts have a recognisable rhythm — balanced sentences, tidy triads, a closing line that summarises. Real people do not speak like that, and in a feed that polish reads as an advertisement instantly.

The AI gets you to a first draft in four minutes. Everything that makes it perform happens after that.

Step 4 — Production (mixed)

Statics and simple variants are genuinely fast now. Video is more nuanced:

  • Creator footage still wins for anything that needs trust — skincare, clinics, anything going into a person's body or home.
  • AI-generated visuals work for abstract concepts, backgrounds, pattern interrupts and B-roll.
  • AI voice is usually a mistake in Indian markets. People clock it, and the ad inherits a scam feeling you cannot argue your way out of.

Where creator content does perform, it is worth whitelisting and running it as paid rather than letting it die after one organic post.

Step 5 — Testing (systematic, not AI)

Volume without a testing framework is just faster waste. Ours is deliberately boring: one variable at a time, enough budget per concept to reach a decision, and a fixed kill rule so nobody argues on Friday about a favourite ad. We wrote the whole thing up in a framework you can run on ₹50,000.

Where an AI ad creative workflow makes things worse

  1. Deciding what won. It will happily rationalise any result you show it. The numbers decide, not a summary of the numbers.
  2. Compliance. AI-written health, finance and income claims get accounts restricted. Every line still goes past a human who knows the policies. Eight years, zero bans, and we intend to keep it that way.
  3. Brand voice over time. Left alone, everything converges on the same neutral tone. Every few weeks we re-read the ads and ask whether they still sound like the client.

What it actually buys you

Not cheaper ads. More attempts. The win rate per concept is roughly what it always was — most ads still lose. What changed is that testing twelve angles a month instead of three means you find the winner sooner, and the compounding on that is where the money is.

Questions people ask

How many ads can an AI ad creative workflow actually produce?

Around forty assets a week for one brand, from roughly six to eight genuinely distinct angles. The volume comes from variation — different openings, formats and crops of the same idea — not from forty separate concepts, which no team produces weekly.

Does AI replace the editor or the strategist?

Neither. It replaces the slow middle: first drafts, resizing, variant scripts and rough cuts. Choosing the angle and judging which cut is worth spending on still needs somebody who knows the customer.

Where does AI output fail?

Faces, hands, product accuracy and anything a customer could check. A generated image of your own product is a liability, not a shortcut. Use AI for backgrounds, concepts and text, and shoot the product itself.

Is AI-generated creative allowed on Meta?

Yes, and Meta supplies its own generative tools. The usual rules still apply: no misleading claims, no implied endorsement by a real person, and disclosure if a synthetic person could be mistaken for a genuine customer.
