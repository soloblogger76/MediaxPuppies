A technical SEO checklist you can actually work through

Not 200 items. The checks that actually change whether Google can find, understand and rank your pages.

Media Puppies · 12 August 2026

Technical SEO audits routinely arrive as a 200-row spreadsheet in which everything is red and nothing is prioritised. Most of it does not matter for a 40-page business site.

Here is what does, in the order worth doing it.

First: can Google see your pages at all?

  1. Check the index count. In Search Console, Pages report. If you have 40 pages and 6 are indexed, nothing else on this list matters yet.
  2. Read the exclusion reasons. Discovered – currently not indexed usually means thin or duplicate content. Crawled – currently not indexed means Google looked and was not impressed.
  3. Check robots.txt is not blocking anything important. This breaks entire sites more often than it should.
  4. Check for stray noindex tags, especially on pages moved from a staging site. A forgotten noindex is the single most common catastrophic finding in audits.
  5. Submit a sitemap and make sure every URL in it returns 200. A sitemap full of redirects and 404s wastes crawl budget and trust.

Second: does each page have one address?

  • One canonical version — http or https, www or not, trailing slash or not. Pick one, redirect the rest.
  • Self-referencing canonical tags on every page.
  • No duplicate content across URLs with parameters. Common on filtered category pages.
  • Case consistency. On case-sensitive hosting, /seo-services/ and /seo/ are different URLs, and one of them is a 404.

Third: speed, on the device people use

Test on a mid-range Android on 4G, not a laptop on office wifi. The usual offenders are unoptimised hero images, too many font families and a head full of analytics tags. Detail in Core Web Vitals on Indian networks.

Fourth: structure Google can follow

  • Every important page reachable in three clicks from the homepage.
  • Internal links with descriptive anchor text. Not click here. This is free and consistently under-used.
  • One H1 per page, describing that page specifically.
  • No orphan pages. If nothing links to it, it will not rank.
  • Breadcrumbs on anything deeper than one level.

Most technical SEO problems are not exotic. They are a noindex tag, a blocked folder, or a page nothing links to.

Fifth: schema that earns something

Add the types that can change how your result looks:

  • LocalBusiness if you have a catchment area, with details matching your Business Profile exactly.
  • FAQPage on pages with genuine questions.
  • Product with price and availability for ecommerce.
  • Article on blog posts.
  • BreadcrumbList so results show a path instead of a URL.

Validate it. Broken schema is worse than no schema.

What to ignore

  • Keyword density. Not a thing.
  • Meta keywords tag. Unused for over a decade.
  • Chasing a perfect audit score. Tools flag issues by pattern, not by impact.
  • Minor heading hierarchy warnings on pages that read fine to a human.

How often

Full pass twice a year. Index coverage monthly. Check after every site change, because that is when noindex tags and broken redirects appear.
