Marketing says leads are up. Sales says they are rubbish. Both are right.

This is not a personality clash. It is what happens when two teams are measured on different events in the same funnel.

Media Puppies · 9 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026

B2B lead quality is the argument running inside every account: marketing is measured on leads and leads are up, sales is measured on revenue and revenue is not. Nobody is lying, and the argument will continue indefinitely until something upstream changes.

Why volume rises and quality falls

Both platforms optimise toward whatever you told them to count. Tell them to count form submissions and they will find people who submit forms — students, researchers, competitors, job seekers, and companies far too small to buy what you sell.

Lower friction accelerates this. Instant lead forms with three prefilled fields produce enormous volume and a hit rate that collapses, because the person barely interacted with anything.

Step one: define qualified, in writing

If marketing and sales have not agreed what makes a lead qualified, there is nothing to optimise toward and no way to settle the argument.

It has to be specific enough to be a field somebody fills in: company size, role, budget range, timeline — whatever genuinely predicts a deal in your business. Not a feeling. A value in the CRM.

Until "qualified" is a field somebody completes, every conversation about lead quality is two people describing different anecdotes.

Step two: send it back to the platforms

Once qualification is recorded, upload it as an offline conversion. The platforms then optimise toward sources that produce qualified leads rather than sources that produce submissions.

Brace the stakeholders: cost per lead will go up. That is the trade you are making. The number that should fall is cost per qualified opportunity, and it usually does — but the first fortnight looks like a regression if nobody was warned.

Step three: use friction deliberately

Friction is a filter, and filters are not automatically good. Which direction to move depends on one question: is your constraint sales capacity or lead supply?

  • Sales has spare capacity. Keep volume. More leads, even at a lower hit rate, are better than idle salespeople.
  • Sales cannot work through what they have. Add friction. A longer form, a qualifying question, a business-email requirement. Fewer, better.

Most teams never ask themselves this and default to "more", which is right about half the time.

Step four: clean out what is not real

A meaningful share of paid B2B form fills are junk. Honeypot fields, rate limiting, email validation and deduplication belong in the funnel from the start. Otherwise your cost per lead is flattered by records nobody can call, and the quality argument gets a layer of noise on top of it.

Step five: fix speed to lead before blaming the ads

This is the uncomfortable one. Contact rates fall steeply with time. A lead called within minutes connects dramatically more often than one called the next morning, because they still remember submitting the form.

Most inbound leads are never contacted more than twice. A defined sequence — several attempts at different times of day, backed by email and where appropriate WhatsApp — routinely lifts contact rate more than any bidding change available to us.

We raise this on the first call, and occasionally clients find it presumptuous. It is also usually true, and it costs nothing to fix.

What to report on once B2B lead quality is fixed

Cost per qualified lead, cost per opportunity, and pipeline generated against spend. Impressions and clicks belong in an appendix. If the weekly report leads with lead volume, you have rebuilt the original argument in a new document.

Questions people ask

Why does lead quality fall as volume rises?

Because the platform is optimising toward whatever you told it to count. Ask for form fills and it finds people who fill in forms, who are not the same people who sign contracts. Volume and quality only move together when the conversion you report is the one that matters.

How do I define a qualified lead?

In writing, agreed by sales and marketing before anything is measured: budget, authority, territory, and whatever else disqualifies someone in practice. Without that definition, "quality" means whatever each side needs it to mean that month.

How do I send qualified status back to the ad platforms?

Offline conversion import from your CRM — Google and Meta both support it. Once the platform sees which leads became opportunities, bidding starts hunting for people who look like those rather than people who look like form-fillers.

Is speed to lead really part of this?

Yes, and it is usually the cheapest fix available. A lead contacted in minutes converts far better than the same lead contacted tomorrow, so a slow follow-up makes good leads look like bad ones and sends the whole argument off in the wrong direction.
