Google Ads management fees: what 29 US, UK and Australian agencies publish

We read the pricing, service and terms pages of 29 agencies that print their own Google Ads fees. Most charge a fixed monthly fee, few state a minimum ad spend, and four show a longer contract in the small print than on the sales page.

Key findings

  • Of 29 agencies that publish their own Google Ads management fees, 18 (62.07%) charge only a fixed monthly fee. 8 publish a percentage of ad spend anywhere on their pages.
  • The median lowest published monthly fee was US$775 in the US (10 agencies, US$199 to US$6,000), £499.50 in the UK (8, £179 to £2,000) and A$600 in Australia (5, A$330 to A$1,995).
  • Published percentages ran from 12% to 15% of spend in the US, 6% to 11% in the UK and 12% to 20% in Australia. 5 agencies use a "greater of" fee or percentage.
  • Only 6 of 29 state a minimum ad spend and 6 print a setup fee amount. 15 say nothing about setup.
  • 8 of the 19 agencies that state a contract length say rolling or no lock-in, and 10 of the 13 that state notice ask for 30 days. 4 show a longer minimum term on another page or in their terms.

Most articles on Google Ads management fees quote ranges with no source. We wanted the figures agencies put their own name to. On 19 November 2026 we read the pricing, service and terms pages of 43 US, UK and Australian agencies and kept the 29 that print a fee for their own Google Ads work. Every value we use has a short quote from the page behind it.

The short answer: most of these agencies charge a fixed monthly fee, and the median lowest published fee was US$775 in the US, £499.50 in the UK and A$600 in Australia. Percentages of spend are now the minority on public pages. The terms that change the real cost, such as minimum ad spend, setup fees and notice, are missing from most pages.

This is the data behind our guides to Google Ads management cost and agency fee models. Those pages explain the models. This one counts what agencies actually publish. We kept every currency as printed and did not convert anything.

Google Ads management fees by model

We expected percent of spend to be common, since it is the model most guides lead with. It was rare. Of the 29 agencies, 11 charge a flat monthly fee and 8 charge a flat fee whose tier is set by an ad spend band. Together that is 18 agencies (62.07%) with no percentage anywhere on their pages, once one flat-fee agency that also offers a 10% option is set aside.

Spend bands blur the line between flat and percentage pricing. The fee is fixed inside a band, and the band moves with spend. One US agency, for example, prints three plans at $199, $349 and $499 a month for click budgets up to $1,000, $2,500 and $5,000. A UK agency prints £360, £450 and £720. The client pays a set number, and it still rises when the budget does.

Three agencies sell Google Ads only inside wider bundles with SEO, content, websites or shared hours. We count their model, and we leave their prices out of the fee medians below, because no part of the bundle price is the Google Ads fee.

The spread is the finding. In the US the highest entry fee is about thirty times the lowest. The cheap end is packaged small business plans. The top end is agencies that state they only take larger accounts: one US agency prices at $3,000 a month for accounts spending $3,000 or more, and another starts at $6,000 a month.

Several entry figures need a footnote, and data.csv carries each one. A UK agency's pricing page says from £780 while its PPC page lists £1,500 as the first tier. Another UK figure, £179, is the minimum fee on a 12-month contract; the rolling minimum is £199. A third UK agency's £500 buys a Display or Performance Max module, and Search alone is £1,500. We used the lowest printed figure each time, so these medians lean low.

On tax, 8 of the 10 UK agencies say something about VAT: most quote prices plus VAT or ex VAT, and one says it is not VAT registered. In Australia 4 of the 8 say plus GST or GST exclusive. The rest do not say. Compare UK and Australian quotes on the same tax basis before you compare the numbers.

Hourly pricing is rare. One UK agency prints £120 to £150 an hour, another sells pay-as-you-go hours at £90, and one US agency scopes custom work from $185 an hour. An Australian agency prices in units of $135, at 2 units per hour of work, and does not print a monthly total.

Percent of spend and "greater of" fees

8 agencies publish a percentage somewhere. We used 7 of them for the ranges below. The eighth, a UK agency, says its fee is 30% "of your monthly payment" and its own example does not add up, so we report it apart.

The "greater of" structure is where the published percentage misleads most. HigherVisibility's page states the greater of $800 per month or 15% of ad spend. Stackmatix states $6,000 a month or 15% of media spend, whichever is greater. WebFX lists a flat $750 plan for small budgets, then $975 or 15% and $4,500 or 12%, whichever is higher. At a small budget the floor is the real price, and the percentage only bites once spend is large.

Sayu in the UK works the other way round. Its page prices at 11% of spend on a rolling contract, with a minimum fee of £199 and a maximum of £3,500 a month. On 12 months the rate falls from 10% to 6% as spend grows. A cap is rare in this sample, and it matters to any account that scales.

Two Australian agencies use a step instead. Perth Digital Edge states a $600 monthly fee, or 20% of ad spend once spend passes $3,000 a month. Another prints $495 plus GST up to $3,000 of spend, then moves to a percentage it does not publish. In both, a client crossing the threshold changes fee model, so ask for the figure at the budget you plan to reach.

Setup. 6 of 29 print a setup amount, 8 say there is none, and 15 say nothing. The US$5,800 figure is a first-month "initial optimization" charge on a top plan, which is how some agencies bill setup without the word. One US agency waives a $500 setup fee on a six-month term. One "zero setup fees" line is marked as a limited time offer.

Minimum ad spend. Only 6 of 29 state one, and they mean different things. Two US agencies state a hard minimum ($1,500 and $3,000 a month). One prints a lowest plan band starting at $100. A UK agency recommends £2,000 a month for B2B, another says there is no minimum, and an Australian agency's stated minimum contradicts its own lowest tier. More pages describe a typical budget than a minimum: £10,000 a month as a "typical fit" at one UK agency, and about A$1,500 a month at two Australian ones.

Contract. 19 agencies state a minimum term on their sales pages. 8 say rolling or no lock-in, 7 say three months, 3 say six and 1 says twelve. 10 say nothing.

Then read the terms. For 4 agencies, the terms page or another page shows a longer commitment than the sales page. A UK agency's PPC page says it rolls monthly after 3 months, while its terms say PPC retainers run for a minimum of 6. An Australian page says no lock in contracts, while its terms let the client terminate only after 3 months. Another Australian agency advertises no lock-in, and its terms add a cancellation fee of one month's fee if the term is under 3 months. A US agency's local plans are month to month, and its services page says three months minimum for custom PPC.

Notice. 13 agencies state a notice period. 10 ask for 30 days. The others ask for 7, 28 and 60 days; the 60 days applies to non-renewal of a 12-month contract that renews itself.

How to read an agency's fee page (our view)

After reading 29 of these pages, this is the order we would read any agency's pricing in, ours included.

  1. Find the fee at your own budget. Most entry figures are the smallest plan. Work out which band or formula your current spend falls into, and the one you expect in six months.
  2. Look for a floor and a cap. In a "greater of" deal the floor is the price at small budgets. A cap, like the one on Sayu's page, protects you as spend grows. Most percentage pages print a floor and no cap.
  3. Check what a threshold does. If the fee changes model above a spend level, ask for the exact figure on the other side of it.
  4. Read the terms page as well as the sales page. In this sample, "no lock-in" sat beside a 3-month clause more than once. The terms decide.
  5. Ask about setup in writing. More than half the pages say nothing. A first-month charge can be a setup fee by another name.
  6. Compare on the same tax basis and currency. A UK fee plus VAT and an Australian fee plus GST are not the same number as a US list price, and none of them should be converted for a quick ranking.
  7. Separate the Google Ads fee from a bundle. If PPC only comes with SEO or a website, ask what the Google Ads part costs alone.

Our view on percent of spend: it is fine when there is a cap and the floor fits your budget. The public pages suggest buyers have pushed agencies toward flat and banded fees, and we think that is healthy because the fee no longer rises every time you add budget. Banded fees still rise, so the band edges are worth asking about.

Questions about published Google Ads management fees

How much are Google Ads management fees per month?

Among 29 agencies that published their own fees on 19 November 2026, the median lowest monthly fee was US$775 in the US, £499.50 in the UK and A$600 in Australia. Ranges were wide: US$199 to US$6,000, £179 to £2,000 and A$330 to A$1,995.

What percentage of ad spend do agencies charge for Google Ads?

Only 8 of the 29 agencies published a percentage. The ranges we could use were 12% to 15% in the US, 6% to 11% in the UK and 12% to 20% in Australia. Most of these sit inside a "greater of" deal with a monthly floor.

What does "greater of" mean in a Google Ads management fee?

You pay whichever is higher: a fixed monthly fee or a percentage of spend. 5 agencies in our sample use it. At small budgets the fixed floor is the price you pay, so check it before the percentage.

Do Google Ads agencies charge a setup fee?

6 of 29 printed a setup amount and 8 said there was none. The other 15 did not say. Printed amounts ran from US$500 to US$5,800, £250, and A$500 to A$2,000, and one first-month charge worked as a setup fee.

Is there a minimum contract for Google Ads management?

Of the 19 agencies that stated one on their sales page, 8 said rolling or no lock-in and 7 said three months. 4 agencies showed a longer minimum in their terms or on another page, so read both.
