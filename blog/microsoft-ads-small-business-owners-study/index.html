Do Microsoft Ads (Bing Ads) work for small business? We coded 23 owners' own reports

We searched public forums for small business owners describing their own Bing and Microsoft ads and found 23 reports. The sample is small, and it says something consistent: fewer clicks than Google, buyers who convert as well, and tracking that breaks.

  • We found only 23 owner reports about their own Microsoft or Bing ads (19 on Hacker News, 4 on the Shopify Community), short of the 40 we aimed for. 9 said it worked, 3 were mixed, 4 said it did not work and 7 could not tell.
  • Of the 16 owners who gave a clear verdict, 9 (56%) said Microsoft Ads worked.

Do Microsoft Ads work for small business? In the 23 owner reports we could find in public forums, 9 owners (39%) said their Bing or Microsoft ads worked, 3 had mixed results, 4 said no and 7 could not tell. Of the 6 who compared conversion with Google Ads, none said Bing converted worse.

This is the third study in a series. Our Google Ads owner study coded 89 reports and our Facebook ads owner study coded 100. We used the same sources and rules here and aimed for at least 40 reports on Microsoft Ads. We found 23. That gap is a finding in itself: small business owners write about Microsoft Ads far less than about Google or Meta, and the people who run it mostly stay quiet. We run search accounts ourselves and label our opinions. None of our own client or account data is in here.

Where the 23 reports come from, and why there are so few

On 23 November 2026 we searched Hacker News and the Shopify Community, two public forums that load without a login. Reddit blocked plain requests two days earlier, so we did not try it, and we did not work around any block.

On Hacker News we ran 52 searches such as "bing ads", "microsoft advertising" and "bing ads cheaper", which returned 10,693 comments. A text filter cut those to 98 that mention Bing or Microsoft ads alongside a first-person phrase and a result, and we read all of them. Most were about Windows, Bing search or ads people saw. 19 were owners describing their own ads. On the Shopify Community, 81 topics had Bing or Microsoft in the title. Most were about Bing search rankings, Microsoft 365 or tag set-up questions with no ads running yet. 4 were owners with ads running.

So read this as 23 owners talking, with all the limits that brings. 15 of the 23 wrote before 2020, when the product was called Bing Ads or adCenter. None gave a monthly spend. Only 1 named a country, the US. We expected a small, negative sample, because people post when something breaks. The sample is small. It is less negative than we expected.

Leave out the 7 who could not tell and 9 of the remaining 16 owners (56%) said Microsoft Ads worked. The groups are small and mostly older, so we would not rank Microsoft against Google on this. It does suggest that owners who stuck with Microsoft Ads long enough to write about it were often happy with it.

Most of the winners were software and web businesses: 6 of the 12 software owners said it worked. A software founder wrote that "the Bing search ads I've run for my software product convert better than my AdWords search ads." An HR software company for small firms said its buyers were office workers on Windows machines, where Bing is the default search engine, and that it still advertised there.

Fewer clicks, similar buyers. Low volume was the most common comparison. 6 owners said Microsoft brought less traffic than Google. Half of those 6 also said the traffic converted as well or better. An internet startup founder summed it up: it "works about as well as Google for me, there's just less volume available." That is the trade-off in one line. In our view, Bing can be a good second channel. It rarely replaces Google.

Nobody said it converted worse. 6 owners compared conversion directly: 3 said better and 3 said about the same. With 6 people this proves little, and owners who saw worse results may simply have stopped and said nothing. Still, it is the opposite of the reputation Bing traffic has among some marketers.

Cheaper clicks came up less than we expected. Only 2 owners said clicks cost less than on Google. In our view, the usual advice that Bing is the cheap option gets more weight than these owners gave it.

Recent reports are mostly about tracking

The older reports describe results. Before 2020, 8 of 15 owners said Microsoft Ads worked and 2 could not tell. From 2020 on, 1 of 8 said it worked and 5 could not tell. In our view, the likelier cause is that owners lost the ability to see what the ads did.

All 4 Shopify reports, from 2024 and 2025, were owners whose Microsoft Ads conversions stopped making sense. One saw purchases reach Microsoft with the right values while the campaign still showed zero conversions. Another found the Microsoft conversion goal kept reverting to an old thank-you page address after Shopify changed its checkout. A third had Microsoft claiming sales that had come from Google Ads. None of them could say whether Microsoft Ads was paying.

That matches what we saw from the other side. In our PPC agency websites study, 20 agencies listed Microsoft Ads as a service and none of those 20 had the Microsoft UET tag on its own home page. If the agencies selling it skip the tag, it is easy to see why small stores struggle with it. Our guide to Shopify customer events tracking covers the newer checkout setup these owners were fighting.

What went wrong for the owners who said no

Only 7 reports were failures or mixed, so each reason here is one or two people.

  • Too little traffic (3). A small business owner who tried to move off Google wrote: "I tried switching to bing ads, Facebook ads, but its just not possible. No one use bing."
  • Cost more than it returned (2). A web app maker wrote: "I tried Microsofts Bing ads but that was just an expensive experience with no conversion."
  • The ad platform was hard to use (1). An owner who had run Google Ads for years found Microsoft's tools too far behind to keep going. One more owner, a few days into a test, called the clicks cheaper and the tools poor, and could not yet tell if it paid.
  • Account suspended (1). A US software company had its account suspended for "suspicious activity" a week into a small test that was starting to convert.

One failure named no reason: a desktop software seller who said only that he had given up on Bing PPC.

What we would change (our view)

In our view, Microsoft Ads is a second search channel for a small business that already has Google Ads working. It is rarely a first one. The owners who did well had a product people search for, buyers who sit at work computers, and campaigns already proven on Google. The owners who did badly expected Google-sized traffic, or could not see their conversions. These are the checks we would run first:

  1. Is Google Ads profitable already? Import your best Google search campaigns into Microsoft Ads rather than starting from scratch. Fix Google first if it is losing money. Our Google Ads for small business guide covers that.
  2. Does the UET tag fire on the real thank-you page? Place a test order and check the conversion appears against the right campaign. Shopify's newer checkout broke this for every Shopify owner in our sample.
  3. Do Microsoft and Google both claim the same sale? Add up the two platforms' conversions for a week and compare with your orders.
  4. Are your buyers likely to use Bing? Office workers, B2B buyers and older searchers on Windows are where owners saw the best results.
  5. Will the volume ever matter? Expect a fraction of your Google clicks. Judge Microsoft Ads on cost per sale, and do not expect it to replace Google.

If you want a second pair of eyes, our Google Ads agency page explains how we run search accounts, and our free ad account audit starts with the tracking checks above.

For agencies serving small businesses, these owners tell a simple story. Microsoft Ads tends to convert, the volume is small, and it is only worth the effort once tracking is set up properly.

This study copies the method of our Google Ads and Facebook ads owner studies from 21 and 22 November 2026. Reddit blocked plain requests on 21 November, so we did not try it, and we did not work around any block, CAPTCHA or login. We used two sources that are readable without a login: Hacker News, through its public search API, and the Shopify Community, through its public JSON.

On Hacker News we ran 52 comment searches such as "bing ads", "microsoft ads", "microsoft advertising", "bing ppc", "bing ads cheaper", "bing ads small business" and "adcenter", kept the 10,693 comments they returned, and filtered to 98 that name Bing or Microsoft ads near a first-person phrase and a money or results word. We read all 98. We also searched 863 Ask HN and Show HN posts. On the Shopify Community we ran 20 searches, found 270 topics, downloaded the 81 with Bing or Microsoft in the title and read them. Most were about Bing search rankings, Microsoft 365, Clarity or tag set-up, with no ad result. Some search pages returned a rate-limit error and were not retried.

A report counts when a person describes Microsoft or Bing ads for their own business and says something about the result. We excluded agencies and PPC managers describing client accounts, employees, job ads, publishers, consumers talking about ads they saw, second-hand stories, a promotional post, set-up questions where no ads had run, unpaid Microsoft listings, bans with no ad result, repeat posts by the same person, and anyone identifiably outside the US, UK, Canada and Australia. Shopify threads where ads were running but conversions could not be matched to them were coded "could not tell". Each report was coded once, by one person.

Limits. 23 reports is a small sample, and every split in this article is a handful of people. Everything is self-reported and unchecked. Hacker News leans toward software founders and older posts: 15 of the 23 reports predate 2020, when the platform was still called Bing Ads or adCenter. No owner gave a monthly spend, and only 4 said how long they ran the ads. No usernames are stored; the data file holds thread and post links only.

Questions about Microsoft Ads for small business

Do Microsoft Ads work for small business?

In the 23 owner reports we found, 9 said Microsoft Ads (Bing Ads) worked, 3 had mixed results, 4 said they did not work and 7 could not tell. Of the 16 with a clear verdict, 9 (56%) said it worked. The sample is small and mostly older Hacker News posts.

Are Bing ads cheaper than Google Ads?

Sometimes, but it came up less than you might expect. Only 2 of the 12 owners who compared the platforms said Bing clicks were cheaper. The most common comparison was volume: 6 of 12 said Microsoft brought less traffic than Google.

Does Bing traffic convert as well as Google?

In this sample, yes. 6 owners compared conversion directly: 3 said Bing converted better than Google, 3 said about the same and 0 said worse. With 6 people that is a small pattern and proves little.

Why can't I see my Microsoft Ads conversions on Shopify?

All 4 Shopify owners in our sample hit this in 2024 or 2025. Shopify's newer checkout changed the thank-you page address and moved tags into a sandbox, so the UET tag either stopped firing or fired without matching the click. Check with a test order that the conversion lands on the right campaign.

Should a small business start with Microsoft Ads or Google Ads?

In our view, Google Ads first. Microsoft Ads works best as a second search channel: import campaigns that already work on Google, check the UET tag, and judge it on cost per sale. Owners who expected Google-sized traffic were the ones who gave up.
