The plumbing, Shopify-specific

Where Shopify tracking quietly goes wrong.

These are the four failures we find most often, and every one of them makes the numbers look better or worse than reality while everything appears connected.

Customer Events and the sandbox

Shopify moved custom tracking into Customer Events, which runs pixels in a sandboxed context rather than letting arbitrary script onto the checkout. Older hand-installed tracking that used to work silently stopped, and a lot of stores never noticed because the sales channel app kept firing something. We verify what is actually being sent from checkout rather than trusting the app’s own status light.

Double-counted purchases

A theme-level pixel plus the sales channel app plus a tag manager container is three sources for one purchase event. Without matching event IDs they do not deduplicate, your conversion count inflates, your ROAS looks excellent and you scale into a loss. This is the single most common thing we find in a Shopify account that "used to work".

Value sent in the wrong currency

With Shopify Markets, a customer in Australia pays in AUD while your store currency is USD. If the purchase value is sent in the presentment currency but the platform assumes your account currency, every ROAS figure for that market is wrong — sometimes flattering, sometimes disastrous. It is quick to check and almost nobody does.

Conversions API on the Shopify path

Server-side events matter more on Shopify than on most platforms because so much of the buying happens in-app and on Safari. We set up CAPI with proper event deduplication and then look at event match quality, because a CAPI that is sending events with no matchable customer data is doing very little for you.

Feed and Shopping

The default feed is a starting point, not a strategy.

Whatever Shopify exports on day one is generic by design. On Google Shopping and Performance Max, that export is the single biggest lever you have.

Titles that match how people search

Shopify product titles are written for your storefront — short, brand-led, often clever. Shopping titles need the attributes people type: brand, product type, material, colour, size, for whom. Rewriting titles through feed rules, without touching what customers see on the site, is frequently the cheapest performance win available in the first month.

Custom labels for margin and stock

Not every product deserves the same bid. Custom labels let you separate high margin from low, bestsellers from long tail, and in-stock from nearly gone. Without them Performance Max will happily spend your budget on the item with the best conversion rate and the worst margin.

Variants, GTINs and disapprovals

Variant-level feed problems and missing identifiers cause quiet partial disapprovals — the campaign runs, just without half your catalogue. We audit Merchant Center diagnostics properly rather than looking at the account-level status.

After the first order

What Shopify makes easy to see, and what it hides.

The store reports revenue. Your acquisition decisions need contribution, repeat rate and payback, and those take a little assembly.

Post-purchase and AOV mechanics

Post-purchase upsells, bundles and free-shipping thresholds change your average order value, which changes what you can afford to pay for a click. These are store-side changes, not media changes, and they are often a faster route to a workable CAC than anything we can do in the ad account. We will tell you when that is the case even though it is not work you are paying us for.

Returns, by product

Apparel returns concentrate in specific SKUs and sizes. A product with a high return rate can be the star of your ROAS report and a net loss. Feeding return data back into which products get promoted is unglamorous and it moves profit more than bid tweaks.

Repeat purchase and the payback window

Shopify has the order history to tell you the real repeat rate and interval per cohort. Once you know it, you can decide deliberately how long you are prepared to wait to recover acquisition cost, instead of capping spend at first-order break-even out of nervousness.

Straight answers.

Do we need Shopify Plus?

No. Most of what matters — Customer Events, Markets, feed rules, CAPI — is available without Plus. Plus helps with checkout customisation and scripts, but it is not a prerequisite for the account being set up correctly.

Is the Shopify Meta sales channel app enough?

It is enough to start and not enough to trust. It gets a pixel firing quickly, which is why so many stores never audit it. We check event deduplication, match quality and currency handling, which the app's status light does not tell you about.

We sell in several currencies through Markets. Does that break anything?

It breaks reporting more often than it breaks selling. The common fault is purchase value reported in the customer's currency while the ad platform assumes yours, which makes some markets look far better or worse than they are. It is one of the first things we check.

Can you work with our theme developer?

Yes, and we would rather brief them than touch a live theme ourselves. Tracking changes get written as a spec with the exact events, parameters and firing conditions, so your developer can implement and you have a record of it.

Will you touch our store or just the ads?

Just the ads, unless you ask. But if the bottleneck is on the store — page speed, a checkout leak, an offer that does not convert — we will say so in the audit. It costs us nothing to tell you and it is usually the highest-value thing in the document.

Do you run email and SMS too?

No. We run paid acquisition. Retention is a different discipline and we would rather tell you to hire someone good at it than pretend it is the same job.
