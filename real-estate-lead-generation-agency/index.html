Qualification

Cost per lead is a vanity metric. Cost per site visit is the business.

It is easy to generate property enquiries cheaply and almost impossible to turn most of them into someone standing in the flat. Every decision below follows from measuring the walk-in rather than the form fill.

Price and locality stated in the creative

The single highest-leverage change in most real estate accounts is putting the number in the ad. "3BHK from ₹1.4 Cr, Sector 150" removes everyone who cannot buy and everyone who will not travel. Volume falls, site visits rise, and your sales team stops dreading the lead list.

Site visits, fed back as the conversion

If you optimise for form fills, the platform finds form-fillers. We send qualified status and booked site visits back from your CRM so bidding hunts for people who resemble buyers who turned up. This is most of what the tracking work is for, and skipping it is why real estate accounts plateau.

Channel partner and portal leads, separated

Enquiries from 99acres, MagicBricks and Housing behave nothing like your own paid traffic, and mixing them in one report hides which channel is actually producing bookings. We keep them separate so the comparison is cost per site visit, channel by channel.

RERA details on every asset

Indian property advertising has to carry the project RERA registration number and the authority website, and platforms reject creative that omits them. We build it into the templates so approval is not a weekly argument. Your legal team signs off the wording; we make sure it is present on every asset.

Structure

One project per campaign, one micro-market per ad set.

Real estate accounts fail structurally more often than creatively. These are the three that cost the most.

Radius targeting versus where buyers actually come from

A radius drawn around the project site catches the people already living there, who are rarely the buyers. Demand for a Noida project often sits in Delhi and Ghaziabad, and for a Gurgaon one in Dwarka. We target where the money comes from, not where the building is.

Investors and end-users are different buyers

An investor cares about rental yield, possession date and resale; a family cares about schools, commute and the floor plan. They want different creative, different landing pages and often different price framing. Mixed into one campaign, each message dilutes the other.

Ready-to-move and under-construction, split

Possession status is the biggest objection in the category and it splits the audience cleanly. Under-construction buyers need construction progress and the developer’s delivery record; ready-to-move buyers need the walkthrough and the paperwork. One campaign cannot answer both, and the one that tries answers neither.

Follow-up

The first call wins. Everything else is second.

Property enquiries have the shortest useful life of almost any category. The person enquiring is comparing three projects this evening, and the comparison is largely decided by who reached them first.

Instant WhatsApp, then a human within minutes

An automatic WhatsApp with the floor plan, price band and location pin goes out on submission — not "our team will call you". A human follows within minutes. A channel partner who calls in five minutes beats a better project that calls tomorrow, and that is the whole competitive dynamic here.

Launches and festive windows drive the auction

Demand spikes on launch announcements, on Navratri and Diwali, and at financial year end when buyers are chasing deductions. Budget held back for those windows earns far more than the same money spread evenly across a flat month.

The quiet weeks are for re-engagement

Most property buyers take months to decide, so the enquiry that went cold in March is often the booking in July. Quiet weeks are for working that list with construction updates and price revisions, which costs almost nothing compared with buying the lead again.

Straight answers.

What does a real estate lead generation agency actually do?

Runs paid campaigns that produce site visits rather than form fills. In practice that means qualifying buyers inside the ad with price and locality, feeding visit data back to Google and Meta so bidding targets people who turn up, and building the follow-up that reaches an enquiry within minutes.

How do you measure real estate lead generation?

Cost per site visit, and then cost per booking. Cost per lead is the number most agencies report and the one least connected to revenue — a cheap lead that never walks in costs more than an expensive one that does.

Can you guarantee a cost per lead?

No. In this category a guaranteed cheap lead almost always means a form fill from someone browsing prices with no budget and no intention this year. We would rather commit to the qualification and show you the walk-in rate.

Do you handle RERA compliance in the ads?

We build the registration number and authority website into the creative templates so campaigns are not rejected and nothing goes live without them. The wording itself should be approved by your own legal team — we make sure it is present, consistently, on every asset.

Should we advertise on 99acres and MagicBricks as well?

Often yes, but keep the reporting separate. Portal leads are sold to several competitors at once and behave nothing like your own paid traffic, so blending them into one cost per lead hides which channel is actually producing bookings.

How long before we see site visits?

Enquiries within the first fortnight, meaningful site visit data from roughly the second month, and a defensible read on cost per booking after about ninety days. Property has a long consideration cycle, so anyone promising a faster verdict is reading noise.
