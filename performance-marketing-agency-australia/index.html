State-level structure, not a national blur

Australia is five real markets and a lot of empty map. Campaigns get built so Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide are separable, because CPMs and conversion rates differ enough between them that a single national number hides the decision you need to make.

Where Australian accounts get into trouble

The TGA is stricter than most advertisers expect.

Australia regulates advertising harder than the US in the categories that spend most on direct response: health, supplements, cosmetic procedures and anything making a therapeutic claim.

Therapeutic goods advertising

If your product or service makes a health claim, the Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code applies. Prescription medicines cannot be advertised to the public at all. Testimonials, before-and-after imagery and claims about outcomes are all constrained, and the constraints differ from what the same brand can say in the US. Supplement, clinic and cosmetic brands that lift their US creative wholesale get disapproved, and repeat disapprovals put the ad account at risk.

Australian Consumer Law

Misleading or deceptive conduct is a statutory prohibition with real penalties, and the ACCC has been active on pricing presentation and on claims that cannot be substantiated. "Was/now" pricing, free-trial mechanics and subscription cancellation flows are all live enforcement areas. Your landing page is part of the advertisement.

The under-16 social media rules

Australia now restricts under-16s from holding accounts on the major social platforms. For advertisers this changes the addressable audience and the reliability of age-based reach estimates, and it matters most to brands whose buyer is a teenager or whose creative leans on teen culture. It is a real change to the media landscape and worth checking against your own audience data rather than assuming it does not apply.

Spam Act consent for email and SMS

Commercial electronic messages need consent, accurate sender identification and a working unsubscribe. If your paid funnel ends in an SMS sequence, the consent you collect on the ad form has to actually cover it.

Seasonality and money

An inverted calendar and a financial year that ends in June.

Two things reliably catch northern-hemisphere brands and their agencies out in Australia.

Summer is Christmas

December is peak retail and peak holiday at the same time. January is slow for B2B and strong for consumer. A media plan copied from a UK or US calendar will have your budget in the wrong months, and the creative will show swimming when you are selling coats.

End of financial year

The Australian financial year ends on 30 June, and the June run-up is a genuine demand spike for anything a business can expense or depreciate — equipment, software, vehicles, professional services. For B2B advertisers it is frequently the single most valuable month of the year and it is nowhere on a northern calendar.
