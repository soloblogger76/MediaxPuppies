The real problem

Volume is a solved problem. Quality is the job.

Every B2B account we inherit has the same argument running inside it: marketing says leads are up, sales says the leads are rubbish. Both are usually right, and the cause is measurable.

Define qualified before you buy anything

If marketing and sales have not agreed in writing what makes a lead qualified, you cannot optimise and you cannot settle the argument. We start by pinning that down — company size, role, budget, timeline, whatever genuinely predicts a deal in your business — and making it a field in the CRM that someone fills in.

Feed outcomes back into the bidding

Once qualification is recorded, it goes back to Google and Meta as an offline conversion. The platforms then optimise toward the sources, audiences and keywords that produce qualified leads instead of the ones that produce the most submissions. This usually raises your cost per lead and lowers your cost per opportunity, which is the trade you want and which you should brace your stakeholders for.

Friction as a filter, used deliberately

Instant lead forms with three prefilled fields produce enormous volume and terrible quality. A longer form, a qualifying question, or asking for a company email will cut submissions and raise the hit rate. Which way to move depends entirely on whether your sales capacity or your lead supply is the constraint — we ask that first rather than defaulting to more.

Spam, bots and the same person four times

A meaningful share of paid B2B form fills are junk. Honeypots, rate limiting, email validation and deduplication belong in the funnel from day one, otherwise your cost per lead is flattered by records nobody can call.

Where the budget goes

Intent first, then everything else.

B2B budgets get wasted by spreading across channels evenly. The order of priority is usually not in dispute once you look at it plainly.

Google search: capture the demand that exists

People searching for your category, your competitors, or "best X for Y" have already decided they have a problem. This is almost always the first place a B2B budget should go, and it is where the most obvious waste hides — broad match without a serious negative list, brand and non-brand mixed in one report, and a search terms report nobody has read this quarter.

LinkedIn: when the job title is the targeting

Expensive per click and worth it when your buyer is defined by role, seniority or company size. It earns its cost for high-value enterprise motions and rarely for anything with a small contract value. We will tell you which you are.

Meta: demand creation and staying present

Cheap reach, weak native targeting for B2B, and much better than its reputation when used for education and retargeting across a long consideration window. It is a poor first channel for enterprise B2B and a good second one.

After the click

The thing that beats every optimisation we can make.

We will say this on the first call and repeat it: how fast and how persistently you follow up affects your results more than anything happening in the ad account.

Minutes, not days

Contact rates on inbound leads fall steeply with time. A lead called within a few minutes of submitting connects dramatically more often than one called the next morning, because you are reaching them while they still remember doing it. If your process cannot manage that, automating an instant acknowledgement and a booking link is the cheapest improvement available to you.

Attempts, not an attempt

Most inbound leads are never contacted more than twice. A defined sequence — several call attempts across different times of day, backed by email and where appropriate WhatsApp — routinely lifts contact rate more than any bidding change we could make.

Tell us what happened

We need disposition data back: contacted, qualified, disqualified and why. Without it we are optimising blind and you are paying us to guess. With it, the account improves every month on its own.

Straight answers.

Will my cost per lead go up?

Very likely, and that is usually the point. Optimising toward qualified leads means buying fewer, better ones. The number that should fall is cost per qualified opportunity. If nobody has prepared your board for that shift, tell us and we will put it in writing before we make the change.

What if our CRM data is a mess?

Then that is month one. There is no honest way to optimise B2B paid media without outcome data, and any agency that skips it is selling you form fills.

Do you do cold outbound or email lists?

No. We run paid media. We do not buy lists, scrape contacts or run cold email, and we would not advise it in most of the markets we serve.

Can you work with our sales team directly?

We prefer it. A fortnightly fifteen minutes with whoever is actually calling the leads is worth more than an hour with marketing, because they know which sources produce real conversations.

How quickly will we see a difference?

Waste comes out in weeks one and two. Quality changes take longer, because the feedback loop runs at the speed of your sales cycle. Judge it fairly at 90 days.

What do you report on?

Cost per qualified lead, cost per opportunity, and pipeline generated against spend, with the decisions we made and why. Impressions and clicks are in the appendix, where they belong.
