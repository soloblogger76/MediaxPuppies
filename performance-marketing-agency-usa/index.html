What actually trips US accounts up

The US platform rules that cost people money.

Most of the damage we see in US accounts is not bad creative. It is a rule nobody read, applied after the fact, that wipes out a month of learning.

Special Ad Categories

If you advertise housing, employment, credit, or social and political issues, Meta strips most of your targeting. No age or gender selection, no detailed interest targeting in the usual sense, and location targeting forced to a wide radius rather than a ZIP code. Real estate, recruitment, lending and insurance accounts get built completely differently because of it — and if you declare the category late, Meta resets your learning.

Advertiser verification and Local Services Ads

Google requires advertiser identity verification, and it will pause an unverified account. If you are a home services business, Google Local Services Ads sit above the search ads and need licence and background checks before you can run them at all. That verification work is slow, so it belongs at the start of an engagement, not in month three when someone notices the competitor above you.

State privacy law and consent signals

California, and a growing list of other states, give people the right to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information, and California requires you to honour the Global Privacy Control browser signal. In practice this means your consent banner, your pixel and your Conversions API have to agree with each other. When they do not, you get under-reported conversions and a compliance exposure at the same time.

Consent language on lead forms

If leads are going to be called or texted, the consent wording sitting next to your submit button matters, and the rules here move. We build the form so the disclosure is visible and logged with the lead, and we tell you to have your own counsel approve the wording. We are media buyers, not your lawyers, and an agency that tells you otherwise is a risk.

Straight answers.

Why would a US brand hire an agency in India?

Cost and seniority. A US agency at our fee level gives you a junior buyer sharing you with nine other accounts. At the same fee we put a senior buyer on it, because our cost base is Indian and yours is not. That is the whole arbitrage, and we would rather state it plainly than dress it up as a global delivery model.

Do you have a US office or US staff?

No. We are one team in Noida, India. Everyone who touches your account is in that room. Agencies that front a US salesperson and quietly deliver from overseas are doing the same thing we do, with a markup for the pretence.

What hours will I actually be able to reach you?

Our US-account buyers work 2pm to 11pm India time, which is 9am to 1pm Eastern and 7am to 10am Pacific. Slack or WhatsApp during that window gets a same-hour reply. Outside it you get a reply the next working morning, and anything genuinely on fire has an escalation route that reaches someone the same day.

Do you understand the US market or just the platforms?

Both. We know the platforms and the US rule surface in detail — special ad categories, advertiser verification, state privacy signals, feed and shopping mechanics. On positioning and cultural reference we work with your team rather than around it: you know your buyer, we know what the auction rewards. That combination produces sharper creative than either side writes alone, and you can judge it on the audit before committing to anything.

How do you charge?

A monthly retainer, month to month, no lock-in, invoiced in dollars. The fee depends on spend level and how many channels are running. We never take a percentage of ad spend — it pays us to spend more of your money, which is exactly the wrong incentive.

Do you guarantee a ROAS or a cost per lead?

No. Anyone quoting you a number before seeing your margins, your offer and your historical data is guessing and building themselves an excuse. What we commit to is the work, a weekly report with decisions in it, and telling you early if this is not going to work.
