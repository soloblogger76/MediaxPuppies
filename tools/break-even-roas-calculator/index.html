Why the headline ROAS lies

“We're doing 3x” means nothing on its own. A brand with a 60% contribution margin is profitable at 1.7x. A brand at 22% is still losing money at 4x. The same number is a triumph for one and a slow death for the other.

What this calculator accounts for

  • Cost of goods — what the product actually costs you landed.
  • Shipping and packing on every delivered order.
  • Payment and platform fees, typically 2–3% in India.
  • Returns and RTO — the big one. A returned order earns nothing and costs shipping in both directions, so it is charged at twice the shipping cost here.

The RTO problem

Cash on delivery makes RTO the single largest hidden cost for Indian D2C. A brand running 30% RTO on COD and 4% on prepaid has two completely different businesses inside one dashboard. Work out this calculation separately for each payment method before you decide the ads are failing.

Blended, not platform-reported

Ad platforms count conversions they believe they influenced, so the numbers in Meta and Google together usually exceed your actual revenue. Judge the business on blended ROAS — total revenue divided by total ad spend — and use platform ROAS only to compare campaigns against each other.

Questions

What is contribution margin?+

What is left from an order after every variable cost of fulfilling it, but before fixed costs and advertising. It is the money available to pay for ads and overheads.

Should I include GST?+

Work in the figures you actually keep. If your price includes GST that you remit, use the ex-GST amount as order value.

Why is my target margin unreachable?+

Because the margin you want is larger than the margin the product leaves you. The fix is pricing, product cost or RTO — not the ad account.

Does this work for services?+

Roughly. Set cost of goods to your delivery cost, shipping and RTO to zero, and the break-even figure is still correct.
