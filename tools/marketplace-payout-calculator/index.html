Why this asks for your rates instead of supplying them

Referral fees on Indian marketplaces vary by category, by price band and by fulfilment method, and they get revised. Any calculator that hardcodes “Amazon charges X%” is wrong for most sellers and stale within a quarter. Your current rates are in your seller panel — use those.

The fees people forget

  • Closing fee — a flat per-order charge that hurts most on low-value items.
  • Weight-band shipping. Going one gram over a slab can cost more than the packaging.
  • Returns. The single largest variable in apparel and footwear, and the one most sellers exclude from their spreadsheet.
  • Sponsored ads. If you run them to stay visible, they are a cost of the channel, not a separate marketing line.
  • Payment cycle. Not a fee, but money locked for a fortnight is working capital you are financing.

The number that actually matters

Profit per delivered order flatters every marketplace. Profit per order placed — after returns — is what lands in your bank. The gap between those two is the whole argument for or against a channel.

The break-even return rate is the most useful output here. It tells you how much room you have before the channel stops paying, which is a far more practical thing to monitor than margin.

Comparing against your own site

Run the same product through the break-even ROAS calculator for your own website. Marketplaces take a large, certain cut; your own site costs you advertising, which is variable. Whichever leaves more per order placed is where the next unit of effort belongs.

Questions

Where do I find my commission rate?+

In the fee schedule inside your seller panel, under your specific category and price band. Do not use a blog post — the rates are revised.

Should I include GST here?+

Work in the amounts you actually keep. If you are registered and claiming input credit, use ex-GST figures throughout so the comparison is consistent.

Does this cover FBA-style fulfilment?+

Yes — put the marketplace’s fulfilment charge into the shipping field and set your own pick-and-pack to zero.

Why is my break-even return rate so low?+

Because the profit on a delivered order is small relative to what a return costs. That is normal on thin-margin categories and it is exactly why return rate is the metric to watch.
