The comparison people actually make

Salary versus retainer. It is the wrong one, and it almost always makes hiring look better than it is, because a salary is the smallest part of what an employee costs.

What gets left out

  • Employer on-costs. Benefits and statutory contributions add meaningfully to the headline figure.
  • Tools. A media buyer needs reporting, competitor research, call tracking and creative software. An agency already owns these and spreads them across clients.
  • Recruitment. Either a fee or weeks of your own time. Real money either way.
  • Ramp-up. A good hire takes a quarter to be fully productive. You pay for twelve months and get nine.
  • Single point of failure. One person cannot cover their own leave, illness or resignation. When they go, the account knowledge goes with them.

What the numbers usually show

In year one an agency is normally cheaper. From year two the employee is cheaper, provided they stay and provided the role is genuinely full-time. The honest decision therefore turns on two questions: is there enough work to fill a full-time role, and how confident are you of retention in a market where good media buyers are actively recruited?

The hybrid most teams land on: hire the strategist, outsource the execution. It is also the cheapest of the three.

What this calculator cannot weigh

Quality. A brilliant hire beats a mediocre agency and the reverse is equally true. Use this to size the financial question, then decide on the thing that actually matters, which is who will do better work on your account.

Questions

Does this work for any role, or just media buyers?+

Any role you could outsource. The structure is the same for SEO, design or development — only the tool costs and ramp-up differ.

What ramp-up should I assume?+

Three months is a reasonable default for an experienced hire joining an established account. Six is more honest for a junior, or for a complex product.

Should I count my own management time?+

If you want the full picture, yes — an employee needs managing and an agency needs far less of it. It is not in the calculator because estimates of it vary wildly and people tend to put in a number that proves what they already wanted.

Is the cheapest option the right one?+

Often not. This sizes the financial question so you can stop arguing about it and decide on quality, control and risk instead.
