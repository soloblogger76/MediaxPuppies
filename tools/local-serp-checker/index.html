    What this tool shows you

    Google ranks local searches by where the searcher is. A plumber in Dallas who searches "plumber near me" from the office sees the results for that office. Their customers across town see something else, and a client in another state sees nothing like either. This checker lets you look from the customer's position: pick a place and a keyword, and it opens the Google page a searcher there would get.

    Use it to check the map pack for a client in several suburbs, see which competitors run search ads or Local Services Ads in a city, confirm a new location page ranks where it should, or report local rankings to a client without asking them for screenshots.

    How it works

    Google accepts three parameters on a normal search URL. gl sets the country, hl sets the interface language, and uule sets an exact location. The uule value is a short text record holding a latitude and longitude, encoded in base64 and prefixed with a+. This tool finds the coordinates for the place you type, builds that record, and hands Google the finished URL. You can see the full URL in the result box before you open it.

    Since October 2017 the Google domain no longer decides the country of the results, so google.com with gl=GB gives British results. That is why one tool can cover every country.

    Getting accurate results

    • Use a private window. Your signed-in history and past clicks change the results more than most people expect.
    • Be specific about the place. There is a Springfield in dozens of US states. Add the state, postcode or country, and pick from the matches if several appear.
    • Check where Google thinks you are. The Check location button searches for "where am i" with the same settings. On desktop the footer of every results page names the location too.
    • Do not click competitors' ads. Each click is charged to their account. Read the ad and move on.
    • Repeat over a few days before acting on one ranking. Local results move with time of day, device and the search's wording.

    Questions

    How does a local SERP checker work without a VPN?+

    Google reads three URL parameters: gl (the country), hl (the interface language) and uule (an encoded location). The tool turns the place you type into latitude and longitude, encodes them into a uule value and opens a normal Google search with all three set.

    Will I see the same results as a real searcher in that city?+

    Very close, but not identical. A real searcher's history, signed-in account and device also shape what they see. Open the link in a private window to remove your own history from the picture.

    Can I check the map pack and Local Services Ads with it?+

    Yes. The map pack, Local Services Ads and search ads that Google shows for that location appear on the results page. Do not click a competitor's ads while checking, because each click costs them money.

    How can I confirm which location Google used?+

    Press the Check location button. It searches for "where am i" with your settings, and Google answers with the location it is using. The footer of a desktop results page also names the location.

    Is anything I type stored?+

    No. The tool runs in your browser. Place names are sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service to find their coordinates, and your search goes straight to Google. We do not receive either.
