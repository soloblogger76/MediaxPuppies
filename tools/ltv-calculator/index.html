Revenue LTV versus profit LTV

Most LTV figures quoted in meetings are lifetime revenue, which is a vanity number. A customer who spends ₹12,000 over two years at a 20% margin is worth ₹2,400 to you, not ₹12,000. Spend against the second figure.

The 3:1 benchmark, and when to ignore it

LTV to CAC of 3:1 is a widely used rule of thumb: a third of the lifetime gross profit goes to acquiring the customer, and the rest covers overheads and profit. It is a guide, not physics.

  • Below 1:1 — you lose money on every customer. No volume fixes this.
  • 1:1 to 3:1 — viable but fragile. One bad month or a rise in CPMs erases the margin.
  • 3:1 to 5:1 — the healthy band for most businesses.
  • Above 5:1 — usually means you are under-spending. A competitor willing to run at 3:1 can outbid you on every impression.

Payback period matters as much as the ratio

A 6:1 ratio is no comfort if it takes eighteen months to recover the acquisition cost and you are funding that gap from working capital. The orders-to-payback figure above tells you how many purchases it takes to get your money back. For a cash-constrained business, getting that to one order matters more than a beautiful lifetime ratio.

Being honest about lifespan

Most businesses overestimate this badly. If you have been trading for eighteen months, you cannot claim a three-year customer lifespan — you have no evidence. Use the period you can actually observe, and revise it upward when the data earns it.

Questions

Should LTV use gross or net margin?+

Gross margin — revenue minus the variable costs of delivering the product. Net margin already subtracts marketing, which would double-count.

How do I calculate CAC?+

Total sales and marketing spend for a period divided by the number of new customers acquired in it. Include agency fees and tools, not just ad spend.

What if repeat purchase rate is unknown?+

Assume one purchase and treat anything beyond it as upside. A model built on a guessed repeat rate produces a confident wrong answer.

Does this work for services?+

Yes. Use average contract value as order value and renewals per year as frequency.
