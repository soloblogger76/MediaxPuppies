A forecast is a conversation, not a promise

Nobody can tell you what a budget will produce before it runs. What a forecast does is expose whether the plan can work arithmetically — and a surprising number cannot, which is much better to discover before the money is committed.

Where the numbers come from

  • Cost per click. From your own account if you have one. If not, run a small test for two weeks rather than guessing; estimates for competitive categories are usually optimistic by a wide margin.
  • Click to lead. Your landing page conversion rate. Two to five percent is common for cold paid traffic; above eight is unusual and worth verifying before you plan around it.
  • Lead to customer. From your CRM. If you do not have it, this is the number to go and find before spending anything.

Change the close rate by five points and the whole forecast moves more than any bidding change ever will.

Read it backwards too

Set the budget aside and ask what you need. If you want thirty customers a month and the funnel says you need eight hundred leads to get them, you now know the real question is whether your sales team can call eight hundred people — and that is a staffing decision, not a media one.

Watch the volume floor

Automated bidding needs conversions to learn from. A forecast showing four customers a month is telling you to optimise to an earlier event, because no algorithm improves on four data points.

Questions

How accurate are these forecasts?+

As accurate as the rates you put in. With real historical data they are a reasonable planning tool. With guessed rates they are arithmetic, not a forecast, and should be labelled as such to anyone you show them to.

What if I have never run ads?+

Run a small test for two to four weeks to get a real cost per click and conversion rate, then come back. A forecast built on benchmarks from an article is the most expensive kind of guess.

Should I use gross margin or net?+

Gross. This compares advertising against the profit a sale contributes before fixed costs, which is the right frame for an acquisition decision.

Does this work for ecommerce?+

It works, but ecommerce usually buys the purchase directly rather than a lead. Set the lead-to-customer rate to 100% and treat click-to-lead as your store conversion rate.
