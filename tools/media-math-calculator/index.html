The formulas

MetricFormulaWhat it tells you
CPMspend ÷ impressions × 1,000What the auction costs you. Rises with competition and with narrow audiences.
CPCspend ÷ clicksA blend of CPM and CTR. Falls when the creative improves.
CTRclicks ÷ impressionsWhether the creative and audience match. The fastest signal you have.
CVRconversions ÷ clicksWhether the landing page delivers what the ad promised.
CPAspend ÷ conversionsThe only number that pays your bills.

Reading them together

A single metric never tells you what to fix. The pair does.

  • Low CTR, any CVR — creative or audience problem. Nothing downstream matters until this moves.
  • Good CTR, low CVR — the ad over-promised or the landing page is slow, unclear or asks too much. This is the most common and most fixable case.
  • Good CTR, good CVR, high CPA — your funnel is fine and the auction is simply expensive. Widen the audience, lower frequency, or accept the cost if the customer value supports it.
  • High CPM, everything else fine — usually too narrow a target, high frequency, or a seasonal auction spike.

These are relationships, not benchmarks. What counts as a “good” CTR varies enormously between industries, placements and offers — compare against your own previous months, not against a number from a blog post.

Questions

Why is my CPM suddenly higher?+

Usually competition. Auction prices in India climb sharply around festive periods and end-of-quarter pushes. A narrow audience or rising frequency will do it too.

Should I optimise for CPC?+

No. Cheap clicks from the wrong people are the most expensive thing in a paid account. Optimise for cost per conversion.

Link clicks or all clicks?+

Use link clicks. All clicks includes likes, comments and profile taps, which flatters CTR and tells you nothing about traffic.

Do platform conversions match my actual sales?+

Rarely exactly. Platforms count conversions they believe they influenced within an attribution window. Reconcile monthly against your own records.
