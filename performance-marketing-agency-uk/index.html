Where UK accounts get into trouble

The CAP Code is not a formality.

UK advertising is self-regulated but the regulation has teeth, and the rules bite hardest in exactly the categories that spend the most on performance: finance, health, and anything with a price comparison in it.

The ASA and the CAP Code

Non-broadcast advertising in the UK sits under the CAP Code and is policed by the ASA. Claims must be substantiated before they run, not after a complaint. "Up to" figures, best-price claims, before-and-afters and testimonials all have specific requirements. Rulings are published with your brand name on them, and the ASA works with the platforms to have offending ads taken down — which in practice means your winning ad disappears mid-flight.

Financial promotions

If you are promoting an investment, a credit product or crypto to UK consumers, the promotion has to be issued or approved by an FCA-authorised firm. This is a statutory requirement, not a platform policy, and Meta and Google both ask for the authorisation before they let the ads run. If you are a fintech without this sorted, no amount of media buying skill matters and we will tell you that first.

UK GDPR, PECR and the consent banner

Cookies and similar tracking need consent under PECR, and your consent management platform has to be wired to Google Consent Mode and to Meta’s consent signal. Most of the "my conversions dropped and nothing changed" accounts we audit turn out to have a banner that was updated without anyone telling the marketing team.

Pricing and reviews

Consumer protection law now treats drip pricing and fake or incentivised reviews as enforceable matters rather than best practice. If your landing page reveals mandatory fees late, or your review widget is not what it looks like, that is a conversion rate problem and a legal one at once.
